# Depth Psychology

Navigation: [[domains/_index]] | [[index]]

Depth psychology is the family of psychological approaches that treat the unconscious as real, autonomous, and causal - not merely as the absence of awareness but as an active region of the psyche with its own structure and contents. The term encompasses psychoanalysis (Freud), analytical psychology (Jung), and individual psychology (Adler), though in common usage it often refers specifically to the Jungian tradition.

---

## Core Distinction from Other Psychology

Where academic (experimental) psychology studies behavior and cognition accessible to observation and measurement, depth psychology works with contents that are not directly observable: dreams, symbols, complexes, and the dynamics of the unconscious. The claim is that these invisible contents determine much of human behavior, affect, and meaning-making.

## Analytical Psychology (Jung)

The vault currently holds material primarily from Jung's analytical psychology. Core framework:

| Concept | Brief description |
|---------|------------------|
| [[Collective Unconscious]] | The layer of the psyche that is inborn and universal, not individually acquired |
| [[Archetype]] | Structural predispositions within the collective unconscious; tendencies to form certain images |
| [[Shadow]] | The inferior, dark aspect of personality; the personal and collective contents that ego rejects |
| [[Anima and Animus]] | The contrasexual archetype: anima in men, animus in women |
| [[Mother Archetype]] | The archetypal pattern of the maternal: nature, unconscious, origin |
| [[Trickster]] | The chaotic, pre-moral collective shadow figure in mythology |
| [[Individuation]] | The lifelong process of becoming a whole, integrated Self |
| [[Mandala Symbolism]] | Circular symbols of psychic wholeness and the Self |
| [[Colonizer Archetype]] | The polarized colonizer-colonized pattern manifesting in oppression and inequality (Charura & Bushell 2023) |
| [[Ethnic Shadow]] | Collective shadow content organized around race, conquest, and colonization |
