# Synchronicity in Post-Jungian Astrology: A Cosmological Quest

[[Jingchao Zeng|Zeng]] & [[Nathan Fraikin|Fraikin]]'s JAP paper examining how two major post-Jungian astrologers — [[Liz Greene]] and [[Richard Tarnas]] — received (and, the authors argue, distorted) Jung's concept of [[Synchronicity|synchronicity]] in order to build a cosmology for astrology. The paper is as much a corrective on *what Jung actually claimed* as it is a study of astrology.

## Jung's Cautious, Empirical Synchronicity

The authors reassert the "threshold" of Jung's original conception: synchronicity as a claim about **empirical phenomena** — rare, numinous, meaningful coincidences — *not* a metaphysical assumption. Key facts they foreground:

- Jung's **astrological experiment** (correlating married couples' horoscopes with traditional marriage aspects) produced **statistically insignificant** results — the connection was "merely chance" (CW *Synchronicity* ¶901). Jung published it anyway, to underline that synchronicity must be approached "through experience rather than from a purely theoretical point of view."
- Jung was "wary of any claims about [metaphysical] reality that are not based on experiences amenable to psychological investigation" (Roderick Main). He recognized two obscure layers of synchronicity — "a special class of natural events" *and* "the sum of countless individual acts of creation occurring in time" — and left the field deliberately "very obscure," a task for others to work out (von Franz: "the big breakthrough had really already been done… the work now is to work it out… explore it empirically").

## Qualitative Time — The Model Jung Abandoned

Full treatment: [[Qualitative Time]]. In his 1930 Richard Wilhelm memorial talk, Jung floated an early model: the birth moment "contains a certain quality that exists in time" — "if there are any astrological diagnoses of character that are in fact correct, this is due not to the influence of the stars but to our own hypothetical time qualities." He **abandoned this** in the 1952 synchronicity essay after the failed experiment, moving to a post-Kantian view of synchronicity as *beyond* time, space, and causality. The paper's core diagnosis: **post-Jungian astrology revived the qualitative-time model Jung himself discarded.**

## Liz Greene: Psychical + Physical Synchronicity Confused

[[Liz Greene]] made two kinds of claim — a *psychical* aspect (synchronicity as meaningful coincidence, faithful to Jung) and a *physical/metaphysical* one (the birth moment's "qualities of time," backed by Hermeticism and Paracelsus, "as above so below"). In *Saturn* (1976) she reads the discovery of Uranus as synchronistic with the industrial/revolutionary age; in *The Astrology of Fate* (1984) she affirms Jung's *abaissement du niveau mental* (emotional lowering of the mental threshold activating the unconscious) but then "slips into the qualitative-time model" where synchronicities become predictable and predetermined from the birth chart — which violates Jung's own reflection on his experiment.

## Richard Tarnas: Synchronicity as Cosmological Axiom

[[Richard Tarnas]]'s *Cosmos and Psyche* (2006) remains faithful to Jung's *summary* of synchronicity but puts it to a different use: as a **metaphysical axiom shaping reality**, the formal-causal principle by which planetary archetypes order human collective history. The authors' critique: once synchronicity becomes a systematic frame for predicting archetypal themes across past and future, "the uniqueness of the present moment might disappear" — **"the abnormal becomes normal,"** and the numinous, rare, acausal character Jung insisted on is lost. Tarnas notably omits the inconclusive outcome of Jung's astrological experiment. (There is a real ambiguity the authors credit: Jung did treat the archetype as the *formal cause* linking synchronistic events — Aristotelian formal causality — so synchronicity is "acausal" only in the efficient-cause sense; Tarnas exploits this imprecision.)

## The Alternative: Synchronicity II and the Numinous Act of Interpretation

Maggie Hyde (*Jung and Astrology*, 1992) distinguishes **Synchronicity I** (an objective observer + an objectively observed synchronistic event) from **Synchronicity II** (which includes the observer's *subjective participation*). Natal-astrology data supports neither as cosmology (Synchronicity I fails statistically). The authors' proposal: recover synchronicity in astrology **not as a cosmological principle but as Synchronicity II** — the astrologer's numinous, present-moment, participatory encounter with the chart's symbols, "partly the astrologer's meaningful encounter with a symbol, and partly that strange feeling that the material is also about oneself." Hyde's own example: contemplating a client's Saturn-in-Scorpio square Sun-in-tenth-house (death/restriction vs. authority/heart) at the exact moment she received a call that her boss had a critical heart attack. The emphasis returns to Jung's grounding in **numinosity** (Otto) — "synchronicity closely resembles numinous experiences where space, time, and causality are abolished."
