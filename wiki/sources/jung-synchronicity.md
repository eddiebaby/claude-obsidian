# Synchronicity: An Acausal Connecting Principle — C.G. Jung (1952)

> [!abstract] Source Summary
> Jung's most sustained philosophical argument. Published alongside physicist Wolfgang Pauli's study of Kepler in the joint volume *The Interpretation of Nature and the Psyche* (1952). The essay proposes a fourth explanatory principle beside causality, energy, space, and time — meaningful coincidence — and grounds it in the archetype as an ordering factor common to both psyche and matter.

## Core Argument

### 1. The Problem — Causality's Limits

Jung opens by noting that science's causal axiom — every event has a sufficient antecedent cause — works for regular, repeatable phenomena but fails to explain the category of *meaningful coincidences*. These are events where two or more things occur simultaneously and hold the same meaning, yet no causal chain connects them. Science dismisses them as "mere chance." Jung argues the dismissal is a methodological prejudice, not a finding.

The move is careful: he does not deny causality. He proposes that causality and synchronicity are *complementary* explanatory principles — the first for regular, repeatable events; the second for singular, meaning-laden ones.

### 2. Definition

> "Meaningful coincidence of two or more events, where something other than the probability of chance is involved" (¶969).

More precisely: a coincidence in time of two or more causally unrelated events that have the same or similar *meaning*. The Latin root of "synchronicity" (syn + chronos) names the simultaneity but the defining feature is meaning, not merely temporal overlap.

Three structural forms:
1. A psychic state (e.g. a dream, vision, or thought) coincides with a corresponding outer event that the subject could not have known about.
2. A psychic state coincides with an outer event occurring at a distance (space-transcending).
3. A psychic state corresponds to something that has not yet happened (time-transcending).

### 3. The Acausal Connecting Principle

The title phrase is precise: synchronicity is a *connecting* principle (it relates events that share meaning) but *acausal* (the connection is not effected by any physical force or information transmission). What does the connecting? Jung's answer: the *archetype* as an ordering factor that is neither purely psychic nor purely physical — a *psychoid* principle operating in both.

"Synchronistic events rest on the simultaneous occurrence of two different psychic states. One of them is the normal, probable state (i.e. the one that is causally explicable), and the other, the critical experience, is the one that cannot be derived causally from the first" (¶947).

### 4. The Pauli Quaternio

Working with Pauli, Jung proposed that reality requires four explanatory principles arranged as a quaternio (a fourfold structure, itself an archetypal pattern):

```
Indestructible Energy
        |
Causality -------- Synchronicity
        |
Space-Time Continuum
```

Causality (constant connection through effect) and Synchronicity (inconstant connection through contingence/meaning) are orthogonal, not opposed. Energy and space-time are the physical substrate; causality and synchronicity are the two modes of connection across that substrate. This is Jung's formal answer to where synchronicity fits in a complete picture of reality.

### 5. The Archetype as Ordering Principle

The key move theoretically: Jung grounds the synchronistic connection in the *archetype*. The archetype is not merely a psychological image; it is "the introspectively recognizable form of *a priori* psychic orderedness" (¶965). When an archetype is *constellated* (activated) in the psyche, it can produce a pattern that shows up simultaneously in both psychic and material events — because the archetype is a common ordering factor underlying both.

This gives the archetype a new dimension beyond its role in dreams and myth. Here it is understood as an ordering principle of *reality as such*, not just of psychological experience.

Synchronistic events are therefore "acts of creation in time" (¶965) — singular events that cannot be derived from prior causes, analogous to quantum events in physics (a parallel Pauli emphasized).

### 6. The Golden Scarab — Paradigm Case

The most famous clinical example (¶982-983): a female patient with a rigid rationalist attitude — intellectually "difficult" in the pejorative sense Jung intends — was recounting a dream in which she had been given a golden scarab (an expensive jewel). While she described the dream, Jung heard a tapping at the closed window. He opened it and caught the insect flying in: a rose-chafer beetle (*Cetonia aurata*), gold-green in color, the closest thing to a golden scarab in the central European fauna. He held it out to her and said: "Here is your scarab."

The effect: her rationalist armor cracked. Treatment, which had been blocked, could proceed.

The significance Jung draws: the scarab is an ancient symbol of rebirth (Egyptian *khepri*). The constellation of the rebirth archetype was apparently powerful enough that it organized both her inner dream and the outer event around the same symbol simultaneously. Neither caused the other. The meaning — rebirth — preceded and ordered both.

### 7. Empirical Sections — Astrology and Rhine

Roughly half the essay is taken up with Jung's attempt at empirical demonstration using:
- Astrological conjunctions in married vs. unmarried couples (the "astrological experiment," later statistically contested).
- J.B. Rhine's ESP and psychokinesis experiments from Duke University (taken seriously by Jung as evidence that the psyche can affect matter and transcend space-time under certain conditions).

These sections are the most dated in the essay. Jung himself acknowledged the statistical methodology was imperfect. The conceptual and clinical argument is the enduring core.

### 8. The Unus Mundus

Late in the essay and in subsequent work (especially *Mysterium Coniunctionis*, 1956), Jung invokes the scholastic concept *unus mundus* — the "one world" underlying the visible multiplicity of phenomena. If synchronicity is real, it points to a stratum of reality that is prior to the psyche-matter distinction. The archetype operating there is neither psychological nor physical; it is ontologically prior to both.

This becomes the deepest level of Jung's late metaphysics: individuation as participation in the unus mundus, and synchronicity as the momentary visibility of that participation. See [[Unus Mundus]].

### 9. The Pauli Collaboration

Wolfgang Pauli (1900-1958), Nobel physicist and creator of the exclusion principle, worked with Jung from the mid-1930s to Pauli's death. Pauli was Jung's patient first, then collaborator. He brought two things to the synchronicity project:
1. The quaternio structure (four principles) — Pauli was convinced that quaternary, not binary, structures governed physical reality.
2. The parallel from quantum mechanics: the observer effect, the irreducibility of singular quantum events to prior causes, and the non-locality already visible in quantum entanglement all pointed, for Pauli, toward something like synchronicity in the physics domain.

The 1952 joint volume was the published summit of their collaboration. See [[Wolfgang Pauli]] (entity page, to be created).

## Key Quotations (from CW 8)
