# Alchemical Studies (CW 13)

Five essays spanning 1929–1954. Each treats a distinct body of alchemical or proto-alchemical material: the Chinese Taoist *Golden Flower*, the Gnostic-alchemical visions of Zosimos, the Renaissance physician Paracelsus, the pagan figure of Mercurius, and the philosophical tree. The volume builds on CW 12 (*Psychology and Alchemy*) and provides the most concentrated treatment of alchemy as psychological symbolism outside *Mysterium Coniunctionis* (CW 14).

## Essay I: Commentary on "The Secret of the Golden Flower"

*Tài Yī Jīn Huá Zōng Zhǐ* — a Chinese Taoist meditation text transmitted by Richard Wilhelm to Jung in 1928. Published with Wilhelm's translation (1929). As the editorial note records: "In medieval alchemy we have the long-sought connecting link between Gnosis and the processes of the collective unconscious."

### Key Extracted Concepts

**Wu wei** (par. 20): Action through non-action. "As far as I could see they did nothing (wu wei) but let things happen." Attitude of allowing unconscious processes to unfold without forceful ego direction.

**Outgrowing** (pars. 17–18): "All the greatest and most important problems of life are fundamentally insoluble... They can never be solved, but only outgrown." A new, wider personality doesn't solve old problems — it makes them shrink into relative insignificance. See [[Individuation]].

**Midlife individuation** (par. 16): "This way of development has scarcely any meaning before the middle of life (normally between the ages of thirty-five and forty)." The inner work cannot begin before the ego is sufficiently built.

**Golden Flower synonyms for the centre** (par. 33): "dragon castle at the bottom of the sea," "yellow castle," "heavenly heart," "terrace of living," "square inch field of the square foot house," "purple hall of the city of jade," "dark pass," "space of former heaven." All are synonyms for the germinal vesicle / Self-centre.

**Mandala** (pars. 31–45): The commentary contains Jung's most detailed clinical account of mandala formation. The *sulcus primigenius*, *temenos*, circumambulatio, and quaternary structure developed here. Ten European patient-produced mandalas appended, all created independently of Eastern influence (earliest: 1916). See [[Mandala Symbolism]].

**Monotheism of consciousness** (par. 51): "Our time has become so utterly godless and profane: we lack all knowledge of the unconscious psyche and pursue the cult of consciousness to the exclusion of all else. Our true religion is a monotheism of consciousness, a possession by it, coupled with a fanatical denial of the existence of fragmentary autonomous systems."

**The gods have become diseases** (par. 54): "Zeus no longer rules Olympus but rather the solar plexus, and produces curious specimens for the doctor's consulting room, or disorders the brains of politicians and journalists who unwittingly let loose psychic epidemics on the world."

**Mass psychosis** (par. 52): Repressed autonomous systems are projected outward as enemy → collective delusions, revolutions, war. "Destructive mass psychoses."

**Anima / animus from hun / p'o** (pars. 57–63): Chinese *hun* (cloud-demon, yang; after death = *shen*/spirit) ↔ animus. Chinese *p'o* (white ghost, yin; after death = *kuei*/ghost) ↔ anima. Key definition: "The anima can be defined as the image or archetype or deposit of all the experiences of man with woman." *Mulier non habet animam, sed animum* — woman has not an anima but an animus. See [[Anima and Animus]].

**Participation mystique** (pars. 65–66, 78): Liberation from unconscious identity = the shift from "I live" to "It lives me." See [[Participation-Mystique]].

**The Self** (par. 67): "If the unconscious can be recognized as a co-determining factor along with consciousness... then the centre of gravity of the total personality shifts its position. It is then no longer in the ego, which is merely the centre of consciousness, but in the hypothetical point between conscious and unconscious. **This new centre might be called the self.**"

**Diamond body** (pars. 68–69, 76–78): The Chinese *chin-kang* / *kin tan* = incorruptible spirit-body that grows through the work. Western parallel: "Yet not I, but Christ liveth in me" (Galatians 2:20). Key formulation: "It is not I who live, it lives me." See [[Diamond-Body]].

**Imitatio Christi critique** (pars. 80–81): "The *imitatio Christi* has this disadvantage: in the long run we worship as a divine example a man who embodied the deepest meaning of life, and then, out of sheer imitation, we forget to make real our own deepest meaning—self-realization." The deeper imitatio: realize one's own deepest conviction with the same courage Jesus showed.

**East-West contrast** (par. 79): "In the Pauline Christ symbol the supreme religious experiences of West and East confront one another: Christ the sorrow-laden hero, and the Golden Flower that blooms in the purple hall of the city of jade. What a contrast, what an unfathomable difference, what an abyss of history!"

**Jung's epistemological stance** (pars. 73–75): "I quite deliberately bring everything that purports to be metaphysical into the daylight of psychological understanding." Eastern philosophers are "symbolical psychologists, to whom no greater wrong could be done than to take them literally." "Everything of which we are conscious is an image, and that image *is* psyche." "The psyche is a world in which the ego is contained. Maybe there are fishes who believe that they contain the sea."

## Essay II: The Visions of Zosimos (pars. 85–117)

[[Zosimos-of-Panopolis]] (fl. c. 300 AD, Panopolis, Egypt) is the oldest alchemical author whose substantial texts survive. His vision series — addressed to his *soror mystica* Theosebeia — is the richest source of proto-alchemical transformation symbolism in the ancient world.

### The Vision Series

**First Vision (III, i, 2)**: The priest Ion submits to "an unendurable torment." The sacrificer-Ion draws off the skin of Ion's head with a sword, mingles bones with flesh, burns them — "till I perceived by the transformation of the body that I had become spirit." Ion changes into a mutilated anthroparion and tears his own flesh with his teeth.

**Second Vision (III, i, 3)**: Bowl-shaped altar; boiling water; numberless people burning yet alive. An aged barber: "This is the place of the operation called embalming. Those who seek to obtain the art enter here, and become spirits by escaping from the body." The brazen man: "This brazen man whom you see is the priest who sacrifices and is sacrificed, and spews forth his own flesh."

**Later Visions**: Agathodaimon (the white-haired old man) → pillar of fire; groans: "I am the leaden man, and I submit myself to an unendurable torment." Priest in white celebrates "terrible mysteries": "It is he who changes the bodies into blood, makes the eyes clairvoyant, and raises the dead." Fourth step: the "Meridian of the Sun" is beheaded; "You have completed the descent of the seven steps."

**Temple Instruction (III, i, 5)**: "Build a temple from a single stone, like to white lead, to alabaster, to Proconnesian marble, with neither end nor beginning in its construction... A dragon lies at the entrance... The priest, that brazen man... has become the silver man; and if you will, you will soon have him as the golden man." Sequence: brazen → silver → golden = nigredo → albedo → rubedo.

### Key Extracted Concepts

**The sacrificer = the sacrificed** (par. 91): "the priest is at once the sacrificer and the sacrificed." From the Dionysian tradition (sparagmos) and the Christian (Christ's self-sacrifice). Ion = uroboros = sacrificer: three aspects of the same principle. See [[Uroboros]].

**The aqua permanens** (pars. 89–105): The central alchemical symbol. *Humidum radicale*, *anima aquina*, *anima mundi* imprisoned in matter; extracted through the torment of fire. Connected to Osiris (Nile = dismembered body, 14 parts; lead = sealed tomb). "The dragon slays itself, weds itself, impregnates itself." "Water is spirit" (*Rosarium philosophorum*). "Nature applied to nature transforms nature." See [[Aqua-Permanens]].

**The krater / bowl-shaped altar** (pars. 96–97): "Unquestionably related to the *krater* of Poimandres" — the vessel the demiurge sent down filled with Nous. Zosimos to Theosebeia: "Hasten down to the shepherd and bathe yourself in the *krater*." = Vas Hermetis = uterus of spiritual renewal. Dorn: vessel must be made by "squaring of the circle" = "a psychic operation, the creation of an inner readiness to accept the archetype of the self." See [[Vessel-Symbol]].

**Massa confusa and the four elements** (par. 111): Dismembering the uroboros = dividing chaos into four elements. Jung: "Without knowing anything of alchemy, I worked out many years ago a psychological typology based on the four functions of consciousness... Unconsciously, I was making use of the same archetype."

**The Meridian of the Sun / solificatio** (par. 95): The white-clad initiate who undergoes solar transformation. Head struck off = obtaining the "omega element" (*στοιχεῖον στρογγύλον*) = arcane substance = seat of consciousness. Zosimos: "sons of the Golden Head."

**Angels as unconscious transmitters** (par. 108): "If the conscious mind is not ready to assimilate these contents, their energy flows off into the affective and instinctual sphere. This produces outbursts of affect, irritation, bad moods, and sexual excitement."

**The temple = lapis** (par. 112): "neither beginning nor end" = circular = uroboros. The spring within = fountain of life = increase of consciousness. In later alchemy: *domus thesaurorum* (treasure-house).

**The homunculus and metallic stages** (par. 118): The dragon/serpent = initial state of unconsciousness; loves "caverns and dark places." Unconsciousness must be sacrificed to find the entrance into the head. The hero-dragon struggle → sun rises = consciousness dawns. The transformation takes place "inside the temple, that is, in the head." The inner man as homunculus passes through copper→silver→gold, "undergoing a gradual enhancement of value."

### §3: The Personifications (pars. 121–125)

**Participation mystique and personification** (par. 122): The personification of lifeless things in alchemy is "a remnant of primitive and archaic psychology... caused by unconscious identity, or what Lévy-Bruhl called *participation mystique*." Alchemists concretized all their most important ideas — four elements, vessel, stone, prima materia, tincture. Man as microcosm: "Man is to be esteemed a little world, and in all respects he is to be compared to a world."

**The Yellow Man and Red Man** (par. 123): Personified metals guarding the Lapidem Philosophorum — an anonymous alchemical text has the seeker pass through two shut doors; a Yellow Man (lead, ugly but sweet-worded) and a Red Man (fiery red with red eyes, gentle) guide him to the stone. These are not allegories but spontaneous personifications from unconscious identity with matter.

**The stone extracted from man** (par. 125): "And as man is composed of the four elements, so also is the stone, and so it is [dug] out of man, and you are its ore, namely by working; and from you it is extracted, namely by division; and in you it remains inseparably." *Consilium coniugii*: "Four are the natures which compose the philosophical man." "They say that the stone is a man, because one cannot attain to it save by reason and human knowledge." See [[Lapis-Philosophorum]].

### §4: The Stone Symbolism (pars. 126–133)

**The inner spiritual man / Pandora as anima** (par. 126): Zosimos distinguishes fleshly man (*σάρξ*) from spiritual man (*πνευματικός*). Earthly man = Thoth-Adam, bearing within him the spiritual man "whose name is light (*φῶς*)." Spiritual-fleshly pair also named Prometheus-Epimetheus. Pandora/Eve = the anima who seduced the spiritual man into embodiment, "just as Shakti or Maya entangles man's consciousness with the world." The lapis = ἄνθρωπος πνευματικός = *natura abscondita*. See [[Lapis-Philosophorum]], [[Anima and Animus]].

**The lapis-Christ parallel** (par. 127): The lapis's attributes (incorruptibility, permanence, divinity, triunity) = *deus absconditus* in matter. Earliest: Zosimos. The lapis *complements* not signifies Christ — "far from *signifying* Christ, the lapis *complements* the common conception of the Christ figure." Khunrath (16th c.): lapis = *filius macrocosmi* (Son of the Great World) vs. Christ = *filius microcosmi*. The Assumption of Mary adds the 4th feminine principle to the Trinity → real quaternary as symbol of totality. Trinity = "mere postulate" (adversary outside). See [[Lapis-Philosophorum]].

**Stone-birth mythology** (pars. 128–130): Stone as God-image = birthplace of gods (Mithras). Australian *churinga* — soul-stones; charge by burial among dead; heal by blow to abdomen. Navaho Estsánatlehi ("woman who rejuvenates herself") — turquoise goddess; immortal; four daughters from body, fifth from spirit; sun from right breast, moon from left; simultaneously anima and Self: "her stone-nature, her immortality, her duality as sun and moon, her role as paramour" (par. 131). The self of a matriarchal man is still immersed in his unconscious femininity.

**Stone and immortality** (par. 133): *Vivus lapis philosophicus* = panacea, tincture, Anthropos, immortality. "Body of a saint becomes stone." *Transitus*: "the 'other side' is the same as eternity."

### §5: The Water Symbolism (pars. 134–138)

**The lapis = projected Self** (par. 134): Historical/ethnological symbols identical with those spontaneously produced by the unconscious. "The lapis represents the idea of a transcendent totality which coincides with what analytical psychology calls the self." The lapis consists of body, soul, and spirit — a living being, a homunculus. Its parallelism with Christ rooted in Hellenistic syncretism. Priscillian (4th c. Gnostic-Manichaean): "One-horned is God, Christ a rock to us, Jesus a cornerstone."

**The divine water and death/rebirth** (par. 135): "The wonder-working water... is both water and spirit, and kills and vivifies." Every process of death and rebirth is naturally a symbol of the divine water. See [[Aqua-Permanens]].

**Zosimos / Nicodemus parallel** (par. 136): John 3 = alchemist's scripture. When Jesus said "Except a man be born of water and of the spirit," "an alchemist of that time would at once have understood what he meant." John 4:14: "a well of water springing up into everlasting life." The Nicodemus dialogue's "earthly and heavenly things" = *physika and mystika / somata and asomata / corporalia and spiritualia* (since Democritus).

**The deus absconditus** (par. 138): "The divine water or its symbol, the uroboros, means nothing other than the *deus absconditus*, the god hidden in matter, the divine Nous that came down to Physis and was lost in her embrace. This mystery of the 'god become physical' underlies not only classical alchemy but also many other spiritual manifestations of Hellenistic syncretism." See [[Uroboros]], [[Lapis-Philosophorum]].

### §6: The Origin of the Vision (pars. 139–144)

**Alchemy = individuation in projection** (par. 140): Jung's key statement: "The mystical side of alchemy, as distinct from its historical aspect, is essentially a psychological problem. To all appearances, it is a concretization, in projected and symbolic form, of **the process of individuation**. Even today this process produces symbols that have the closest connections with alchemy." See [[Individuation]].

**The incubation sleep** (par. 139): Zosimos's visions came through the sleep of incubation — a dream "sent by God." The drama shows "how man would act and what he would have to suffer if he were drawn into the cycle of the death and rebirth of the gods."

**Kékulé vs. Zosimos** (par. 143): Kékulé dreamed of dancing pairs → deduced the benzol ring. He succeeded where Zosimos failed: "Zosimos strove for in vain. His 'composition of the waters' did not fall into as neat a pattern as did the carbon and hydrogen atoms of the benzol ring. Alchemy projected an inner, psychic experience into chemical substances that seemed to hold out mysterious possibilities but nevertheless proved refractory to the intentions of the alchemist."

**Final assessment** (par. 144): "Although chemistry has nothing to learn from the vision of Zosimos, it is a mine of discovery for modern psychology, which would come to a sorry pass if it could not turn to these testimonies of psychic experience from ancient times."

## Essay III: Paracelsus as a Spiritual Phenomenon (pars. 145–)

[[Paracelsus]] (1493–1541) is the most important figure in the history of Renaissance alchemy as spiritual phenomenon — a physician whose compassion drove everything, and whose unconscious conflict between Mater Ecclesia and Mater Natura generated a daemonic creative energy that Jung traces directly to Goethe's Faust and Nietzsche's Zarathustra.

### §1A: The Two Sources of Knowledge (pars. 145–150)

**Paracelsus's character** (pars. 145–147): Physician first; driving force = compassion ("the physician's schoolmaster"). Mother died young → formidable mother-imago → two mothers: **Mater Ecclesia** (Church, remained faithful despite criticism) and **Mater Natura** (nature, the *lumen naturae*). Conflict = energy: "without a tension of opposites there is no energy."

**The two epistemological sources** (pars. 148–149): Divine revelation + *lumen naturae* (light of nature). Named his pagan-natural writings "Pagoyum" (*paganum* + goyim). *Philosophia sagax*: "There are two kinds of knowledge: an eternal and a temporal. The eternal springs directly from the light of the Holy Spirit, but the other directly from the Light of Nature." Both ultimately from God. "If Father and Son are one, how then can I honour two lights?" See [[Lumen-Naturae]].

### §1B: Magic (pars. 151–156)

**Paracelsus's magic** (pars. 151–155): Believed in magic as fervently as in divine revelation — both proceed from the *lumen naturae*. For him magic = *mysterium et magnale Dei*. He practiced wax manikins, amulets, spells. Conrad Gessner's testimony: accused of sorcery, demons, geomancy, necromancy ("Theophrastus has assuredly been an impious man and a sorcerer, and has had intercourse with demons").

**The unconscious split** (par. 155): Paracelsus "consisted of two persons who never really confronted one another." Always projected the inner conflict outward. "He was the 'Monarcha,' the sovereign ruler, which secretly and unknown to himself was the very thing he was not." The tension between "I under God" (piety) and "God under me" (alchemy's presumption) = "every unconscious conflict works out like that: one obstructs and undermines oneself." See [[Paracelsus]].

**Magic language** (par. 155–156): Symptom of the unconscious conflict = power-words, hapax legomena, "bombastic" style. Witch-language: Swindafnerz, Dallen, Chely, Daphne (inverted words). "Paracelsus must have been thoroughly steeped in the lowest folk beliefs and popular superstitions."

### §1C: Alchemy (pars. 157–158)

**The alchemical tradition** (par. 157): Chief figure = Mercurius (dual: quicksilver + world soul), companion figures Sol (gold) and Luna (silver). *Coniunctio* = hierosgamos of Sol and Luna → *filius sapientiae* or *filius philosophorum* = transformed Mercurius = hermaphrodite. The *opus alchymicum* = *opus divinum*; Melchior Cibinensis (early 16th c.) represented it as a Mass. Long before Paracelsus, the *filius* or *lapis* = allegory of Christ.

**Paracelsus and alchemy** (par. 158): Used alchemy for pharmacology (*materia medica*) but also for "philosophical purposes." The *Paragranum* says the physician himself is "ripened" by the art — alchemical maturation = physician's maturation. Probably naive about the deeper mystical content, but his closest followers were found among alchemical physicians.

### §1D: The Arcane Teaching — The Primordial Man (pars. 159–168)

**The Filius Philosophorum as Primordial Man** (pars. 161–163): The light of alchemy is personified as the *filius* = δαιμόνιον. Khunrath (Paracelsus's follower): "This [the *filius philosophorum*], the Son of the Macrocosm, is God and creature... [Christ] is the son of God, the θεάνθρωπος, that is, God and man; the one conceived in the womb of the Macrocosm, the other in the womb of the Microcosm, and both of a virginal womb. Without blasphemy I say: From the stone you shall know in natural wise Christ, and from Christ the stone." The *filius* complements Christ (as in Essay II); here the Macrocosm/Microcosm distinction becomes the *filius macrocosmi* vs. *filius microcosmi*.

**Historical trajectory** (par. 163): "I under God" (Paracelsus/Angelus Silesius) → Goethe's Faust ("God under me") → Nietzsche's Zarathustra ("create a god for yourself out of your seven devils"). "Medieval alchemy prepared the way for the greatest intervention in the divine world order that man has ever attempted... Science and technology have indeed conquered the world, but whether the psyche has gained anything is another matter."

**The Primordial Man / Homo Maximus / Adech** (pars. 165–168): Paracelsus's central arcane concept: the Primordial Man = "the star in us." Multiple secret names: Adech (from Adam), *homo maximus*, Archeus, Idechtrum, Protothoma. "The true man is the star in us. The star desires to drive man towards great wisdom." "For heaven is man and man is heaven, and all men are one heaven, and heaven is only one man." Adam Kadmon (Cabalistic) = *filius philosophorum*. The ἄνθρωπος φωτεινός (man of light), imprisoned in Adam. "In the whole Ides there is but One Man, the same is extracted by the Iliaster and is the Protoplast." Cross-cultural: Purusha (India), Gayomart (Persia), Metatron (Zohar), Celestial Man of Daniel/Ezra/Enoch/Philo, Gnostic Anthropos — "always connected with the question of creation and redemption." See [[Anthropos]].

### §2: "De Vita Longa" — The Iliaster (pars. 169–171)

**The treatise** (par. 169): *De vita longa* — written in Latin, based on notes from Paracelsus's lectures, edited by Adam von Bodenstein. "Difficult to understand... wrapped in obscurity." Jung uses the alchemical tradition to interpret its arcane terminology.

**The Iliaster** (pars. 170–171): Life = "a certain embalsamed Mumia, which preserves the mortal body from the mortal worms and from corruption by means of a mixed saline solution." The preserving agent = balsam. The balsam = *spiritus mercurii* = coincides with the Iliaster. The Iliaster (ὕλη + ἀστήρ) = stellar life-substance, higher than the four elements; determines length of life. Three forms: *Iliaster sanctitus*, *paratetus*, *magnus*. See [[Iliaster]].

### §3: Melusina and the Filius Regius (pars. 181–184)

**Melusina's longing** (par. 181): "The longing of Melusina for a soul and for redemption has a parallel in that kingly substance which is hidden in the sea and cries out for deliverance." Michael Maier gives the *filius regius*'s lament: "He lives and calls from the depths: Who shall deliver me from the waters and lead me to dry land?" Only a few believe his lament — most think they hear "the crashing and roaring of Scylla and Charybdis." See [[Melusina]].

**The unconscious as sea** (par. 183): "The sea (*mare nostrum*) of the alchemists is their own darkness, the unconscious." The Church Fathers took the *limus profundi* (mire of the deep) as evil; the alchemists took a more optimistic view: the dark background of the soul contains not only evil but "a king in need of, and capable of, redemption." *Rosarium*: "At the end of the work the king will go forth for thee, crowned with his diadem, radiant as the sun, shining like the carbuncle." "Despise not the ash, for it is the diadem of thy heart, and the ash of things that endure."

**Hippolytus and the Naassenes** (pars. 182–184): Hippolytus gives insight into Gnostic thinking parallel to alchemy — the "Uncharacterized Man" (ἀχαρακτήριστος) dwelling in earthly clay, god who dwells in "the great flood," calling from many waters. Psalm 23 / Isaiah 43 references: the ascent (ἄνοδος) or regeneration (ἀναγέννησις) of Adam. The *Tractatus aureus Hermetis* invocation of Mater Natura: "O mightiest nature of the natures, who containest and separatest the midmost of the natures... who art the mother of all beings!"

### §4: Production of the One — The Centre (pars. 185–189)

**Retorta distillatio** (par. 185): Paracelsus's process = distillation turned back upon itself (the Pelican vessel). Purpose: purify the human body until it unites with the *maior homo* (inner spiritual man). "This was not an ordinary chemical operation — it was essentially a psychological procedure." Proceeds *ex medio centri* (from the midst of the centre).

**The centre** (pars. 186–188): Dorn: "Nothing is more like God than the centre... an infinite abyss of mysteries." Gold = symbol of eternity. The sidereal balsam = *corpus astrale* = dwells in the heart like the sun in the macrocosm. The egg-yolk's red sun-point = the young chick = bird of Hermes = mercurial serpent. Dorn's formula: the One = "simple and consists of the number four." *Infallibile medii centrum* = "immeasurable order reaching to infinity."

**KEY CONCLUSION** (par. 189): "From this context we can see that the *retorta distillatio ex medio centri* results in the activation and development of **a psychic centre, a concept that coincides psychologically with that of the self.**" See [[Self (archetype)]].

The *spagirica foetura* (spagyric foetus) = *filius philosophorum* = "the inner, eternal man in the shell of the outer, mortal man." The *filius* cures not only bodily defects but "the subtle and spiritual sickness in the human mind."

### §5: Coniunctio in the Spring — Melissa and Aniadus (par. 190+)

**Separation of Saturn and Sol**: Saturn = cold, dark, heavy, impure. Sol = the opposite. The separation is prerequisite for the *coniunctio*. See [[Coniunctio]].

**Melissa** (lemon balm): purges "black, burnt-out blood" (Saturnine melancholy); used in ancient medicine for happiness and melancholia. In Paracelsus: Melissa unites "the powers of the 'supracelestial coniunctio.'" Purification of body via Melissa → coniunctio with the long-living astral man.

**Iloch and Aniadus**: Iloch = the coniunctio itself, whose essence is the Aniadus. Dorn: Aniadus = "the efficacy of things" = "springtime condition." Comes about by "exaltation of both worlds." "In the true May, when the exaltations of Aniada begin, these should be gathered." The conjunction produces the **Enochdianus**. Goal: *tranquillitas animi* — tranquillity of mind, freed from Saturnine turbulence. See [[Iliaster]].

**Lightning of Saturn** (par. 190): At the end of the process a "physical lightning" appears; the lightning of Saturn separates from the lightning of Sol. What remains pertains "to longevity, to that undoubtedly great Iliaster."

### §6: The Komarios Dialogue — Waters Awakening the Dead (pars. 191-193)

**Two illustrations** at this transition:
1. The Pelican vessel (Rhenanus, *Solis e puteo emergentis*, Frankfurt 1613): the vessel of circulatory distillation; distillate runs back into the belly = *retorta distillatio* made visible
2. Melusina as *coniunetio animae cum corpore* (MS. Sloane 5025, Ripley *Scrowle*, 1588): anima embracing man rising from the sea; gnomes = planetary spirits as *paredroi* (familiars). See [[Melusina]]
3. King's Son (*filius regis*) and mystagogue Hermes on mountain (Lambspringk, *Musaeum hermeticum*, Frankfurt 1678): allusion to the Temptation (Luke 4); spirit and soul = volatile substances rising during heating of prima materia. See [[Lapis-Philosophorum]]

**Instruction of Cleopatra by the Archpriest Komarios** (par. 191): One of the earliest Greek alchemical texts. "Make known to us how the highest descends to the lowest... how the blessed waters come down from above to awaken the dead, who lie round about in the midst of Hades, chained in the darkness; how the elixir of life comes to them and awakens them."

**Cleopatra's answer** (par. 192): "When the waters come in, they awaken the bodies and the spirits, which are imprisoned and powerless... they bestir themselves, rise up, and clothe themselves in bright colours, glorious as the flowers in spring."

**Aniada expanded** (par. 193): Ruland: Aniada = "fruits and powers of paradise and heaven; they are also the Christian Sacraments... those things which by thought, judgment, and imagination promote longevity." *Opus alchymicum* should begin in spring (Aries/Mars). The Aniada gathered "like healing herbs" = "the gathering together of all the psychic powers for the great transformation." Hierosgamos of Poliphilo (*Hypnerotomachia Poliphili*) in May. Nettle = *flammula* = symbol of youth = "most prone to the flames of lust" — Venus reigns in May as well as Mary. Anachmus = Aniadus (indistinguishable).

### §3: The Natural Transformation Mystery (pars. 194-196)

**Aniadus as inner man** (par. 194): Ruland: Aniadus = "the regenerated spiritual man in us, the heavenly body implanted in us Christians by the Holy Ghost through the most Holy Sacraments." Paracelsus uses arcane language deliberately to segregate the "natural" transformation mystery from the religious one. More pagan than Christian; closer to *Hypnerotomachia Poliphili* than the Church. Paracelsus as physician answered psychological questions from knowledge of nature, not from "should."

**Paracelsus's daemons as reality** (par. 195): His homunculi, Trarames, Durdales, nymphs, Melusines = "grossest superstitions for us so-called moderns" but living forces in his day. They were projections, "but this reality did far greater justice to their psychological effect than does our rationalistic assumption of the absolute unreality of projected contents."

**KEY JUDGMENT** (par. 196): The *opus alchymicum* claimed equality with the *opus divinum* of the Mass — "not grotesque presumption but the fact that a vast, unknown Nature, disregarded by the eternal verities of the Church, was imperiously demanding recognition and acceptance." Even without gold: "he was yet on the track of a process of psychic transformation that is incomparably more important for the happiness of the individual than the possession of the red tincture."

### §3A: The Light of the Darkness (pars. 197-199)

**The two lights** (par. 197): The *lumen naturae* vs. revealed light: "In the very darkness of nature a light is hidden, a little spark without which the darkness would not be darkness." Paracelsus's creative yearning belonged to the *lumen naturae*, "the divine spark buried in the darkness." The light from above made darkness darker; *lumen naturae* "is the light of the darkness itself, which illuminates its own darkness." It "turns blackness into brightness, burns away 'all superfluities,' and leaves behind nothing but 'faecem et scoriam et terram damnatam.'" See [[Lumen-Naturae]].

**Union of natures** (par. 198): "Not separation of the natures but union of the natures was the goal of alchemy." Democritus: "Nature rejoices in nature, nature conquers nature, nature rules over nature." The hierosgamos of Sol and Luna = the gamonymus/hierosgamos of light and darkness. The Great Magic Papyrus of Paris — the "Secret Inscription" — quoted in full as an example of the religious feeling embedded in magical sources.

**The symbol as tertium** (par. 199): "Here only the symbol helps, for... it represents the 'tertium' that in logic does not exist, but which in reality is the living truth." The symbol "not only conveys a visualization of the process but also brings a re-experiencing of it." "When the heavenly marriage is accomplished, who will deny its superexcellent virtue?"

### §3B: The Union of Man's Two Natures (pars. 200-205)

**Man's two life forces** (par. 200): Paracelsus: man has two life forces — one natural, the other "aerial, wherein is nothing of the body." = physiological + psychic.

**The aerial life and the anima iliastri** (par. 201): Those who live longest have lived the "aerial life" (*vitam aeream*) — 600 to 1100 years — imitating Aniadus "by means of the air alone" — i.e., by psychic means. The *anima iliastri* = non-sentient (*impassibilis*) soul dwelling in the fire of the heart ("the sun in the Microcosm"). The cagastric soul = *passibilis*, "floats" on the water of the capsule. When the *anima iliastri* is filled with air and moved to the centre (outside the *capsula cordis*), it "resounds only as Aniadus, Adech, and Edochinum. Whence comes the birth of that great Aquaster, which is born beyond Nature."

**Soul freed from the body** (par. 202): The soul brought into the centre is now outside its previous habitation — a degree of freedom from bondage. In the liberated sphere, "unheard by the body," the soul can re-echo the upper triad: Aniadus, Adech, and Edochinum.

**The upper triad** (par. 203): Adech = inner *homo maximus*; Aniadus = springtime efficacy; Edochinum = variant of Enochdianus (Enoch "tasted not death"). Three names = amplifications of the same conception — the deathless Original Man. The *homo maximus* concept explains why the lapis had a "thousand names": elixir, panacea, tincture, quintessence, light, east, morning, Aries, living fount, fruit-tree, animal, Adam, man, *homo altus*, *pater mirabilis*, king, hermaphrodite, *deus terrenus, salvator, servator, filius macrocosmi*. See [[Anthropos]].

**Dorn on the Aquaster** (par. 204): The three (Aniadus, Adech, Edochinum) form the one "pure and well-tempered element" (*elementum purum temperatum*). "From the Aniadic mother, with the aid of Adech and through the power of imagination, comes the great vision, which impregnates the supernatural matrix so that it gives birth to the invisible foetus of longevity." Dorn insists on THREE vs. FOUR (polemical stance against the axiom of Maria) — but overlooks that the FOURTH = the microcosmic mortal man, who "complements the upper triad."

**Vita cosmographica** (par. 205): Union with the *homo maximus* → "vita cosmographica." In this life "time appears as well as the body Jesahach." *Locus* = time as well as space. *Corpus Jesahach* = *corpus glorificationis* = the resurrected body of the alchemists = *corpus astrale*.

### §C: The Quaternity of the Homo Maximus (pars. 206-212)

**The Scaiolae** (par. 206): Paracelsus's four "spiritual powers of the mind" (*spirituales mentis vires*) = fourfold, corresponding to the four elements = the four wheels of Elijah's chariot. Originate in the mind of man, "from whom they depart and to whom they are turned back." "In them is nothing of mortality" — whoever lives by them is immortal. See [[Scaiolae]].

**Four functions** (par. 207): Ruland interprets: *phantasia, imaginatio, speculatio, agnata fides* = four empirical functions: thinking, feeling, sensation, intuition. Every archetype is a *fascinosum* that "excites and grips the imagination" and clothes itself in religious ideas. The four Scaiolae also = four articles of Christian faith (baptism, Christ-belief, Last Supper, love of neighbour). The *homo maximus* = "the basis and cause of all division into four: four elements, seasons, directions, etc."

**Mortal / immortal paradox** (par. 208): Contradiction: in other writings the *lumen naturae* is mortal; here the Scaiolae are immortal, belonging to the *Archa* (principle that existed before the world). Resolution: Paracelsus was working from "intuitive introspection, which was able to grasp the quaternary structure of consciousness and its archetypal nature. The one [mortal man] is mortal, the other [archetypal man] immortal."

**The prima materia and the labor Sophiae** (par. 209): The prima materia is "tantalizing" — "cheap as dirt and can be had everywhere, only nobody knows it." Saturnine: "thrown out into the street," "cast on the dunghill," "found in filth." The alchemist's perplexity reflects "his psychic background, which animates the darkness lying before him, so that he discovers in the projection the qualities of the unconscious." The *labor Sophiae* = "a process of coming to terms with the unconscious, which always sets in when a man is confronted with its darkness."

**§D: The Rapprochement with the Unconscious** (par. 210): Goethe's *Faust* Part II = "an alchemical encounter with the unconscious, comparable to the *labor Sophiae* of Paracelsus." The finite consciousness confronted with its archaic foundations — the mortal ego with the immortal Self, "Anthropos, purusha, atman." "That difficult Adech who crosses [the ego's] purpose at every misguided step, who gives fate an unexpected twist, and sets it as a task the very thing it feared." The Anthropos doctrine was dangerous because "from that point of view Christ was a reflection — and only a reflection — of the inner Anthropos." See [[Anthropos]].

**The quaternity as city** (par. 212): "He implants himself in the Scaiolae, the quaternity of the Original Man." Quaternity = Paradise with four rivers = Metropolis with four gates; alchemical equivalent = *domus sapientiae* + squared circle. Adech deflects the *propositum* (purpose) but not the *modus* (procedure) — the opus always remains the same general method.

### §4: The Commentary of Gerard Dorn (pars. 213-214)

**The final chapter** (par. 213): *De vita longa* concludes in an "extremely condensed way." Paracelsus's last chapter = the most obscure in all his writings. "One would be inclined to let the whole treatise go hang did it not contain things which seem to belong to the most modern psychological insights."

**The original text and Dorn's gloss** (par. 214): Paracelsus (*De vita longa*, 1562, Lib. V, cap. V): "Through the intervention of the Scaiolae, Melusina departs from her nymphididic nature to remain in another transmutation if that difficult Adech permit." Goal: "that life over which Aniadus dominates and reigns, and which endures for ever with him, in whom we are present without end. And herewith we end our discourse on longevity."

Dorn's gloss (1583): **"Melusina, i.e., the vision appearing in the mind** (*apparentem in mente visionem*)." The Cyphanta (distilling vessel): "the supermonic figments, that is, enigmatical speculations, open a window, that is, the understanding." To become fixed, they must "oppose the acts of Melusina" = resist the fleeting nymphididic quality until the vision crystallizes into stable knowledge. The characters of Venus / the year Aniadin = immortal life achieved through imagination. See [[Melusina]].

### §4A: Melusina and the Process of Individuation (pars. 215-222)

**Scaiolae as organs** (par. 215): The four Scaiolae = "the four parts, limbs, or emanations of the Anthropos" — the organs by which the *homo maximus* actively intervenes in the world. As *imaginatio, speculatio, phantasia, fides*, they stimulate Melusina to change from water-nixie to human form. Dorn: "a vision appearing in the mind" — not a projection on a real woman. Lady Polia (*Hypnerotomachia Poliphili*) dissolves on the first day of May: "Poliphilo, most dear beloved, farewell!" Helen in *Faust II* vanishes with Euphorion's dissolution.

**Anima at the catastrophic threshold** (par. 216): Melusina appears at the "sudden collapse of a form or style of life." At this moment: "an abysmal void that is now suddenly filled with an alluring vision, the palpably real presence of a strange yet helpful being." Her reality = both psychic vision and "distinct objective entity, like a dream which temporarily becomes reality." See [[Melusina]].

**Raymond of Poitiers legend** (pars. 217-218): Raymond accidentally kills his adoptive father, flees in despair, finds Melusina at a bubbling spring — "who by her clever counsel saved him from dishonour." The anima appears at the moment of catastrophic collapse. Legend roots: Celtic, Indian (Urvashi/Pururavas), North American Indian. Melusina = Mercurius = both anima and Wise Old Man (*senex*).

**Ministering spirit** (par. 219): Alchemists needed a *πάρεδρος* to whose invocation the Greek Magic Papyri bear witness. The snake = universal form of the revelation god.

**KEY — Individuation defined** (par. 220): "There is no doubt that the goal of the philosophical alchemist was higher self-development, or the production of what Paracelsus calls the *homo maior*, or **what I would call individuation**."

**KEY — Wholeness** (par. 221): "Not his ego, that fragment of a personality, is meant; it is rather that **a wholeness, of which he is a part, wants to be transformed from a latent state of unconsciousness into an approximate consciousness of itself.**"

**The veil of Maya** (par. 222): The "acts of Melusina" = "deceptive phantasms compounded of supreme sense and the most pernicious nonsense, a veritable veil of Maya." The wise man extracts the *liquor Sophiae* from these phantasms by distillation. "**Stupidity is the mother of the wise, but cleverness never.**" The "fixation" = alchemically the lapis; psychologically **the consolidation of feeling**.

### §4B: The Hierosgamos of the Everlasting Man (pars. 223-227)

**Melusina as Shakti** (par. 223): "Melusina, the deceptive Shakti, must return to the watery realm if the work is to reach its goal... she must become what she was from the beginning: a part of his wholeness." "Conceived in the mind" = union of conscious and unconscious → "an unassailable feeling of eternity or timelessness."

**Melusina = Morgana = Aphrodite** (par. 225): Union with the feminine personification of the unconscious = "a well-nigh eschatological experience." Christian form: Apocalyptic Marriage of the Lamb (Rev. 19:6-10). The angel who says "I am thy fellowservant, and of thy brethren" = in Paracelsus's language, "the *homo maior*, Adech."

**KEY** (par. 226): "**Adech is not my self, he is also that of my brothers.**" The coniunctio experience: "the self which includes me includes many others also, for the unconscious that is 'conceived in our mind' does not belong to me and is not peculiar to me, but is everywhere. **It is the quintessence of the individual and at the same time the collective.**"

**Goal** (par. 227): "The year Aniadin," time of perfection, "when the One Man reigns for ever." See [[Coniunctio]], [[Self (archetype)]].

### §4C: Spirit and Nature (pars. 228-230)

**Why Venus, not the Mother of God** (par. 228): Paracelsus and Colonna both used Venus because "the Christian formula inevitably suggested the Christian solution." *Chymical Wedding* (Rosencreutz): underground chamber with copper door (copper = Venus/Aphrodite); Venus found asleep, "revealed in all her naked beauty."

**Nature cannot lose** (par. 229): "You may pitch Nature out with a fork, yet she'll always come back again." The unconscious is not a "dustbin of consciousness" but "a largely autonomous psychic system for compensating the biases and aberrations of the conscious attitude... it also extends beyond consciousness and, with its symbols, anticipates future conscious processes. It is therefore quite as much a **'supra-consciousness.'**" See [[Lumen-Naturae]].

**Moral signposts** (par. 230): Convictions = "reliable signposts for certain stretches of the way." Moral values enjoy "permanent validity" as far as essential human nature remains the same. But "sometimes the Gordian knot can only be cut with a very unchristian sword."

### §4D: The Ecclesiastical Sacrament and the Opus Alchymicum (pars. 231-233)

**Alchemy = unconscious groping** (par. 231): None of the alchemists "ever had any clear idea of what his philosophy was really about." Paracelsus was "trying to grasp the ungraspable with his countless esotericisms, and snatched at any symbolic hint that the unconscious offered. Using the old remnants to give new form to a renewed archetypal experience."

**The sacrament of the opus** (par. 232): "A procedure for attaining nothing less than immortality." The *opus alchymicum* replaces the ecclesiastical sacraments "less by word than by deed, and without the least sign of any conflict with the orthodox Christian standpoint."

**Final word** (par. 233): "Which way did Paracelsus hold to be the true one? Or were both of them true for him? Presumably the latter, and the rest he 'leaves to the theoreticians to discuss.'"

### §4E: The Characters of Venus and the Final Conclusion (pars. 234-236)

**Venus's characters** (par. 234): The "characters of Venus" = sapphire, *cheyri*, *ladanum*, *muscus*, *ambra* — all sacred to Venus per Agrippa. Venus = *docta, sublimis, magistra rerum humanarum divinarumque*. Dorn: "shield and buckler of love" — but *Venus armata* is also a figure (*Amor* = son of Venus and Mars, i.e. the coniunctio). Dorn's polemical attitude to alchemy made him prefer Christianized love. But for Paracelsus, Venus Magistra or Aphrodite Urania or Sophia = more appropriate to the mystery of the *lumen naturae*. The Venus episode in *Chymical Wedding* = more relevant than Dorn's "well-meant circumlocution."

**The year Aniadin** (par. 235): "A life without end" under the dominion of Aniadus = Rev. 20:4 — "they lived and reigned with Christ a thousand years." The year Aniadin = the thousand-year reign of the Apocalypse.

**Final conclusion** (par. 236): "In conclusion I would remark that the survey of the secret doctrine which I have attempted to sketch here makes it seem likely that besides the physician and Christian in Paracelsus there was also an alchemical philosopher at work who, pushing every analogy to the very limit, strove to penetrate the divine mysteries." The parallel with Christian mysteries = "a most dangerous conflict" — no Gnostic heresy for him, yet "man had been entrusted with the task of bringing to perfection the divine will implanted in nature, and this was a **truly sacramental work**." Lazarello's reply to Pope Alexander VI: "I am a Christian, O King, and it is no disgrace to be that and an Hermetic at the same time."

### 5. Epilogue (pars. 237-238)

**Alchemy and psychology** (par. 237): "I had long been aware that alchemy is not only the mother of chemistry, but is also the forerunner of our modern psychology of the unconscious. Thus Paracelsus appears as a pioneer not only of chemical medicine but of empirical psychology and psychotherapy."

**The two Paracelsuses** (par. 238): "That other Paracelsus, whose soul was intermingled with a strange spiritual current which, issuing from immemorial sources, flowed beyond him into a distant future. But—***ex tenebris lux***—it was precisely because he was so fascinated by magic that he was able to open the door to the realities of nature for the benefit of succeeding centuries." "The Christian and the primitive pagan lived together in him in a strange and marvellous way to form a conflicting whole." **"As a man he had one father, but as a spirit he had two mothers."** His spirit was "heroic, because creative, and as such was doomed to Promethean guilt." The secular conflict at the turn of the sixteenth century = "prerequisite for higher consciousness; for analysis is always followed by synthesis, and **what was divided on a lower level will reappear, united, on a higher one.**"

## IV. The Spirit Mercurius (pars. 239-404)

*Given as two lectures at the Eranos Conference, Ascona, Switzerland, 1942. Theme: "The Hermetic Principle in Mythology, Gnosis, and Alchemy." Published as "Der Geist Mercurius," Eranos-Jahrbuch 1942; revised in Symbolik des Geistes (1948). English translation by Gladys Phelan and Hildegard Nagel, 1953.*

**Epigraph (Magic Papyrus, Preisendanz II, p. 139)**:
> Hermes, ruler of the world, dweller in the heart, circle of the moon, Round and square, inventor of the words of the tongue, Obedient to justice, wearer of the chlamys, shod in winged sandals, Guardian of the many-sounding tongue, prophet to mortals.

### Part I: §1 — The Spirit in the Bottle (pars. 239-246)

**The Grimm fairytale** (par. 239): The "Spirit in the Bottle" — a poor woodcutter's son finds a sealed glass bottle among the roots of an immense oak. A spirit rushes out and threatens to strangle him ("I have had my punishment and I will be revenged! I am the great and mighty spirit Mercurius!"). The boy tricks the spirit back into the bottle, extracts a promise of reward, lets it out, and receives a healing cloth (one end heals wounds, other turns iron to silver). Jung: this story contains "the quintessence and deepest meaning of the Hermetic mystery as it has come down to us today."

**Fairytales = unconscious self-statement** (par. 240): Fairytales = "spontaneous statements of the unconscious about itself" — to be treated like dreams.

**The forest and the oak** (par. 241): The forest = "container of the unknown and the mysterious... appropriate synonym for the unconscious." The oak = "prototype of the **self**, a symbol of the source and goal of the individuation process." The hero = one of the "sleepers," "blind" or "blindfolded" — "the unawakened who are still unconscious of themselves, who have not yet integrated their future, more extensive personality, their 'wholeness.'" See [[Philosophical-Tree]].

**The spirit in the roots** (par. 242): The secret is hidden in the ROOTS (not the top) of the tree. The spirit has personality — voice, speech, conscious purpose. Caught by a magician (= alchemist) and imprisoned.

**KEY** (par. 243): The spirit = *spiritus vegetativus* of the tree = **"*principium individuationis*"** — "the spiritual quintessence abstracted from it." "The tree would then be the outward and visible sign of the realization of the self." *Aurelia occulta*: "The philosophers have sought most eagerly for the centre of the tree which stands in the midst of the earthly paradise." Christ himself is this tree. Eulogius of Alexandria: "Behold in the Father the root, in the Son the branch, and in the Spirit the fruit." Mercurius: *trinus et unus*. Self has its roots in the body — "indeed in the body's chemical elements."

**The Master who imprisoned Mercurius** (par. 244): The mercurial essence (*principium individuationis*) "would have developed freely under natural conditions, but was robbed of its freedom by deliberate intervention from outside, and was artfully confined and banished like an evil spirit." Who imprisoned it? "The principle of individuation is the source of all evil" (Schopenhauer, Buddhism; also Christian original sin). "God himself is this Master — and the alchemist, in his small way, competes with the Creator in so far as he strives to do work analogous to the work of creation."

**The vas Hermeticum** (par. 245): Natural evil banished to the "roots" = the body. The glass bottle = *vas Hermeticum*, "hermetically sealed" (= sealed with the sign of Hermes). Must be round = to represent the cosmos. Glass = solidified water or air (both synonyms for spirit). The alchemical retort = **the *anima mundi***, "which according to an old alchemical conception surrounds the cosmos." The "spiritual" (= *aethereus*) philosophers' stone = precious *vitrum* (*malleabile*) = the *aurum vitreum* of the heavenly Jerusalem (Rev. 21:21).

**Mercurius as Wotan** (par. 246): The German fairytale calls the spirit "Mercurius" = the pagan god = German national god Wotan. Mercurius = "the soul of the metals, the metallic man (*homunculus*), the dragon (*serpens mercurialis*), the roaring fiery lion, the night raven (*nycticorax*), and the black eagle — the last four being synonyms for the devil." The fairytale interprets the evil spirit as a pagan god "forced under the influence of Christianity to descend into the dark underworld and be morally disqualified." Hermes → demon of the mysteries; Wotan → demon of forest and storm. Like the devil: bestows wealth by changing base metal into gold; also gets tricked. See [[Mercurius]].

### §2: The Connection Between Spirit and Tree (pars. 247-249)

**Spirit imprisoned under the oak** (par. 247): Not identical with the tree or its roots — put there by artificial means. Landmark for a hidden treasure. Primitive animism: tree and daemon originally one and the same — a nature deity, a *tremendum*, morally neutral. The separation = "a secondary phenomenon corresponding to a higher level of culture and consciousness."

**Five levels of consciousness** (par. 248): A history of how consciousness has treated the psychic phenomenon:
1. Primitive: tree and daemon = one (animism)
2. Differentiation: tree ≠ psychic content ("an act of enlightenment")
3. Moral: separated content declared "evil" spirit
4. Modern rationalism: denies objective existence; "auditory hallucination" — the whole phenomenon "vanishes into thin air"
5. Level 5: "wonders about this conjuring trick" — "But what, then, was all the rustling in the woods?" Affirms: "a phenomenon thrusting up from the unconscious, the existence of which cannot be denied if one is minded to grant the psyche any kind of reality." If level 4 were right, one would have to "extend God's *creatio ex nihilo*... to include steam engines, automobiles, radios" — the Creator renamed "Conglomeratio."

**KEY** (par. 249): "The unconscious exists and has a reality just like any other existent... the 'spirit' is also a reality, and the 'evil' spirit at that. **The distinction between 'good' and 'evil' is suddenly no longer obsolete, but highly topical and necessary.**" See [[Mercurius]].

### §3: The Problem of Freeing Mercurius (pars. 250-251)

**The boy was alchemically incorrect** (par. 250): Mercurius's gifts don't vanish by morning — his gold stays silver, his cloth keeps healing power. He is "tricked into his own better nature." But the alchemist would be horrified: he "always sealed the stopper with magic signs and set it for a very long time over the lowest fire, so that '**he who is within may not fly out.**' For if he escapes, the whole laborious opus comes to nothing and has to be started all over again." The boy received the self-renewing tincture — "the opus needed to be performed only once" — but he could never produce it again himself.

**KEY** (par. 251): "The behaviour of the boy... must be described as alchemically incorrect." He infringed on "the legitimate claims of an unknown Master." The alchemists wanted to **keep Mercurius in the bottle in order to transform him**. The golden age of alchemy = 16th–17th centuries — "at that time a storm bird did indeed escape from a spiritual vessel." Lead: "so bedevilled and shameless that all who wish to investigate it fall into madness through ignorance." Same said of Mercurius: "**a real trickster who drove the alchemists to despair.**"

### Part II: §1 — Introductory (pars. 252-254)

**Alchemy as Gnostic doctrine** (par. 252): Reitzenstein (philologist) recognized "the mythological and Gnostic ideas embedded in alchemy." Western alchemical literature in four groups: (1) Greek texts / Arab transmission, 1st–8th c.; (2) Early Latinists / Harranite school + Geber/Avicenna, 9th–13th c.; (3) Later Latinists, 14th–17th c. (principal group); (4) Modern European languages, 16th–17th c. "**Alchemy contains as its core the Gnostic doctrine of the Anthropos and by its very nature has the character of a peculiar doctrine of redemption.**"

**Participation mystique and psychic reality** (par. 253): "Prolonged preoccupation with an unknown object acts as an almost irresistible bait for the unconscious to project itself into the unknown nature of the object." The Bororos think themselves red cockatoos. Alchemical fantasies = "psychic material contaminated with chemical substances" stemming from the collective unconscious. "**Alchemy, so barren a field for the chemist, is for the psychologist a veritable gold-mine of materials which throw an exceedingly valuable light on the structure of the unconscious.**"

### §2: Mercurius as Quicksilver and/or Water (par. 255)

*Hydrargyrum* (Hg) / *argentum vivum* — as such, called *vulgaris* and *crudus*. The *mercurius philosophicus* distinguished from common quicksilver. Water names: "aqua manus non madefaciens" (water that does not make the hands wet); *aqua vitae*; *aqua alba*; *aqua sicca* (dry water — paradoxical); *aqua septies distillata* (seven times distilled); *humiditas radicalis* (*incombustibilis et unctuosa*); *aqua aurea*; ὕδωρ θεῖον (divine water in Greek texts). Doctrine of *humidum radicale* = original root-moisture of all life. See [[Aqua-Permanens]], [[Mercurius]].

### §3: Mercurius as Fire (pars. 256-257)

**Fire as well as water** (par. 256): *Ignis elementaris*, *noster naturalis ignis certissimus*. *Aqua mercurialis* = "even a divine fire." "Really the only fire in the whole procedure." "**An 'invisible fire, working in secret.'**" Heart at the North Pole = northern lights. "The universal and scintillating fire of the **light of nature**, which carries the heavenly spirit within it" — connects Mercurius directly to the [[Lumen-Naturae]].

The *ignis mercurialis* = fires of hell — but alchemists viewed hell "as an internal component of the deity." **"The concept of an all-encompassing God must necessarily include his opposite."** *Coincidentia oppositorum* cannot be too extreme "otherwise God would cancel himself out." "The principle of the coincidence of opposites must therefore be completed by that of **absolute opposition** in order to attain full paradoxicality and hence psychological validity."

**Mercurial fire at earth's centre** (par. 257): Benedictus Figulus: "Visit the centre of the earth, there you will find the global fire." Another treatise: "the secret, infernal fire, the wonder of the world, **the system of the higher powers in the lower**." See [[Mercurius]].

**Hell-fire as rearranged heavenly power, salamander image** (par. 258): Hell-fire is "none other than a rearrangement of the heavenly, spiritual powers in the lower, chthonic world of matter" — already in St. Paul's time thought ruled by the devil. Hell-fire = "the manifest counterpart of the spiritual and the good, and essentially identical with it in substance." Another treatise calls mercurial fire "the fire in which God himself burns in divine love." Mercurius, being of fiery nature, is not harmed by fire — "he remains unchanged within it, rejoicing like the salamander." (Unlike literal quicksilver, which vaporizes under heat — the alchemists knew this; the *philosophic* Mercurius is not the literal metal.)

### §4: Mercurius as Spirit and Soul (pars. 259-266)

**Why so many names were needed** (par. 259): If Mercurius were simply quicksilver, none of the many appellations would have been necessary. "Our" Mercurius = the essence, moisture, or principle behind/within quicksilver — "that indefinable, fascinating, irritating, and elusive thing which attracts an unconscious projection." The philosophic Mercurius = *servus fugitivus* or *cervus fugitivus* (fugitive slave/stag) — a highly important unconscious content. **"The philosophic Mercurius... is obviously a projection of the unconscious, such as always takes place when the inquiring mind lacks the necessary self-criticism in investigating an unknown quantity."**

**A. Mercurius as an Aerial Spirit** (par. 260-261): The alchemists defined the psychic nature of the arcane substance as "spirit" and "soul." Hermes (originally a wind god) and Egyptian Thoth ("makes the souls to breathe") are forerunners of Mercurius in his aerial aspect — *pneuma* and *spiritus* in the original concrete sense of "air in motion." Mercurius called *aereus et volans* (winged), *totus aereus et spiritualis*; the *lapis elevatus cum vento* (stone uplifted by the wind); *spirituale corpus* and *spiritus visibilis, tamen impalpabilis* (visible yet impalpable spirit). Penotus (pupil of Paracelsus): Mercurius is "nothing other than the spirit of the world become body within the earth" — showing the contamination of spirit and matter typical of medieval thought, since the *spiritus mundi* was the spirit ruling nature, not just a pervasive gas. Mylius: Mercurius is an "intermediate substance" (*media substantia*) = *anima media natura* — "spirit and soul of the bodies."

**B. Mercurius as Soul** (par. 262-263): "Soul" is a higher concept than "spirit" in the airy sense — the "subtle body" or "breath-soul," non-material and finer than air, whose essential characteristic is to animate and be animated = the life principle. Mercurius designated *anima* (hence feminine: *foemina*, *virgo*) or *nostra anima* — "nostra" here refers to the arcane substance, not literally "our own." *Anima* often connected with or equated to *spiritus* — hence *spiritus vegetativus* (spirit of life) or *spiritus seminalis*. Mercurius as "life-giving power like a glue, holding the world together and standing in the middle between body and spirit" → leads to identification with the **anima mundi**. Avicenna (12th-13th c.): "He is the spirit of the Lord which fills the whole world and in the beginning swam upon the waters... the spirit of Truth, which is hidden from the world." Another text: the "supracelestial spirit which is conjoined with the light," rightly called *anima mundi*. Alchemists linked the *anima mundi* to both Plato's world-soul in the *Timaeus* and the Holy Spirit (present at Creation, *obumbratio* of Mary). "Mercurius non vulgaris" = a "life-force" that "flies like solid white snow" — the snow represents purified Mercurius in the state of *albedo* (= spirituality). **Key duality**: the immortal *anima rationalis* given by God to man (distinguishing him from animals) vs. the mercurial life-soul connected with the *inflatio*/*inspiratio* of the Holy Spirit — "this fundamental duality forms the psychological basis of the two sources of illumination."

**C. Mercurius as Spirit in the Incorporeal, Metaphysical Sense** (pars. 264-266): Dorn: "Mercurius possesses the quality of an incorruptible spirit, which is like the soul, and because of its incorruptibility is called intellectual" — i.e. pertaining to the *mundus intelligibilis*. Other texts: "spiritual and hyperphysical"; "the spirit of Mercurius comes from heaven." Laurentius Ventura (associating with neo-Platonist Harranite ideas) defines the spirit of Mercurius as "completely and entirely like itself" (*sibi omnino similis*) and *simplex* — Harranite texts define the arcane substance as the *res simplex*, equating it with God. The oldest reference to the mercurial *pneuma*: an Ostanes quotation — "Go to the streamings of the Nile, and there you will find a stone that has a spirit." In Zosimos, Mercurius is incorporeal (ἀσώματον); another author calls him ethereal (αἰθερῶδες πνεῦμα) and become rational/wise (σώφρων γενομένη). In "Isis to Horus," the divine water is brought by the angel Amnael — of celestial or daemonic origin, since Amnael is not morally irreproachable. Mercurius has a secret connection with the goddess of love (Aphrodite pours quicksilver from a vessel in the "Book of Krates"; the *Chymical Wedding* of Christian Rosencreutz centers on the visit to the sleeping Venus). **Par. 266 KEY**: The fact that Mercurius is interpreted as spirit and soul, despite the spirit-body dilemma this involves, shows the alchemists conceived of their arcane substance as what we today call a **psychic phenomenon**. "Whatever else spirit and soul may be, from the phenomenological point of view they are psychic structures." Water and fire are classic opposites and can be valid definitions of one and the same thing only if that thing unites in itself the contrary qualities of both. **"The psychologem 'Mercurius' must therefore possess an essentially antinomian dual nature."**

### §5: The Dual Nature of Mercurius (pars. 267-269)

Mercurius is many-sided, changeable, deceitful — "that inconstant Mercurius," *versipellis* (changing his skin), *duplex*. He "runs round the earth and enjoys equally the company of the good and the wicked." He is "two dragons," the "twin," made of "two natures" or "two substances" — the "giant of twofold substance" (text cites Matthew 26, the Last Supper, making the Christ analogy explicit). As the dragon he is "winged and wingless"; an "ever-waking dragon... covered with eyes on both sides of his body... and he sleeps with some open and some closed" (Pantophthalmos). There is the "common and philosophic" Mercurius; he consists of "the dry and earthy, the moist and viscous" — two passive elements (earth, water), two active (air, fire). He is both good and evil.

**The "Aurelia occulta" self-description** (par. 267, full quote filed as key quote below): "I am the poison-dripping dragon, who is everywhere... My water and fire destroy and put together... I bestow on you the powers of the male and the female, and also those of heaven and of earth... By the philosophers I am named Mercurius; my spouse is the [philosophic] gold; I am the old dragon... father and mother, young and old, very strong and very weak, death and resurrection, visible and invisible, hard and soft... I am the highest and the lowest, the lightest and the heaviest... I contain the light of nature; I am dark and light... I am known and yet do not exist at all."

**Hermaphroditism** (par. 268): Because of his united double nature, Mercurius is hermaphroditic — sometimes body masculine/soul feminine, sometimes reversed. The *Rosarium philosophorum* has both versions: as *vulgaris* he is the dead masculine body; as "our" Mercurius he is feminine, spiritual, alive, life-giving. Also husband and wife, bridegroom and bride, lover and beloved. His contrary natures are *Mercurius sensu strictiori* (feminine, earth, Eve) and sulphur (masculine, water, Adam). Dorn: the "true hermaphroditic Adam." Khunrath: "begotten of the hermaphroditic seed of the Macrocosm," "an immaculate birth from the hermaphroditic matter" (= prima materia). Mylius: the "hermaphroditic monster." As Adam he is also the microcosm, or "the heart of the microcosm," containing the four elements and the *quinta essentia* called Heaven. *Coelum* for Mercurius predates Paracelsus, found earlier in Johannes de Rupescissa (14th c.). Named the "Philosophic ambisexual Man." In the "Dicta Belini," the "man rising from the river" (probably referencing the vision of Ezra); illustrated in Trismosin's *Splendor solis* (16th c.) — possibly traceable to the Babylonian Oannes. Abraham le Juif forgery calls Mercurius **Adam Kadmon** — an unmistakable continuation of the **Gnostic doctrine of the Anthropos**, which "coincides with the psychological concept of the self," as do the *atman*/*purusha* doctrines.

**Senex and puer** (par. 269): Another dual aspect — Hermes as old man (attested archaeologically) relates him to Saturn. Mercurius "truly consists of the most extreme opposites; on the one hand he is undoubtedly akin to the godhead, on the other he is found in sewers." Rosinus (Zosimos) calls him the *terminus ani*; in the Bundahish, the anus of Garotman ("hell") is "like hell on earth."

### §6: The Unity and Trinity of Mercurius (pars. 270-271+)

**Unity within duality** (par. 270): Despite his obvious duality, Mercurius's unity is emphasized, especially in his form as the lapis: "In all the world he is One." This unity is simultaneously a trinity — explicitly echoing the Holy Trinity, though the triadic nature predates Christian dogma (as early as Zosimos's treatise *peri aretēs*). Martial calls Hermes *omnia solus el ter unus* (All and Thrice One). Three-headed Hermes worshipped at Monakris (Arcadia); three-headed Mercurius in Gaul, a psychopomp. Triadic character = attribute of underworld gods generally (three-bodied Typhon, three-bodied/three-faced Hecate, the τριτοπάτορες/"ancestors" with serpent bodies, per Cicero — three sons of Zeus the King, called wind-gods; cf. Hopi belief that snakes are flashes of lightning). Khunrath: Mercurius *triunus* and *ternarius*; Mylius represents him as a three-headed snake. "Aquarium sapientum": Mercurius is a "triune, universal essence which is named Jehova... He is divine and at the same time human."

**Mercurius = Azoth, the All** (par. 271): Mercurius corresponds not only to Christ but to the triune divinity in general. The "Aurelia occulta" calls him **Azoth**: "he is the A and O that is everywhere present. The philosophers have adorned [him] with the name Azoth, which is compounded of the A and Z of the Latins, the alpha and omega of the Greeks, and the aleph and tau of the Hebrews."

**Par. 272 — Uroboros and the three-and-four problem**: Mercurius's ability to beget himself relates him to the Godhead and to the primitive creator god. "Allegoriae super librum Turbae": "The mother bore me and is herself begotten of me." As the uroboros dragon, he impregnates, begets, bears, devours, and slays himself ("himself lifts himself on high," *Rosarium*). Despite being *trinus et unus*, he also strongly shares the **quaternity** of the lapis, exemplifying the 3-vs-4 dilemma — Maria Prophetissa's axiom. *Hermes tetracephalus* as well as *Hermes tricephalus*. Sabaean temple: triangle inside a square. Scholia to "Tractatus aureus": Mercurius's sign = square inside a triangle surrounded by a circle = "symbol of totality."

### §7: The Relation of Mercurius to Astrology and the Doctrine of the Archons (pars. 273-277)

Roots of alchemical Mercurius philosophy: ancient astrology and Gnostic doctrine of archons and aeons. Quicksilver = planet Mercury (= sun in the earth). Both metal and planetary spirit could be personified as a *paredros* or "familiar."

**Mercurius as mystagogue** (par. 273): Plays the mystagogue in the *Corpus Hermeticum*, Zosimos's visions, and *Aurelia occulta* (Anthropos with a crown of stars). Child of sun and moon; also begetter of his parents (gold gets its qualities from Mercurius, per Wei Po-yang). Half-feminine → identified with moon and Venus — "most chaste virgin," yet ithyphallic.

**Mercurius-Saturn identity** (par. 274): Most important astrological connection = **Saturn**. Mercurius *senex* = identical with Saturn. For the earlier alchemists, lead (not quicksilver) usually represents the prima materia. Arabic *Turba*: quicksilver = "water of the moon and of Saturn." Saturn: "My spirit is the water that loosens the rigid limbs of my brothers" = *eternal water* = Mercurius. Saturn is hermaphroditic, "old man on a mountain with all four elements bound in him." Saturn = father and origin of Mercurius → "Saturn's child." Gnostic parallel: Kronos = "power of the colour of water" (ὑδατόχρους), destroys everything.

**The lion** (par. 275): Manifestation of Mercurius in transformation = the lion. Khunrath: "luring the lion out of Saturn's mountain cave." In Gnosticism, Saturn = highest archon = lion-headed **Ialdabaoth** ("child of chaos"). In alchemy: child of chaos = Mercurius.

**Mercurius and the devil** (par. 276): Saturn = dwelling-place of devil himself. Sulphur (masculine principle of Mercurius) = *diabolus*. Mercurius is not exactly the Evil One but contains him — "Good with the good, evil with the evil" (Khunrath). His nature more exactly defined as a **process** beginning with evil and ending with good.

***Verus Hermes* poem** (par. 276, 1620): "A weakling babe, a greybeard old, / Surnamed the Dragon: me they hold / In darkest dungeon languishing / That I may be reborn a king... / To a black crow am I akin... / O that the Three would make the One! / O soul, O spirit with me stay... / Hero of peace, come forth from me, / Whom the whole world would like to see!"

**Projection of individuation** (par. 277): The poem = Mercurius's transformation = the mystic transformation of the *artifex* = projection of the **individuation process** (a natural psychic occurrence going on even without consciousness). When consciousness participates with understanding, the process is accompanied by all the emotions of a religious revelation. Result: Mercurius identified with **Sapientia and the Holy Ghost**. Heretical lineage: Euchites, Paulicians, Bogomils, Cathars → developed the Paraclete concept → continued in alchemy, "partly unconsciously and partly under a deliberate disguise."

### §8: Mercurius and Hermes (pars. 278-281)

Classical character of Hermes faithfully reproduced in alchemical Mercurius — partly unconscious repetition, partly spontaneous re-experience, partly conscious reference to the pagan god.

**Michael Maier** (par. 278): consciously alluded to Hermes as *hodēgos* (ὁδηγός, pointer of the way); found a statue pointing to paradise. Erythraean Sibyl: "He will make you a witness of the mysteries of God and the secrets of nature." Mercurius = *divinus ternarius*, revealer of divine secrets; fructifier of philosophical tree; Theutius = Thoth; messenger-hermeneut. Related to Hermes Kyllenios (ithyphallic Arcadian Hermes). In Rosencreutz's *Chymical Wedding*, appears as Cupid, wounds adept with *telum passionis* for curiosity about Venus. "Nowhere to be found on earth" → daemon. Nabu (Mesopotamian Mercurius) and Istar (Astarte): mother-son incest motif; Nabu = "Messiah of the Lie," kept in prison by sun. Found "in the dung-heaps."

**Nigredo and the uroboros** (par. 279): Dark Mercurius = initial *nigredo* state. Lowest = symbol of highest (*Anfang und Ende / Reichen sich die Hände* — "Beginning and end reach each other's hands"). He is the **uroboros**, One and All, the union of opposites accomplished during the alchemical process.

**Penotus and the Tabula smaragdina** (par. 280): *Tabula smaragdina*: "He ascends from earth to heaven and descends again to earth." Power is complete when he **returns to earth** — circular movement, contrasting with the Christian Redeemer (from above downward, returning above). The *filius macrocosmi* starts from below, ascends high with the powers of Above and Below united in himself, then **returns to earth** — carrying the circular, uroboric nature. Mercurius = *circulus simplex*; "I am One and at the same time Many in myself." Centre in man = earth = "salt" (Christ: "Ye are the salt of the earth"). This reverse direction defines Mercurius's fundamental difference from Christ.

**Hermes and the Church** (par. 281): Hermes = god of thieves and cheats, but also god of revelation. Patrizi proposed to Pope Gregory XIV that Hermetic philosophy replace Aristotle in ecclesiastical doctrine — two worlds must yet be united. "A psychological differentiation of religious as well as scientific views is still needed before a union can begin to be brought about."

### §10: Summary — Mercurius, Christ, and the Two Lights (pars. 282-303)

**Six-point summary of Mercurius** (par. 284): (1) consists of all conceivable opposites, named a unity despite innumerable inner contradictions; (2) both material and spiritual; (3) the transformation-process between lower/material and higher/spiritual; (4) devil, redeeming psychopomp, evasive trickster, God's reflection in physical nature; (5) reflects the *artifex*'s mystical experience, coinciding with the opus itself; (6) **represents on the one hand the self and on the other the individuation process**, and via his limitless names, the collective unconscious.

**Psychic reality vs. matter** (pars. 285-289): Alchemy's deeper concern was the experience of the unconscious, not gold-making — misunderstood for centuries for want of any concept of the collective unconscious. Mercurius bottled = the ego/*principium individuationis* (Upanishadic personal atman); freed = the supra-personal atman, the *hiranyagarbha*, the *filius macrocosmi*. **KEY** (par. 289): the lapis is "at most a counterpart or analogy of Christ" — not Christ renamed. Mercurius-lapis represents **the part of the psyche never moulded by Christianity**, forced into dark Hermetic symbols.

**Christ/Antichrist split** (pars. 290-294): The "superabundance of light" of Christian dogma produced "an all the blacker darkness" on the other side (Satan: celestial family member in Job → chained dragon in Revelation) — a necessary, expedient differentiation of consciousness, but every developmental optimum exceeded produces *enantiodromia*. Freud's uncovering of "the abysmal darkness of human nature" reopened what religious optimism had shut.

**Mercurius as Christ's compensatory counterpart** (pars. 295-299): **KEY** (par. 295): Mercurius "stands in a compensatory relation to Christ" — not a replacement, but a bridge across the psychological abyss the Christ-dogma's purity created. **KEY** (par. 299), reversing rationalist causality: "instead of deriving these figures from our psychic conditions, [we] must derive our psychic conditions from these figures." **Christ = archetype of consciousness; Mercurius = archetype of the unconscious.**

**Cognitio matutina / cognitio vespertina** (pars. 299-303): Augustine's evening-knowledge (of creatures) vs. morning-knowledge (of the Creator), read psychologically: consciousness darkens like nightfall, but "evening gives birth to morning" — the *stella matutina*, Lucifer, light-bringer. **KEY** (par. 301): "When all visible lights are extinguished one finds... the light of the self" (Yajñavalkya); Augustine's first day of creation = *cognitio sui ipsius* = knowledge not of the ego but **of the Self**. Closing image (par. 303): Mercurius as *lumen naturae* redeems only those whose reason strives toward the highest light; for the unmindful, "the lumen naturae turns into a perilous *ignis fatuus*, and the psychopomp into a diabolical seducer" — Lucifer becomes "the father of lies" whose voice, amplified by press and radio, leads millions to ruin.

## V. The Philosophical Tree (pars. 304-482)

*Originally written for a Festschrift for botanist Gustav Senn (unpublished due to Senn's death); published as "Der philosophische Baum" (1945), revised and expanded 1954. Epigraph, Faust: "All theory, my friend, is grey, / But green life's golden tree."* Full page: [[Philosophical-Tree]].

### Part I: Individual Representations of the Tree Symbol (pars. 304-349)

32 spontaneous patient tree-pictures — 19 made before Jung knew any alchemy, the rest before *Psychology and Alchemy*'s publication, offered as evidence the parallels are archetypal, not learned. **Par. 304 KEY**: "If a mandala may be described as a symbol of the self seen in cross section, then the tree would represent a profile view of it: **the self depicted as a process of growth**."

Recurring motifs: world-tree/world-axis on an island or globe (Figs. 1-2); fire-and-water tree, growing simultaneously up and down (Figs. 5-6); tree breaking through the earth's crust by irresistible force (Fig. 7); dragon/crocodile-guarded tree = Mercurius *duplex* (Figs. 9-10); tree/snake pair = passive-corporeal vs. active-animal aspects of Mercurius (Figs. 11-13); sapphire/treasure hidden in the roots, uroboros as hieroglyph of eternity, lapis as "forever dying yet eternal" (Figs. 16-17); regressive/inturned tree = justified fear of individuation's demands (Figs. 18-20); tree-nymph/independent Eve emerging (Figs. 21-23); quaternity of light and four rivers = intuitive apprehension of the self (Fig. 24, with a testy aside about critics accusing Jung of a "special liking for the number four").

**KEY — danger of inflation** (Fig. 25, pars. 332-333): identification with the Self is a real temptation, not a goal — recognizing the impulse and not acting on it is "primarily a moral" task, not merely intellectual; failure produces repression/dissociation rather than development. **Par. 333 formulation**: "the tree has no leaves, and its branches could just as well be roots... A person whose roots are above as well as below is thus like a tree growing simultaneously downwards and upwards. **The goal is neither height nor depth, but the centre.**"

Theosophy-influenced patient's inverted-cross/svādhiṣṭhāna-chakra mandala (Fig. 26) draws Jung's critique: "One does not become enlightened by imagining figures of light, but by **making the darkness conscious**." Animus figures (Figs. 29-30) cite the Bhagavad Gītā ("I am the game of dice" = Krishna as self) — caution that the animus/anima, like any archetype, has "a Janus face" and cannot represent totality. Closes with Noël Pierre's poem "Soleil Noir" (1952) as an independent modern confirmation of the same archetypal cluster (universal midpoint, spiral rotation, tree of life/death, dwarf of natural wisdom, island, stone cube).

### Part II: History and Interpretation of the Tree Symbol (pars. 350-482)

**§1 Tree as archetypal image** (par. 350): growth, life, development both directions, maternal aspect, old age, personality, death and rebirth.

**§2 Jodocus Greverus's treatise** (pars. 354-357): the opus as sowing/tending a tree in a walled garden; soil = purified Mercurius; four planets = trunk; sun/moon = seed; Greverus's own trinity comparison defended by Jung as genuine parallel via shared archetype, not mystification.

**§3 The Tetrasomia** (pars. 358-368): quaternio reduced to unity; four sons of Horus; Ezekiel's four cherubim/wheels; Daniel's four beasts (fourth beast's monstrous devouring = daemonization of the human quarter under power-lust, "recurred countless times in the history of mankind"); the cross as quaternity = **the self, man's wholeness**. See [[Fourfold-Quaternio]].

**§4 The image of wholeness** (pars. 369-373): aqua permanens = Zosimos's "the whole" (τὸ πᾶν); microcosm=macrocosm → apocatastasis; "every grain becomes wheat" (Eckhart); moral equivalent of transmutation = self-knowledge. See [[Aqua-Permanens]].

**§5 Nature and origin of the philosophical tree** (pars. 374-379): Dorn's metallic tree — veins as a planetary circulatory system, death and renewal; Dorn vs. Aristotle/empiricists = the realism/nominalism dispute; the Hermetic philosopher keeps archetypal premises as inalienable, where the modern empiricist represses them in the name of objectivity — vindicated, per Jung, by modern physics' discovery that the observer cannot be eliminated.

**§6 Dorn's interpretation of the tree** (pars. 380-382): *vegetabilia materiae* (literal organisms) vs. *vegetabilia naturae* (archetypal/psychic "organisms" — bleeding sponge, shrieking mandrake); the "Sophists"/frivolous triflers who take symbolic names concretely.

**§7 The rose-coloured blood and the rose** (pars. 383-391): Khunrath's lion; filius macrocosmi (lapis) = filius microcosmi (Christ) parallel — serious primordial religious experience, not bad taste; Mechthild of Magdeburg's rose-heart vision; rose = mandala = Mary = feminine Eros; Rosicrucian *per crucem ad rosam*. **KEY** (par. 391): "Love alone is useless if it does not also have understanding... a compensatory Logos has been joined to it as a light that shines in the darkness."

**§8 The alchemical mind** (pars. 392-397): Petrus Bonus's blindness — believing the ancients had *prophesied* Christ rather than that alchemy was unconsciously projecting Christian material onto matter; anti-reductionist critique of Freud (incest-reduction as over-generalization from one genuine archetypal discovery). **KEY**: "symbols mean very much more than can be known at first glance."

**§9 Various aspects of the tree** (pars. 398-403): the climbing/Melusina-Lilith motif (Ripley Scrowle); Seth's vision of the dead-then-renewed paradise tree with the Christ-child in its crown; the truncated tree/tree of death (Egyptian tale of Bata); the fruit-bearing tree and its four colors; tree-fruit as "bread of life."

**§§10-17: Uroboros, bird/snake, feminine tree-numen, the lapis, dangers of the art** (pars. 404-448): inverted tree in shamanism; bird (spirit) vs. snake (instinct); tree-numen = Self (women's pictures) or anima (men's); torture/dismemberment as the price of transformation.

**§18 Suffering and the coniunctio** (pars. 450-457): Dorn's "golden hook" → Manichaean Primordial-Man-as-bait → Gnostic Sophia-Achamoth myth (Irenaeus): Christ gives Sophia form but not intelligence, then withdraws, leaving her to feel her own suffering. Read as anima-loss in a man who identifies wholly with reason/spirituality — masculine consciousness perceives psychic suffering without becoming conscious of its cause. Neither light nor darkness will yield first; hence discrimination cannot be reunified without extinguishing consciousness, and the **unconscious**, not the rational intellect, produces the symbols of wholeness. The coniunctio = "a culminating point of life and at the same time a death."

**§19 The tree as man** (pars. 458-462): old Rabbinic tradition that the tree of paradise was a man; **tree-numen = Self for women, anima for men** (unless the man's own femininity is sufficiently differentiated/integrated); the inverted tree of East Siberian shamanism (Nakassä) with a carved human face at the roots — the shaman's greater personality, climbed in ecstatic trance; Eliade quoted confirming shamanism = individuation projected, structurally identical to alchemy.

**§20 Interpretation and integration of the unconscious — the eagle dream** (pars. 463-481): Jung's fullest worked example of refusing to reduce a dream symbol to a known person. Y dreams of an eagle circling a concentration camp, shoots it down with a rocket plane; the dream follows a day after reading *Answer to Job*. X assumes the eagle is himself; Y thinks it's Jung. **KEY**: "when our dream says 'eagle' it means an eagle" — rejects Freud's censor/disguise model; the task is to investigate what the eagle means *as an eagle* (cross-culturally), then connect that to the dreamer's actual situation. **KEY — devaluation, not disguise** (par. 479): a patient's dream of her analyst as a hairdresser is not concealing his identity but **devaluing** his authority — he should have no more significance than someone who "fixes the head" so she can use it herself. General method: existing living traditions (e.g. Niklaus von der Flüe's vision reshaped via a devotional text into the Trinity) guide integration where available; amplification, not reduction, lets a symbol do its integrative work. See [[Dream-Analysis-and-Interpretation]].

**Closing paragraph** (par. 482): mere intellectual understanding is not sufficient — "no understanding by means of words and no imitation can replace actual experience." Alchemy lost its vital substance when it split into *oratorium* (nebulous mysticism) and *laboratorium* (chemistry) — "no one asks about the fate of the psyche, which thereafter vanished from sight for several hundred years."
