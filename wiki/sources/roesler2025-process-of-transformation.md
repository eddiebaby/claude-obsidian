# The Process of Transformation — The Core of Analytical Psychology

[[Christian-Roesler|Christian Roesler]]'s framing paper for the 2024 Freiburg conference "The Process of Transformation" (JAP 2025, 70:3, 355–373), condensing his book *Deconstructing Archetype Theory* (2023). Its central move: **archetype theory is four theories wearing one name**, and only one of the four survives contemporary evidence — the one that matters most for clinical practice.

## The Four Strands of Archetype Theory

Full treatment: [[Deconstructing Archetype Theory]]. In brief:

1. **Biological** — archetypes as genetically transmitted, brain-rooted preformed structures/contents ("autochthonous," appearing without cultural influence). Embedded in natural science, genetics, ethology.
2. **Anthropological** — human universals; cross-cultural primary forms (the Great Mother, the hero myth); homology of phylogeny and ontogeny; a maturity scale from archaic to civilized.
3. **Transcendental (unity reality)** — bridging mind and matter via quantum physics; the [[Wolfgang Pauli|Pauli]]–Jung dialogue; [[Synchronicity|synchronicity]] and [[Unus Mundus|unus mundus]].
4. **Process theory of transformation** — a force in the psyche driving integration toward wholeness ([[Self (archetype)|the Self]]), preformatted in the unconscious, whose universal form could map psychotherapy.

**Roesler's verdict:** strands 1 and 2 must be **rejected** — refuted by contemporary genetics, anthropology, archaeology, and religious studies (the "Great Mother" and hero-myth universality "has practically collapsed"; what is genuinely innate are *capacities directed at relationship and sociality*, not preformed images or contents). Strand 3 proved "very fruitful" for transpersonal psychology and consciousness studies. Strand 4 is "the core of AP" — but only if stripped of positivistic biological claims and treated as a **clinical hermeneutics**.

## Four Models Within the Process Idea Itself

Even the surviving process strand contains four distinguishable models of the transformation/individuation process:
1. **Centring** — the Self as autonomous centring factor; mandala imagery (CW 9i ¶282).
2. **Sequence of stages** — shadow → anima/animus → wise old man / great mother → Self ([[Individuation]]); higher-complexity, more nomothetic claims.
3. **Consciousness from an unconscious matrix** — the hero emancipating from the Great Mother (Neumann, Campbell). Roesler flags two problems: brain development does not start from an "unconscious matrix," and the Great-Mother-as-source is a selective, Eurocentric reading.
4. **Alchemy** — nigredo, separatio, mortificatio/putrefactio, dissolutio as stages of psychic transformation.

## Clinical Hermeneutics, Not "Vulgar Jungianism"

Roesler's constructive proposal: keep the idea of an archetypal transformation process, but demote it from biological fact to **"clinically applied hermeneutics"** — "a flexible (pluralistic), comparative, and interdisciplinary exegesis that seeks out interpretive possibilities, not conclusions" (Barnaby & D'Acierno). This is opposed to **"vulgar Jungianism": the mechanical and reductivist allegorical rewriting of a text according to the master code of the archetypes.** Meaning is not fixed in the symbol; it is *produced* in an interactive relationship between at least two minds — which is exactly why psychotherapeutic change needs two persons, and why the therapeutic relationship is not "just a projection screen."

## The Universality Problem and Jung's Method

Roesler is pointed about Jung's own evidence base: Jung "did not occupy himself with the material of the patient but rather held lectures, presenting his theory," and was "willing to force [his concepts] onto his patients." Case reports are "compromise formations" (Westen & Morrison 2001) — low replicability, low reliability of inference. Even classic Jungian cases "could be well explained without any reference to archetypes" (Merchant 2019). Hence the call for a new, sceptical, adequately-methodologized research programme — of which SDA is the exemplar.

## Structural Dream Analysis as the Worked Example

The paper closes by presenting [[Structural Dream Analysis]] as proof that the transformation process *can* be investigated empirically. This is the same 6-pattern dream-ego-agency typology detailed in the companion 2026 paper ([[roesler2026-jung-dream-theory-empirical-research]]) already in the vault. One finding is highlighted as genuine archetypal evidence: across many successful therapies, a **child/baby-in-danger motif** appears at the exact turning point where the dream series leaps from low (threatened) to high (agentic) patterns — matching Jung's divine-child archetype (CW 9i), completely unconscious to the dreamer. Notably, **no fixed symbol meanings** were found (a snake threatened one dreamer, helped another) — only the *patterns* of dream-ego response were cross-culturally stable.
