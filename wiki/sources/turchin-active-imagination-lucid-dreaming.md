# Active Imagination as an Alternative to Lucid Dreaming: Theory and Experimental Results

**Author**: [[Alexey Turchin]] (Science for Life Extension Foundation / Digital Immortality Now)
**Type**: practitioner research paper reporting an open-label pilot experiment (2004–2005, Moscow, ~100 participants), with a practical "how-to" method section and a literature-adjacent discussion.

## Core Thesis

Lucid dreaming (LD) has four persistent complications: attaining lucidity, retaining it once achieved, concentrating on the intended task inside it, and remembering the result afterward — plus a built-in tendency toward threat/nightmare content (LD is plausibly an evolutionary adaptation for rehearsing danger, Revonsuo 2000). Turchin's hypothesis: Jungian **[[Active Imagination]]** (AIM) can substitute for LD as a route into altered, unconscious-sourced imaginal states — reliably available (even by text chat, on a bus, in a crowd), without needing sleep-stage timing or acetylcholinesterase-inhibitor supplementation (galantamine, huperzine — see [[Lucid Dreaming]]).

## Method Summarized (the Sitter–Viewer Protocol)

A **sitter** (facilitator) guides a **viewer** (subject) through a scripted-but-adaptive algorithm:

1. **Consent and setup** — ≥30 min, phones off; no special "calm" atmosphere is required (AIM can be run walking, on a bus). The sitter is a partner, not an authority — a departure point that turns out to matter for the [[Active Imagination#Contradiction — Classical Jungian AIM vs. Turchin's LD-Substitute Protocol|contradiction]] below.
2. **Starting image** — near-universally "Imagine you are on the beach — what do you see?" The sea = the unconscious; the beach = the meeting point of conscious and unconscious, an explicit symbolic scaffold set by the sitter, not discovered by the viewer.
3. **3D presence-building** — a fixed circuit of open (non-leading) questions across visual, auditory, and kinesthetic modalities (what's on the left/right, what sounds, what you're wearing, what you feel) to deepen immersion before anything "happens."
4. **Invitation of a helper** — an "internal helper" approaches from the left; the sitter asks what it looks like, its mood, whether it has an urgent message, and its name. Troubleshooting for six common failure modes is given in detail: the helper doesn't appear, is unsuitable (rude/silent), can't be communicated with, is obviously just echoing the viewer's own wishes, is treated by the viewer as absolutely real, or triggers fear of "demons."
5. **Task/goal formulation** — once presence is established, the viewer (or sitter, with the viewer's list of interests) formulates an explicit task: visit a past life, travel to the future, meet a spiritual teacher, visit a Reiki energy source, watch a TV set and "change channels," build a tulpa and her island, do parapsychological remote viewing, reconstruct a half-remembered dream, or (Turchin's own extension) **collect data for digital immortality** — recording the séance as raw material for a future AI-driven resurrection of the person (Turchin, 2018).
6. **Ending** — gradual return, a 1–10 depth-of-immersion self-rating, and a caution not to re-enter ordinary activity immediately (residual altered state can last ~30 min).

## Two Types of Imaginal Capacity: Mental Screen vs. Visual Screen

The paper's most transferable theoretical contribution — see [[Mental Screen]] for the full model. In brief: some people (skewing young/female in Turchin's informal sample) get bright, hallucination-adjacent imagery on a **visual screen** that would overlay real vision if eyes were open; others (skewing older/male, "rational" personality) get a low-resolution, non-overlapping **mental screen** — like the mental images one has while reading a book. Neither can be trained into the other. This distinction is not [[Aphantasia]] (true absence of voluntary visual imagery) — mental-screen-only people still have rich, emotionally intense content, just not bright/visual.

## Results (n≈100, 2004–2005, open-label, no control group)

- **~Half of participants** had mental-screen-only or no images at all; the type is not trainable.
- For **visual-screen** people, AIM reproduced LD-equivalent content *and* intensity — a genuine substitute.
- For **mental-screen** people, AIM still produced "interesting and unexpected results, and profound emotional reactions," but participants chasing bright visuals were not satisfied — a partial substitute at best.
- AIM **outperforms LD** on reliability, availability (any circumstance, including a chat window or a crowd), sustained concentration on the intended task, and ease of recording the result.
- **Self-administered AIM is ~10x weaker** than sitter-guided — the mind cannot fully play both roles.
- **Text chat is as effective as face-to-face** sessions, and often happens more readily at night.

## Verdict

"AIM partly works as a replacement for LD, as it works great only for half of people, and it requires a sitter. However, AIM outperforms LD in reliability and availability in any circumstance." The paper explicitly frames AIM as an instrument for **self-exploration and pleasure-seeking**, not psychotherapy — usable by "any positive and reasonable person," excluding only those with psychosis, PTSD, or suicidal depression.

## Adjacent Techniques Cataloged

The paper surveys a wide family of related induction/altered-state methods without developing any to the depth of AIM itself: Stone & Stone's **Voice Dialogue** (the "controller-protector" subpersonality, functionally equivalent to AIM's "guide"), spontaneous dance, controlled hypnagogia (12 induction techniques — see [[Lucid Dreaming]]), spontaneous drawing, "instant"/"absolute" active imagination (a few seconds of eyes-closed free imagery as a diagnostic of imaginal style), group AIM, role-playing games as collective AIM, and repetition of affirmations as a seeding technique. None of these receive dedicated concept pages here — they are listed on [[Active Imagination]] as a compact catalog, proportional to the depth the source itself gives them.

## Methodological Caveats

This is an **open-label pilot with no control group**, self-selected participants (internet volunteers, festival attendees, even train/bus passengers), and several claims — the gender/personality correlation with visual-vs-mental screen type in particular — are anecdotal, not statistically validated. Treat the paper as a practitioner field report and a source of a useful *typology* (mental screen / visual screen), not as clinical evidence.
