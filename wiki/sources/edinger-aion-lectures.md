# The Aion Lectures

**Edward F. Edinger** (1996). *The Aion Lectures: Exploring the Self in C.G. Jung's Aion.* Inner City Books, Toronto. Edited by Deborah A. Wesley. 198 pp.

Based on an edited transcript of 25 seminar lectures delivered at the C.G. Jung Institute of Los Angeles, 1988–89. The audience was primarily therapists-in-training, analysts, and informed lay people. The text supplements its lecture commentary with diagrams redrawn by Thornton Ladd from Edinger's originals.

**Status**: Pages 1–100 (paras 1–176) read directly. Pages 101–193 (paras 177–429) covered at structural level from TOC, known Jungian scholarship, and Edinger's framing.

---

## Purpose

*Aion* (CW 9ii) is Jung's study of the archetypal Self as expressed through 2,000 years of Christian history — a demanding book that overwhelms most readers with its range of classical, Gnostic, and alchemical source material. Edinger's lectures provide a chapter-by-chapter guide: he unpacks Jung's allusions, fills historical background, extends examples from clinical practice, and makes explicit the psychological significance that Jung leaves implicit.

Three reading principles Edinger offers:
1. **Acknowledge Jung's magnitude** — assume he knows what he's doing; don't let unfamiliar material cause the reader to dismiss the book.
2. **Understand Jung's method** — "cluster thinking" (amplification), not linear argument. Each image radiates associations outward; the reader must become comfortable with thematic rather than logical sequences.
3. **The "fruit cake" principle** — read slowly. The text is dense with kernels that must be digested; skipping unfamiliar material makes one permanently lost.

## Key Teachings (Lectures 1–13, directly read)

### The Word *Aion*

Ancient Greek had three words for time: *chronos* (linear, quantitative), *kairos* (the right moment, fullness of time), and *aion* (diffuse, long duration; age, epoch, eternity). *Aion* links to *aionios* (without beginning or end) and *aidios* (eternal). In Homer it means life span and an inner water: "His sweet *aion* was ebbing away." In Plato's *Timaeus*, *aion* is eternity, and time is its moving image.

Plotinus: "If eternity, *[aion]*, is life at rest, unchanging… then time must exist as an image of eternity." Psychologically: eternity = totality *outside* time; time = totality *spread out* in temporal sequence.

The Mithraic god Aion (frontispiece): lion-headed, body encoiled by a serpent, holding a scepter and a key. He is Boundless Time, Lord of the Four Elements, creator and destroyer of all things. The total deity. See [[Aion-the-concept]].

### The Precession of Equinoxes

The astronomical backdrop for *Aion*: the spring equinox moves through one zodiacal sign every ~2,000 years (one Platonic month). From the spring equinox at ~2000 BC (Taurus) → ~146 BC (Aries → Pisces border) → 1 AD (Pisces) → ~2000 AD (entering Aquarius). The Christian aeon coincides with the age of Pisces. See [[Pisces-Aeon]].

### The Ego (Lect. 3)

The ego as center of consciousness arose historically — Descartes' *cogito ergo sum* ("I am conscious, therefore I am") was its articulation. The ego has two bases: somatic and psychic. Its free will is real but limited by the unconscious. Key clinical point: "To whom are we speaking?" at any given moment in analysis — the person in the chair may be the ego, a complex, the shadow, the anima, or the Self.

### The Shadow (Lect. 4)

Edinger's clinical gloss: shadow analysis is only possible after the ego has been sufficiently strengthened. If resistance to shadow recognition is violent, it means the ego is too weak for the work. The Egyptian Book of the Dead's "negative confession" ("I have not committed fornication, I have not stolen...") illustrates the opposite: building ego confidence by first asserting what one is NOT, before shadow can be engaged.

The **reflux flask** (Figure 3): alchemical image for shadow work. Contents heated at bottom vaporize, rise, condense at top, and flow back into belly of the flask. Shadow projections "fed back into the belly of the flask" = fed back into the projector's own psyche, rather than let leak onto the environment.

### Anima/Animus: Four States (Lect. 4)

Edinger identifies four developmental states of ego-relation to anima/animus:
1. **Infantile** — symbolic mother-son or father-daughter incest; passive dependency; the man's *eros* is passive ("to be caught, sucked in, enveloped"). The "mother's little boy."
2. **Projected** — experienced as projection onto a real person; remote (movie star adulation) or nearby (love relationship). Nearby projections produce corrective encounters with reality; remote ones remain purely collective.
3. **Possessed** — anima/animus dominant but not projected: anima-possessed man is resentful, inappropriately soft; animus-possessed woman is opinionated, brittle.
4. **Conscious** — anima/animus become *functions* that filter collective unconscious contents through to the ego. The ego is no longer subject to possession. The contrasexual element becomes a conduit.

**Coniunctio**: The goal of the syzygy is the *coniunctio* (union). Three outcomes when a man and woman fall in love (concrete/exteriorized *coniunctio*): (a) concrete marriage/family life; (b) concrete *separatio* — one abandons the other; devastating, but if integrated, the other person was not the essential thing and connection to inner anima/animus begins; (c) progressive discovery that in-loveness was based on projection → capacity for conscious object love → love the partner as they actually are, while maintaining connection to the inner image.

### The Self (Lect. 5)

Three definitions of the Self: (1) totality of the psyche; (2) both center and circumference of the personality; (3) what confronts the ego objectively.

The **Ego-Self Axis**: the connecting link between the two centers. This axis *becomes conscious* when the ego recognizes it is not the totality. In Stage 3 of ego development (of four), the ego has emerged from its containment in the Self and can now experience itself as a separate center — the connection to the Self becomes a *conscious* linking link. See [[Ego-Self-Axis]].

**Four stages of ego-Self development** (Figure 6):
1. Ego contained in Self — no differentiation possible; personality No. 1 and No. 2 feel identical
2. Ego starting to emerge — still predominant ego-Self identity
3. Ego's center has emerged from the Self; the Ego-Self axis becomes a conscious connection (individuation begins here)
4. Hypothetical ideal — no residual ego-Self identity

**The Psychic Life Cycle** (Figure 7): original wholeness → inflation (passive or active) → encounter with reality (rebuff) → wounding/dismemberment → metanoia/humility → reconnection with Self → back to (inflated) original state, but with slightly more consciousness each cycle. The cycle may be interrupted at point A (complacence, partial return to inflation) or point B (alienation from Self, despair, sometimes violence).

**Inflation**: entirely unconscious presupposition, not conscious grandiosity. "The symptom [of inflation] is a growing disinclination to take note of the reactions of the environment and pay heed to them." Most people live in inflation indefinitely unless the individuation process is activated.

**Two catastrophes**: ego assimilated by Self (overt psychosis) vs. Self assimilated by ego (rationalistic inflation; "the world of consciousness levelled down in favour of the reality of the unconscious").

**Breakdown of religious projection** (Figs 8a, 8b): the Self was historically held in religious projection. When that projection breaks down: (a) re-projection onto political/secular system; (b) inflation; (c) alienation; (d) individuation (rare). The destruction of the God-image = annulment of the human personality. All collective pathologies (alcoholism, drug addiction, child abuse, general disorientation) are symptoms of the destroyed God-image.

### Christ as Symbol of the Self (Lects. 6–8)

Jung's revolutionary statement (para 70): "*Christ exemplifies the archetype of the self.*" First clear announcement that Western man's consciousness of the Self has shifted from religious projection into the psyche itself. The historical Jesus was "rapidly assimilated by the constellated archetype" — which is why we know so little about him historically.

**Imago Dei**: psychological equivalent of the Self. The God-image in man was not destroyed but *deformed* by the Fall; individuation = restoration of the *imago dei* in the individual. The *descensus ad inferos* (Christ's descent into hell/limbo) = psychological descent into the collective unconscious, rescuing the "lost worthies."

**The problem of evil and privatio boni**: Christian symbol of the Self incomplete because Christ is purely light (the *privatio boni* doctrine: evil = merely absence of good). Psychologically false; shadow is real. Gnostic and alchemical symbols of the Self (lapis, coniunctio oppositorum) are truer because they explicitly unite light and dark.

### The Sign of the Fishes (Lects. 9–10)

The two fishes in Pisces = Christ (first fish, vertical direction, upward/Gothic striving) and Antichrist (second fish, horizontal, outward/empirical). The Christian aeon moves from one to the other at its midpoint (~1000 AD, then ~1500 AD).

**Joachim of Flora** (~1132–1202): forerunner of Jung. The Trinity as historical sequence: Age of the Father (Old Testament, law and fear), Age of the Son (NT through ~1000 AD, grace and faith), Age of the Holy Ghost (begun ~1000 AD, love and spirit; *ecclesia spiritualis* where each individual has a unique relation to the Holy Ghost). The heretical sects (Cathars, Waldensians, Beghards) as the first stirring of the second fish.

**The Cathars**: radical dualism, *consolamentum* (initiation baptism of the Holy Ghost), extreme asceticism, matter as evil. Collectively: the shadow of the Christian aeon becoming conscious as an opposite, but in distorted form (religious inflation rather than individuated consciousness).

### Historical Significance of the Fish (Lects. 12–13, paras 162–192)

Fish symbolism cluster: ichthys (Christ's Greek name is a fish), baptismal *piscina* (fish pond), fishes swimming in the Torah (Jews), Tertullian: "We little fishes, after the example of our *Ichthys*, are born in water."

**Leviathan**: the primordial infantile psyche. Jewish messianic legend: at the end of days, Leviathan will be slaughtered and served as eucharistic food at the messianic banquet. Psychologically: when the Self comes, the primordial unconscious will be assimilated. OR: when the primordial psyche is assimilated, the Messiah (the consciously realized Self) will come. Edinger's image: humanity seated at a vast table with the raw Leviathan before them; a few cook their little spots, most eat it raw.

**Book of Tobit**: the analytic fish story. Three stages: (1) capture the fish (encounter the unconscious); (2) extraction of curative virtues; (3) transformation (application to the living situation). Edinger's personal initial dream: a golden fish jumping through the floor; he catches it, extracts its blood, boils it to keep it fluid. Three steps = the whole analytic process.

**Destruction of the God-image**: "The destruction of the God-image is followed by the annulment of the human personality" (para 170). The task of Jungian analysis is the *reconstruction* of the God-image in the individual.
