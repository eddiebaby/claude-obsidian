# The Hour that Never Comes and the Time that Remains

Essay in the *Journal of Analytical Psychology* (Wiley) by Jungian analyst [[Daniel Françoli Yago]]. Proposes a symbolic/clinical investigation of psychic temporality through two figures — [[The Hour That Never Comes]] and [[The Time That Remains]] — and argues analytic listening's task is not to cure time but to sustain it: **[[Orphic Listening|Orphic listening]]**.

## The Argument in Brief

Chronological time (Chronos) and the "meaningful irruption" of opportune moments (Kairos, per Yvette Weiner) clash in the consulting room. Two forms of suffering fit neither cleanly:

1. **The hour that never comes** — the time of suspended desire, the event that never occurred, the trauma that froze the flow of experience. Structured not by what happened but by what didn't.
2. **The time that remains** — the time after collapse, what still vibrates after the fall: echo, remainder, fold, fidelity to what no longer returns.

Both draw on Jung's own account in *Memories, Dreams, Reflections* of visionary time as "outside of time... a sense of absolute freedom" (p. 309), read alongside Bergson's duration-vs-clock-time distinction (Jung absorbed Bergson's intuition concept directly into *Psychological Types*) and Walter Benjamin's non-continuous historical time — "the past can be seized only as an image which flashes up at the instant it can be recognized."

## The Hour That Never Comes

Full treatment: [[The Hour That Never Comes]]. Core claim: "it is not always what happened that traumatizes. Sometimes, it is what never happened." Two distinguishable faces — the soul's fidelity-driven postponement (myth: Demeter suspending the seasons) versus anxious hyperaccelerated paralysis (myth: Sisyphus) — that require different clinical responses (listening vs. deceleration) but often intertwine. Dream imagery is thresholds and interrupted crossings: doors that won't open, stairways to nowhere, elevators that never arrive.

## The Time That Remains

Full treatment: [[The Time That Remains]]. Core claim: after real collapse (a death, the end of a defining project), the future is not merely lost — it is, per Mark Fisher, *cancelled*: tomorrow becomes only a remixed version of today. Peter Pál Pelbart's "labyrinth of time" and Byung-Chul Han's "hyperactive stagnation" name the same wound from two more angles. Yago frames this **not only as trauma-consequence but potentially as a specific condition of individuation** — when linear continuity breaks, the ego may yield to the psyche's own irregular, spiral rhythm (drawing on Leda Martins's Afro-diasporic "spiral time") rather than simply falling into emptiness.

## Clinical Vignettes

- **C.** — recurring dreams of narrow spiral staircases toward doors that won't open, or open onto more stairs; an underground library holding a book with her own name. "It's like I live in the path, but the ground still feels more threatening than the demons that live there."
- **A.** — stopped working after his father's death; no acute distress, precise but ungrounded speech. "I still wake up, but the days no longer come with me." The imaginal figure Yago names for this state: not the hero, the orphan, or the pilgrim, but **the wanderer among ruins** — kin to Benjamin's Angelus Novus.
- **T.** — lost her daughter, then her job of 27 years. Stopped dreaming, then one recurring dream returned: walking a dirt road littered with pieces of demolished houses, calm, picking things up. "There's nowhere left to get to, but I'll keep walking." Cited by Yago as spiral time's individuation-inflection: not resignation, a different relationship to time itself.

## Orphic Listening

Full treatment: [[Orphic Listening]]. The essay's proposed clinical stance, drawn from Orpheus descending into Hades "not to seize Eurydice by force, but to sing her absence" — active waiting in the dark, not rescue. The analyst becomes "one who listens at the threshold, who stays beside what falls to unfold." Georges Didi-Huberman's fireflies (fragile, intermittent, real) are the essay's closing image for what such listening tracks.
