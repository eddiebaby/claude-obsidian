# The Inflated Ego
*Ego and Archetype, Part I, pp. 3-36*

## Central Theme
Edinger opens by defining the two psychic centers — ego and Self — and introducing the ego-Self axis as the vital connecting link between them. The chapter traces the developmental logic of inflation: the ego begins life fully identified with the Self (original wholeness), and that state, paradisiacal from one angle, is also unconscious irresponsibility that must dissolve before individuation can begin. Two mythological paradigms — Adam and Eve expelled from Eden, Prometheus stealing fire — give this movement its narrative skeleton.

## Key Ideas
- The **Self** is the ordering center of the total psyche (conscious and unconscious); the empirical equivalent of the *imago Dei*. The **ego** is the center of the conscious personality only.
- The **ego-Self axis** is the vital connecting link that maintains the integrity of the total personality. When intact, the ego draws on archetypal energy. When damaged, alienation and psychological illness result.
- Development is not a linear separation but a **cyclic (spiral) alternation** between ego-Self union and ego-Self separation, repeating throughout life from birth to death.
- The four developmental diagrams: Fig. 1 = original uroboric state (total ego-Self identity); Fig. 2 = ego emerging but still mostly inside Self; Fig. 3 = mature stage with partly-conscious ego-Self axis; Fig. 4 = theoretical limit (complete separation, never actualized).
- **Inflation** = the ego identifying with the Self, arrogating qualities of a larger whole. We are born inflated. The infant experiences itself as the center of the universe, as deity — simultaneously a source of numinous richness and radical irresponsibility.
- Inflation persists into adult life: *puer aeternus*, the "provisional life" (Baynes), spells of rage (Yahweh complex), power motivation, solipsism, the illusion of immortality.
- **Negative inflation** — identification with the divine victim — is equally inflationary: excessive guilt, melancholia, altruism that transcends proper human limits.
- The child's mandala drawings (Rhoda Kellog research) provide empirical evidence that the young psyche is organized as a circle-with-center before ego differentiation occurs.

## Psychological Significance
This chapter establishes the baseline for the entire book. Inflation is the first and most primitive form of the ego-Self relationship. All subsequent chapters map how original wholeness is lost (alienation, Ch. 2), broken through (encounter with Self, Ch. 3), and eventually reconstituted at a higher conscious level (individuation proper). Inflation must be understood before everything that follows can be understood.

## Key Images / Symbols
- **Uroborus** (Neumann): the tail-eating serpent as symbol of the original Self-mandala, before any ego exists; the primordial state of totality.
- **Garden of Eden / Adam and Eve**: Paradise as mandala (four rivers, tree at center) = original ego-Self identity. The Fall = necessary separation from unconscious wholeness. Eve's creation from Adam's rib implies original hermaphroditic wholeness. Expulsion = the beginning of consciousness and suffering.
- **Prometheus**: steals fire (consciousness) from the gods — the same Luciferian act as eating the forbidden fruit. Being bound = fate of the ego that grasps divine power.
- **Plato's round man** (*Symposium*): original wholeness as sphere/mandala; the attack on the gods = inflation; Zeus splitting them = punishment and separation.
- **Hesiod's golden age**: paradise-state of living "like gods without sorrow" — the bliss of pre-differentiated inflation.
- **World Soul by Robert Fludd** (frontispiece): the anima as personification of the ego-Self axis, transmitting guidance from the archetypal psyche to the ego.

## Subsection Notes

**1. Ego and Self (p. 3):** Establishes the two centers and the ego-Self axis. Introduces four developmental diagrams. Key formula: development = ego-Self separation *plus* growing consciousness of the ego-Self axis, occurring simultaneously as a single spiral process.

**2. Inflation and Original Wholeness (p. 7):** Inflation defined; origin in the infant's identification with Self and universe. Mythological parallels (Hesiod, Plato). Children's mandala drawings as empirical correlate. Inflation is double-valued: genuine numinous contact AND irresponsibility.

**3. Adam and Prometheus (p. 16):** Eden interpreted: Garden = mandala = Self; Fall = inevitable and necessary. Jung: "the emancipation of ego consciousness was a Luciferian deed." Prometheus parallel: theft of divine fire earns punishment (Hybris to Nemesis). Both myths state that consciousness costs something sacred.

**4. Hybris and Nemesis (p. 26):** Greek tragic pattern — overstepping (hybris) calls forth its correction (nemesis). "The sun will not overstep his measures" (Heraclitus). The ego that arrogates divine qualities is struck down — not as external punishment but as the psyche's self-regulating function.
