# The Philosophers' Stone
*Ego and Archetype, Part III, pp. 260–end*

## Central Theme
The Philosophers' Stone — the *lapis philosophorum* — is the ultimate symbol of the Self in alchemical literature, and this final chapter is Edinger's sustained reading of Elias Ashmole's 1652 compilation of the Stone's qualities. Each of the twelve paragraphs of Ashmole's text is taken in turn and read as a phenomenological description of what the encounter with the Self is like and what it does. The Stone is simultaneously a rich, complex image *of* the Self and a map of individuation's goal. Edinger moves through the Stone's key attributes — transformation and revelation (the mineral stone), fertility and abundance (the vegetable stone), ubiquity and magical omniscience (the magical stone), and angelic, incorruptible subtlety (the angelical stone) — showing at each point how these "fantastical" qualities correspond exactly to verifiable psychological experiences in the depth-analytic process. The chapter culminates in "The One in the All" — the Stone's prophetic quality as connection to synchronicity and the transpersonal — and closes with "Spiritual Food and the Tree of Life," the Stone as the sustaining nourishment and regenerative ground of the individuated personality.

## Key Ideas
- **The Philosophers' Stone as symbol of the Self**: Jung's most fundamental contribution to our understanding of alchemy is this equation. The Stone is not gold, not a chemical compound, not a theological allegory — it is the projected image of the integrated psyche, the *homo totus* (whole man) that the alchemical process was unconsciously attempting to produce.
- **The fourfold Stone** (Mineral, Vegetable, Magical, Angelical): Ashmole's four categories correspond to the four functions of consciousness (thinking, feeling, sensation, intuition) but also, more fundamentally, to the fourfold structure of wholeness that recurs throughout alchemy. The four are united in the quintessence — the fifth substance that transcends the four elements by including them all.
- **Transformation and Revelation (Mineral Stone)**: The Stone converts base metals into gold and flints into gems. Psychologically: the encounter with the Self transforms the most banal, obstinate, and disagreeable aspects of one's life and character into something valuable. The flint — hard, dull, striking sparks — is the ego that, when worked upon by the Self, reveals the jewel within. The goldmaking intention, Ashmole says, is the *lowest* use of the Stone; the true adepts aimed at something far greater — wisdom, revelation, alignment with the divine order.
- **Jacob's Ladder**: Ashmole's text contains three biblical references unpacked by Edinger. Jacob's dream of the ladder with ascending and descending angels = the ego-Self axis of *sublimatio* (ascending from concrete to universal) and *coagulatio* (descending from archetypal to personal). The Stone enables the perception that the personal and transpersonal dimensions interpenetrate — "to see a world in a grain of sand" (Blake). Jacob's stone-pillow becomes the pillar marking the sacred site: the place where "upper" and "lower" unite is equivalent to the Philosophers' Stone.
- **The Fertility Principle (Vegetable Stone)**: The Stone knows "the nature of man, beasts, fowls, fishes, together with all kinds of trees, plants, flowers" and can make them grow at will. Psychologically: the Self is the *fons et origo* of all psychic life, the ground of biological and spiritual fertility. The saying of Jesus "I came that they may have life, and have it abundantly" (John 10:10) is the New Testament statement of this principle. The Christ-Osiris parallel: Osiris is the plant of life growing from himself; Christ is the grain of wheat that falls into the earth and dies to bear much fruit.
- **The Union of Opposites (Magical Stone)**: The Stone has both a hot, dry, solar (masculine) component and a cold, moist, lunar (feminine) component — each dangerous in isolation, mutually completing in union. This is the *coniunctio* of Sol and Luna that is the central image of alchemy. Psychologically: individuation requires the integration of both the fiery, aggressive, willing, solar principle and the receptive, containing, relating, lunar principle. The solar in excess produces psychic burns (*calcinatio*); the lunar in excess produces paralysis and petrification (*coagulatio*). The alchemical *coniunctio* is the psychological coming-to-terms with one's own contrasexual nature — the Animus or Anima integration that makes whole-person functioning possible.
- **The Medusa parallel**: The lunar principle in its negative, one-sided form is the Gorgon Medusa — to look at her directly turns one to stone (*coagulatio*). The solar rams of Psyche's task — fiery, maddened, destructive at noon — are the solar principle unmediated. The advice in each case is the same: approach indirectly, at the right moment, with the appropriate vessel or angle. The damaging encounters with partial aspects of the Stone are the alchemical operations (*calcinatio*, *coagulatio*) that form the psyche through repeated trials.
- **Ubiquity (Magical Stone continued)**: "By the magical or prospective Stone it is possible to discover any person in what part of the world soever, although never so secretly concealed." The Stone is omniscient. Psychologically: the Self can see everything — it is the integrated personality from which nothing is hidden. The seven eyes of God on the Stone (Zechariah) = the seven planetary spheres = the seven stages of transformation = total awareness. The Stone corresponds to "active imagination" as a practice: bringing an emerging unconscious content into a discernible image (*coagulatio*), thereby giving the infinite a particular form through which it can be perceived. Nothing can remain unconscious in the presence of the Self.
- **Spiritual Food and the Tree of Life (Angelical Stone)**: The Stone is "the food of angels," the "heavenly viaticum," the Tree of Life. As the food of angels it gives them the capacity to appear and converse with humans — psychologically: the eternal, archetypal realm becomes concrete and approachable via awareness of the Self. As viaticum (provisions for a journey) it is the Eucharist given to the dying — the Stone promotes a "death to the world," a withdrawal of projections that is the spiritual precondition of genuine life. The Tree of Life is explicitly equated with the cross of Christ and the alchemical Philosophical Tree — the axis mundi of the individuated psyche, simultaneously grounded in earth and reaching to heaven.
- **The One in the All**: The Stone's prophetic capacity connects it to synchronicity — the acausal meaningful coincidence that occurs when the archetypal level of the psyche is activated. The Stone "transmits knowledge of a transconscious reality beyond the categories of space and time." The alchemists' pre-knowledge of the resurrection, incarnation of Christ, and day of judgment corresponds to the psychological experience of timeless, universal meaning that accompanies genuine encounter with the Self.
- **The orphan quality**: The Stone's self-description ("I am an orphan, alone; nevertheless I am found everywhere") from the text Jung carved on the Bollingen Stone expresses the paradox of the individuated Self: supremely singular (orphan, without family, unique) yet universally present. This corresponds exactly to Chapter 6's analysis of the Monad and Monogenes — the fully individuated person is the most alone and the most universally connected.

## Psychological Significance
This concluding chapter synthesizes the entire book's arc. The Philosophers' Stone is the alchemical name for what the individuation process aims to produce: a human being whose conscious life is transparently related to the transpersonal Self — who can perceive meaning in the banal, who holds opposites in creative tension, who contributes to the collective treasury of consciousness, who is simultaneously most singular and most universally present. The chapter also implicitly answers the question raised in Chapter 1 (ego-Self alienation) by showing what full, though never final, integration of ego and Self looks like. The Stone is not an achievement but a process — like the Tree of Life, it grows continuously; like the Philosophical Tree, it can undergo death and rebirth.

## Key Images / Symbols
- **The fourfold Stone** (Mineral, Vegetable, Magical, Angelical) — four modes of the Self's manifestation, united in the quintessence.
- **Jacob's Ladder / the Bollingen Stone** — the ego-Self axis; the sacred site where personal and transpersonal interpenetrate.
- **The Coniunctio of Sol and Luna** — the union of masculine and feminine principles; the hermaphrodite; the goal of alchemical work and of psychological integration.
- **The Virgin Taming the Unicorn** — the lunar feminine principle capturing and containing the wild masculine spirit; the *coagulatio* of Mercurius by Eros; incarnation as the containing of the divine in the personal.
- **The Crucified Serpent** — the mercurial spirit nailed to the cross; the fixing of the volatile; the necessary suffering that accompanies the *coagulatio* of archetypal content into personal life.
- **The Eye of God / Ubiquity** — the total awareness of the integrated Self from which nothing is hidden; the alchemical parallel to synchronicity.
- **The Alchemical Tree / Tree of Life** — the Self as both ground (roots) and aspiration (branches); the cross/tree as the axis mundi of the individuated personality; the Phoenix of death and rebirth.
- **The Food of Angels / Heavenly Viaticum** — the Self as spiritual nourishment; contact with the Self gives the angelic (archetypal) dimension access to temporal existence; death to the world as liberation from projection.
- **King Midas** — the negative of the Stone's power: the ego that identifies with the Stone and loses its human relatedness; "our gold is not the common gold."

## Subsection Notes

**Transformation and Revelation.** The Stone's power to convert base metals and flints into precious things is the psychological capacity of the Self-relation to find meaning in the most obstinate and unglamorous aspects of life. The alchemical maxim "our gold is not the common gold" means: the goal is not outer success but inner transformation of the base into the valuable. The King Midas story is the cautionary counterpart — the ego inflated by identification with the Stone turns everything to the dead gold of literalism.

**The Fertility Principle.** The Self as *fons et origo* — the source and origin of all psychic life. Edinger connects this to the *benedicta viriditas* (blessed greenness) and the *Anima Mundi* (Soul of the World), both images of the Self as the generative ground from which all forms of life arise. The fertility dreams (the man whose footsteps left grass growing thicker; the phallic grandfather-youth figure) illustrate this attribute spontaneously.

**The Union of Opposites.** The most extended section. Edinger maps the positive and negative aspects of both solar and lunar principles in their one-sided manifestations, then shows how the Stone — the *coniunctio* of both — transcends their mutual destructiveness. The Amor and Psyche myth provides the solar danger (the rending rams); the Gorgon Medusa provides the lunar danger (petrifying congelation). Recovery from either encounter requires time and a wider frame of reference — knowing one is suffering the Stone, not merely being damaged by life.

**Ubiquity.** The Stone sees all because it represents the complete, integrated personality from which nothing is split off. The eye of God is the unified source of consciousness. Active imagination is the practice by which the Stone's omniscience is accessed: bringing unconscious content into image is allowing the "oracle" of the Self to speak in a discernible form.

**Spiritual Food and the Tree of Life.** The final section weaves together the Eucharist, the Tree of Life (Genesis 3), the Philosophical Tree, the cross, and the Phoenix into a unified image of the Self as the ground of inexhaustible regeneration. The individuation process does not end at the discovery of the Stone; it is itself the Tree, growing continuously through the alternation of death and rebirth, loss and recovery, the falling of the huge tree and its eventual renewal.
