# Encounter With the Self
*Ego and Archetype, Part I, pp. 62-106*

## Central Theme
The third stage of the psychic life cycle: after inflation and alienation, the ego arrives — usually through a crisis that exhausts personal resources — at a direct encounter with the Self as a transpersonal center. This chapter maps three dimensions of that encounter: the collective and religious background that has historically mediated it (and what happens when that container fails); the phenomenology of the individual breakthrough moment; and the paradigmatic account in the Book of Job, the richest case study in the text. The chapter closes by sketching what the "individuated ego" looks like — its characteristic sacrificial attitude, its sense of living in a world saturated with meaning.

## Key Ideas
- Collective religion has traditionally protected individuals from both inflation and alienation by carrying the projection of the Self onto a shared god-image (Figure 6). The church holds the archetype for its members, so they need not encounter it personally. This is stable but unconscious — a state of *participation mystique*.
- When the collective container breaks down ("God is dead" — Nietzsche), four outcomes are possible (Figure 7): (1) alienation without restitution, (2) inflation — the ego claims the divine energy, (3) reprojection onto secular or political deities (Communism, Capitalism), or (4) individuation — the individual consciously recovers the inner god-image.
- **The Breakthrough** (section 2): after intense alienation, the ego-Self axis suddenly breaks into conscious view. Jung: "One becomes Two, and the greater figure appears to the lesser personality with the force of a revelation." This encounter always occurs in a state of fugitive alienation — in the wilderness: Moses at the burning bush, Jacob's ladder, Jonah in the whale, the conversion of Paul.
- The encounter with the feminine Self is impregnation by the divine — Danae and the golden shower (Perseus), the Annunciation to Mary, Bernini's Ecstasy of St. Theresa.
- **The Book of Job** as paradigm: read as a record of active imagination; Job's dialogue with his companions represents inner voices of the unconscious. Key psychological moves: (a) Satan and Yahweh are two aspects of the Self — Satan provides the urge to individuation that must break up the psychic *status quo*; (b) Job's inflation is his self-righteousness and his projection of shadow onto others; (c) Yahweh's speech from the whirlwind = the Self's self-revelation, demonstrating the absolute difference between ego and Self; (d) Job's metanoia ("I had heard of thee by the hearing of the ear; but now mine eye seeth thee") = the decisive encounter.
- Elihu (the fourth companion, previously silent) represents Job's previously missing fourth function — his appearance precedes and prepares the theophany.
- The compensatory function of dreams in Job: Elihu reports Job's dreams in which "God makes them listen, and his correction strikes them with terror." This anticipates the Yahweh encounter and is accurate psychological description of the compensatory function of the unconscious.
- **The Individuated Ego** (section 4): individuation is a process, not a destination. The individuated ego is characterized by a *sacrificial attitude* — recognizing its subordinate position and serving the totality rather than making personal demands. A twofold split heals: (1) the split between conscious and unconscious, (2) the split between subject and object, replaced by a sense of unitary reality (*unus mundus*).
- The hidden God is the Gnostic Sophia — the divine wisdom imprisoned in matter (= the Self hidden in the ego's identification). The alchemical opus was the attempt to redeem God by human consciousness — *prima materia* (ego-Self identity) transmuted into the philosopher's stone.
- The individuated ego lives in a world where chance does not exist: "the soul contains the event that shall befall it" (Emerson). Jung's late statement on God: "To this day God is the name by which I designate all things which cross my willful path violently and recklessly."

## Psychological Significance
This is the pivot chapter of Part I. The first two chapters described what the psyche goes through involuntarily; this chapter describes the moment when the ego first consciously meets the agency that has been working on it all along. The Job analysis is Edinger's most extended close reading in the entire book — it demonstrates how to read a religious text as a psychological document and how to use it as a mirror for the individuation process. The chapter also explains why depth psychology appeared as a historical phenomenon: it is what happens when collective religion can no longer carry the Self-projection for modern individuals.

## Key Images / Symbols
- **Figures 6 and 7** (Edinger's diagrams): stable religious community vs. breakdown of projection — the most important schematic in this chapter, showing four possible outcomes when the collective container fails.
- **Jacob's Ladder** (Gustave Dore): encounter with the numinous in the wilderness/exile state; the covenant made in alienation.
- **The Annunciation** (Botticelli) / **Ecstasy of St. Theresa** (Bernini): archetypal images of the feminine ego being impregnated by the divine masculine — the Self entering consciousness through the feeling function.
- **The Conversion of Paul** (woodcut, 1515): the classic ego-Self breakthrough — struck down from the horse of inflation, blinded, transformed.
- **Blake's Job series**: the most sustained artistic rendering of the Job individuation — the fire falling from heaven, Yahweh out of the whirlwind, Behemoth and Leviathan, Job's sacrifice. Behemoth and Leviathan = primordial shadow of God, "the abysmal aspect of God and the depths of his own psyche."
- **Tarot XVI (The Tower)**: the destructive aspect of the breakthrough when the ego is inflated — lightning shatters the tower.
- **St. Christopher carrying Christ** (oil, Master of Messkirch): the individuated ego's burden of carrying the Self-weight through the waters of life.
- **Sophia** (Gnostic): divine wisdom imprisoned in matter = the hidden Self within the ego-inflated psyche. Redemption of Sophia = the alchemical-psychological task.

## Subsection Notes

**1. The Role of the Collective (p. 62):** Collective religion as the historically normal carrier of the Self-projection. Taboos, rituals, confessional — all serve to protect the individual from unmediated inflation or alienation. The Mithraic "Rite of the Crown" as anti-inflation ritual. When the container fails, four outcomes (Fig. 7). The rise of depth psychology is itself a symptom of the breakdown of collective containers.

**2. The Breakthrough (p. 69):** The moment the ego-Self axis breaks into conscious view. Jung's description (CW 9i, par. 217). Characterized by occurring in a state of alienation/wilderness. Biblical examples: Moses, Jacob, Jonah, Paul. The feminine form as divine impregnation. Clinical example: a woman's dream of the three-legged bronze man (Discobolus plus phallus), which correlates with alchemical drawings and sun wheels — an encounter with the Self through the animus.

**3. The Book of Job (p. 77):** Read as individual active imagination. The wager between Yahweh and Satan = transpersonal forces arranging Job's ordeal. Job's inflation = self-righteousness, shadow projection. His three companions = voice of conventional, regressive adaptation. Elihu = fourth function, bridging to theophany. Yahweh's speech = revelation of the total difference between Self and ego. Behemoth and Leviathan = God's own shadow. Job's metanoia. Rudolf Otto on the *numinosum* — the mysterium tremendum et fascinans as the appeasement of Job's soul. Satan as the "bringer of light" — the suffering he inflicts drives man inward.

**4. The Individuated Ego (p. 96):** Not a static goal but an ongoing process. Key qualities: sacrificial attitude (ego serves totality); dialogue between conscious and unconscious replaces the repeating inflation-alienation cycle; unitary reality replaces the subject-object split; events experienced as meaningful rather than accidental. Dreams open transpersonal categories. The anima as bridge to the archetypal psyche. Alchemical parallel: prima materia transmuted to the stone = ego-Self identity refined into conscious relationship.
