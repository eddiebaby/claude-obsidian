# The Spiritual Path of Transformation

[[Eckhard Frick|Eckhard Frick's]] paper (JAP 2025, 70:3, 374–392), companion to [[roesler2025-process-of-transformation|Roesler's]] in the same Freiburg-conference issue. Frick — a Jungian analyst *and* a Jesuit who accompanies Ignatian retreats — reads transformation as a **circular, spiritual** process, using [[Wilfred Bion|Bion's]] concept of **[[Faith in O|O]]** as the bridge between analytic and spiritual language.

## Transformation Is Circular, Not Linear

The paper's spine: transformation "is not a linear but a circular process, encompassing experiences of standstill and deadlock where no longitudinal continuation is visible." Jung describes it in alchemical terms as a shared dialectical "reaction" between patient and analyst (CW 16, "Psychology of the Transference"). Vogel (2017) characterizes the Jungian model as a circular "therapeutic process theory"; Stein (2008): individuation "never comes to a final resting place where one can say 'It is done.'" The **Self never manifests itself entirely** — the analytic couple orients toward an "uncontrollable archetype" that stays partly out of reach. Frick's Figure 1 is a circle joining A (apophatic negation), O (ultimate reality), and K (knowledge).

## Bion's O and Faith in O

Full treatment: [[Faith in O]]. Frick adopts Bion's distinction between **K** (knowledge *about* something) and **O** (the ultimate, unknowable reality — the Kantian thing-in-itself, "the formless infinite"). Transformation is the movement **K → O**; Bion's notation Tα → Tβ = K → O. In religious terms O is "the transcendent mystery of God," approachable only apophatically — via **negative theology** (Eckhart, St John of the Cross's "dark night"), by negation, saying only what may *not* be said of God. "Faith in O" is what both patient and analyst need when the process's goal stays implicit and unconscious.

## Discernment of Spirits and Its Three Failure Modes

Full treatment: [[Discernment of Spirits]]. Frick draws Ignatius of Loyola's method — distinguishing **consolation** from **desolation**, movements from the Holy Spirit vs. the Devil's spirit — and applies it to the archetype of the **spirit (*Geist*)**, which Jung insisted is bipolar: it can inflate and seize (*Ergriffenheit* — Nietzsche seized by the spirit; Jung's own diagnosis of German "Wotan" spirituality). The three defensive extremes when confronting the spirit archetype:
- **Spiritual bypassing** — avoiding transformation by rationalization, persona-shaping, denial of the body, ideology.
- **Spiritual neglect** — not valuing the spiritual dimension (one's own or the other's), reducing it to childish needs.
- **Spiritual inflation** — being seized/possessed by a malignant spirit, "often with the best intentions."

"All in all, spirituality requires discernment." Critically, this discernment "is not cross-sectional, not even longitudinal; it is circular" — the same shape as the whole transformation process.

## Spiritus Contra Spiritum

Full treatment: [[Spiritus contra spiritum]]. Frick's best example of spiritual transformation: Jung's famous letter to **Bill Wilson**, AA co-founder, formulating a discernment between the Devil and the Holy Spirit — *spiritus contra spiritum* — which inspired the AA 12 steps. Frick reads AA spirituality as "the best studied of all Jungian therapies," and as a caution against the "hungry ghosts" of accelerated psychedelic spirituality (noting Bill Wilson's own later LSD experiments). Includes three anonymized AA members' first-person accounts of what "spirituality" and "a power greater than ourselves" mean to them without a fixed creed.

## Createdness

Full treatment: [[Createdness]]. Jung's reading of the Ignatian "Principle and Foundation" — *El hombre es criado* / *Creatus est homo*, "the human person is created": we are *anticipated by the unconscious*, created rather than creators. "You don't create, you are created; in creation you are created." Inflation is precisely the ego's claim to originate its own creative power. This humble acceptance of the unconscious's priority is, in Frick's words, "Jung's way of expressing Bion's faith in O."

## The Body Is Not Bypassed

A guardrail Frick foregrounds via the Zarathustra Seminar: "no individuation can take place… without the animal… that one black spot, which is the earth, is absolutely indispensable on the bright shield of spirituality." Spirituality that tries to escape the body, sex, and instinct "is a mistake, a lie." Neurobiological note: an fMRI study of an Ignatian retreat (Wintering et al. 2021) found increased connectivity in self-processing regions (posterior cingulate, default-mode network) — but Frick stresses this pre-post design is "absolutely linear," unlike the circular process it tries to measure.
