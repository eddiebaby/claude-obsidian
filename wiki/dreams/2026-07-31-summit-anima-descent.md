# 2026-07-31 Friday

> Backlog ingest 2026-09-15: logged in the Claude app Dreams project; synchronicity follow-up added 8/03 (New Yorker article). Analysis ported and condensed from those sessions.

## The Dream

I was mountain climbing with an unknown woman — presumably my anima. We get to the top of the mountain and it's a small little summit, snowy. I take off the safety gear and I saddle the summit, and she comes up with me and we talk. It's sunset. It's getting dark fast. Suddenly it's time to climb down, for all the sunlight is gone. I'm trying to put a sling around the rock so we can safely get down. That's all I remember. But I remember the quality of the light in the scene — very particular. Almost eerie.

**Later dream:** In a cell phone store. People I know from real life, particularly Chris Botta's ex-girlfriend. We're talking about living on the Indian reservation (I lived on the Blackfeet Nation). I commented how it felt like there were no rules — if you shot somebody, a state trooper would have to drive 150 miles over.

## Waking Associations

- **Synchronicity (added 8/03):** The next day I picked up the New Yorker and there was an article about a man who took his girlfriend up Austria's highest mountain, left her there, and got charged with manslaughter. I thought the dream was prospective.

## First Reading — Claude App Dreams Project (ported)

### Symbols & Amplification (app)

**Gear off at the top.** The gear held him to the mountain the whole ascent; at the highest point, on the smallest surface, he removes it. Saddling the summit = the posture of a man with nowhere to stand who must grip with his whole body.

**The climbing anima.** Accepted with one qualification: *she climbed*. Wore gear, arrived under her own power, matched his pace, sat with him where two barely fit. In the series the feminine has been met, followed, pulled toward; this one was roped to him for the whole ascent. Something changed in her capacity — what changed in him to permit it?

**The film stops at the anchor.** The dream ends at the technical, irreversible moment: rigging a sling in the dark for two people's full weight, no light left to check the work. Getting up was the simple half. **The whole test is whether he can bring her down.** (Ripley via Jung: the work begins toward sunset, turns toward midnight, stays dark a long season before the colours come — the descent into darkness is the opening of the opus, which is why the dream did not end at the summit.)

**The eerie light.** The psyche reporting in the dreamer's first language (photography): the hour is the beginning of the work, not the end of the day.

**The reservation scene.** Both dreams place the ego where enforcement is absent — gear removed by his own hand; the trooper 150 miles away. And the gun has moved: from smuggled object → legal object → *language* — a hypothetical inside a subordinate clause, spoken with a woman in a store of devices for reaching people across distance.

### The Synchronicity, Read Strictly

Not prospective in Jung's sense (a dream anticipating psychic development) — synchronicity in the strict sense: two facts bound only by meaning, the archetype live in the psyche when he slept and still live when he opened the magazine. The article supplies the ending the dream withheld — the version where the man *does not bring her down* — and the law names it: **manslaughter, the charge with no intent, the category of failed responsibility for another life.** The second dream said the law is 150 miles away; within a day the world answered that the trooper arrives. Note also what the psyche declined to dream: the dream-ego's hands were *on the anchor, working* — abandonment appeared in the newspaper, not in him. Close enough to be legible, far enough that he is still on the right side of it.
