# Mercurius (Alchemical Symbol)

## Jung's Core Interpretation

Mercurius is the central symbol of alchemy — simultaneously the starting material (*prima materia*), the process itself (*opus*), and the end product (*lapis*). He stands at the beginning and end of the work. In psychological terms he is the autonomous, transforming factor within the unconscious: the archetype of psychic transformation itself.

**The paradox of Mercurius**: "He is metallic yet liquid, matter yet spirit, cold yet fiery, poison and yet healing draught — a symbol uniting all opposites." (CW 12, ¶404 area)

He is not any single substance but the *principle of transformation* projected into matter.

---

## His Many Names and Faces

In the alchemical literature Mercurius appears under a vast list of synonyms:

| Name | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| *Argentum vivum* | Living silver / quicksilver |
| *Aqua permanens* | Permanent water |
| *Aqua nostra* | Our water |
| *Aqua mercurialis* | Mercurial water |
| *Mercurius vivus* | Living Mercurius |
| *Succus lunariae* | Sap of the moon-plant |
| *Vinum ardens* | Burning wine |
| *Aqua vitae* | Water of life |
| *Spiritus* | Spirit |
| *Caput corvi* | Head of the crow (nigredo stage) |
| *Cauda pavonis* | Peacock's tail (iridescent mid-stage) |
| *Lumen novum* | New light (at opus completion) |

All are the same substance at different stages of the work. The alchemists "never tired of devising new and expressive synonyms" (CW 12, ¶94).

---

## His Dual Nature

Mercurius is *duplex* — he contains all opposites:

- **Male and female**: he begins as the hermaphrodite, splits into the brother-sister duality (*Sol* and *Luna*), and is reunited in the *coniunctio*
- **Spirit and matter**: "the divine winged Hermes manifest in matter" — the god of revelation imprisoned in substance
- **Poison and medicine**: the *tinctura* that dissolves all created things also leads to the lapis
- **Beginning and end**: he IS the prima materia, and he IS what the prima materia becomes (the stone)
- **Psychopomp and quicksilver**: the redeemer of souls and the liquid metal
- **Light and darkness**: the *lumen novum* emerges from the *nigredo* of which he is also the origin

The *coniunctio* reunites what Mercurius originally split from himself. He is both the problem and the solution.

## Mercurius as Dragon

The dragon is "probably the oldest pictorial symbol in alchemy" and it IS Mercurius (CW 12, ¶404):

- Dragon = *monstrum* = serpent (chthonic principle) + bird (aerial principle)
- The *Uroboros* (tail-eating dragon) with legend *ἓν τὸ πᾶν* ("the One, the All") — Codex Marcianus, 10th cent.
- The opus was often called *circulare* (circular) or *rota* (the wheel) because Mercurius begins and ends it, like a dragon eating its tail
- As dragon he devours himself and dies, to rise again as the lapis

The dragon-as-Mercurius is the prima materia in its initial, undifferentiated, self-consuming state.

## Mercurius in the Dream Series (CW 12, Part II)

Jung documents Mercurius appearing directly in the dreams of his patient:

**Dream 14** (¶85-88): The dreamer seeks "a man with a pointed beard" in America. Jung identifies this as Mephistopheles — the daemonic aspect of the intellect, the archetypal *familiaris*. "Mercurius as quicksilver is an eminently suitable symbol for the fluid intellect" (¶90). He is "sometimes a spirit and sometimes a water, the so-called *aqua permanens*."

The dream teaches: the intellect, when unconscious, is *daemonic* — not evil, but autonomous and uncontrolled, masquerading as a servant while actually ruling.

**Dream 22** (¶200-211): Mercurius appears as the red-haired man who turns the eight-spoked wheel — the *telum passionis*, the weapon of passion and transformation. He is "the fiery wheel of the essence" in Böhme's mysticism; the master-workman of the *filius philosophorum* (the golden child = the Self).

---

## The Wheel and *Circulatio*

In alchemy the wheel (*rota*) = Mercurius in action:

- The wheel = the *circulatio* = the ascending and descending process (*ascensus et descensus*)
- God's descent to man and man's ascent to God
- Böhme: "The form of the birth is as a turning wheel, which Mercurius causes in the sulphur"
- The wheel's mystical associations: *constantia, obedientia, moderatio, aequalitas, humilitas*
- The mandala (circular drawing of the centre) IS the wheel IS Mercurius in his wholeness function
- ¶216: "the wheel appears here as a concept for wholeness which represents the essence of mandala symbolism and therefore includes the *mysterium iniquitatis*"

## Mercurius as Uniting Symbol

In Gnosticism the Anthropos figure (divine original man) descends into matter, is embraced by Physis, and from that union the seven hermaphroditic beings (planets/metals) are born. Mercurius is this Anthropos:

- The androgynous original man / Anthropos of Gnosticism
- Parallel: Indian *purusha*, the primordial being who divides himself to create the world
- His shadow falls on earth, his image is reflected in water — he is both above and below
- In alchemy, the seven planets spring from the hermaphrodite Mercurius

As caduceus (fig. 148), Mercurius literally holds the paired opposites (the two serpents) in balance. He is the principle that can hold the tension of opposites without collapsing to one side.

---

## Psychological Meaning

Mercurius is the archetype of psychological transformation:

1. **The Self in process** — not the Self as destination (lapis) but the Self as mover, the dynamic force that drives individuation
2. **The autonomous unconscious** — what the alchemist projects into matter is the autonomous factor in the psyche that cannot be controlled but can be worked with
3. **The trickster elevated** — mercurial intelligence, cunning, fluidity; the shadow of the one-sided rational mind
4. **The Third that reconciles** — when two opposites are held in tension long enough, Mercurius produces the reconciling symbol

When Mercurius appears in dreams:
- As a fluid, ambiguous figure — announces the beginning of transformation
- As a daemonic helper (man with pointed beard, red-haired stranger) — the autonomous intellect, or the shadow of intellect
- As a serpent, dragon, or uroboros — the self-consuming beginning of the process
- As light, new fire, or strange illumination — nearing the lapis

---

## Key Quote

"Mercurius stands at the beginning and end of the work: he is the *prima materia*, the *caput corvi*, the *nigredo*; as dragon he devours himself and as dragon he dies, to rise again as the *lapis*. He is the play of colours in the *cauda pavonis* and the division into four elements. He is the hermaphrodite that was in the beginning, that splits into the classical brother-sister duality and is reunited in the *coniunctio*, to appear once again at the end in the radiant form of the *lumen novum*, the stone." (CW 12, ¶404)
