# Sol and Luna (Sun and Moon in Alchemy)

## Jung's Core Interpretation

Sol (Sun) and Luna (Moon) are the primary alchemical pair of opposites whose union (*coniunctio*) produces the *lapis*. Sol represents the masculine, conscious, spiritual principle; Luna represents the feminine, unconscious, lunar principle. They are simultaneously cosmological opposites, psychological principles, and the two partners in the alchemical marriage.

The relationship between Sol and Luna runs through the entire opus as its central dynamic tension.

---

## Sol: The Father / Consciousness Principle

In the alchemical dream series, Dream 15 (¶91):

"The dreamer is rejected by his father."

Jung interprets: the father = collective consciousness = the traditional spirit = "the spirit that has put on the earthly garment of the Church." The father rejects the dreamer because he has dared to recognize the anima (the dark woman) as equal.

**Sol psychologically**:
- Collective consciousness, cultural authority, established order
- The father-principle and its institutions
- Gold in its conventional sense (what collective opinion values highest)
- The masculine rational faculty in its dominant, unquestioned form

Sol as "King Sol with six planet-sons" (fig. 79 in CW 12 — Bonus, *Pretiosa margarita novella*, 1546): "One is powerful, Six are subjects... One is the king, the six are his servants and his son." The king commands but also depends on his court — consciousness cannot function without its subordinate functions.

---

## Luna: The Mother / Unconscious Principle

From ¶92: "Just as the father represents collective consciousness, the traditional spirit, so the mother stands for the collective unconscious, the source of the water of life."

The mother pours water from basin to basin (Dream 15). But who is actually the source of life? "The mother is superior to the son, but the sister is his equal." The anima (sister) is the individualized, personal face of the maternal unconscious.

*Succus lunariae* — "sap of the moon-plant" — refers to the nocturnal origin of the mercurial water. Luna's water nourishes what the Sun cannot reach.

**Luna psychologically**:
- The collective unconscious, especially in its maternal aspect
- The anima as mediating figure
- The instinctual, nocturnal, cyclical dimension of psychic life
- The feminine body wisdom that rational consciousness ignores
- The receptive capacity that consciousness must develop to be fertilized by the unconscious

## The Life-Renewing Bath (Figure 27, CW 12)

"Life-renewing influence of the conjoined sun and moon on the bath" — Milan, Biblioteca Ambrosiana, Codex I.

Three figures bathe beneath the archway. Above them, Sol and Luna together shine on the bath. This image appears in Dream 16 context (¶96-97): the bath (*balneum*) is the place of dissolution — where the old consciousness is softened, liquefied, made ready for transformation.

What heals is not Sol alone nor Luna alone, but their *joint* influence. Analytical psychology: neither pure reason nor pure instinct heals. The healing comes from the tension and eventual union of both.

---

## Sol's Descent

The axiom from ¶26: alchemy as an undercurrent to Christianity. Just as Christ (Sol-spirit) descended into matter, the alchemical process represents a further, fuller descent:

"The higher, the spiritual, the masculine inclines to the lower, the earthly, the feminine; and accordingly, the mother, who was anterior to the world of the father, accommodates herself to the masculine principle and, with the aid of the human spirit (alchemy or 'the philosophy'), produces a son — not the antithesis of Christ but rather his chthonic counterpart."

Sol must descend into Luna's domain (the unconscious, matter, the feminine) if the *coniunctio* is to occur. The king who does not descend from his throne cannot be renewed.

This is why the alchemical dream series shows the dreamer *rejected by the father* (Sol's withdrawal) and taken up by the anima — the path goes through the lunar domain before returning to the solar.

---

## The Conjoined Sol and Luna = the Mercurial Couple

Figure 23 (CW 12): "The mystic vessel where the two natures unite (sol and luna, caduceus) to produce the *filius hermaphroditus*."

In the *coniunctio*, Sol and Luna lose their separate identities to produce a third thing:
- The *filius hermaphroditus* = the hermaphroditic child = the Self
- *Mercurius* in his final form = the new light (*lumen novum*)
- The philosopher's stone = the product of the marriage

The caduceus (fig. 146: "Mercurius as uniting symbol") = two serpents entwined = Sol and Luna in serpentine form, united by the winged staff of Hermes/Mercurius.

## Psychological Meaning

| | Sol | Luna |
|--|-----|------|
| Principle | Masculine, spiritual | Feminine, chthonic |
| Psychic function | Consciousness, ego | Unconscious, Self-in-potential |
| Direction | Transcendence, ascent | Immanence, descent |
| Quality | Gold (enlightenment) | Silver (reflection) |
| Danger | Inflation, rigidity | Dissolution, flooding |
| Task in *coniunctio* | Yield its dominance | Yield its autonomy |

Both Sol and Luna are *necessary* but neither can complete the opus alone:
- Pure Sol = sterile rationalism, cut off from the unconscious
- Pure Luna = flooding by unconscious, loss of any orienting centre

The individuation process moves between them: inflation (too much Sol) → depression/dissolution (Luna's correction) → renewed consciousness that includes both.

## Dream Context

Sol and Luna themes in dreams:
- The sun and moon appearing together — the *coniunctio* approaching or achieved
- A solar eclipse — consciousness being darkened by the unconscious (preliminary to transformation)
- Moonlight on water — the anima making the unconscious luminous
- A king and queen — the masculine and feminine principles facing each other (before the bath)
- Night illuminated by a strange internal light — Sol after the *coniunctio*, shining from within
