# Horse (Symbol)

## Jung's Core Interpretation

The horse is a symbol of the animal component in man, representing instinctual life and libido. Horses are creatures of wind and speed, connected to sexual instinct and the unconscious drives that underlie consciousness.

**Key claim**: The horse symbolizes properties and qualities that psychologically belong to the unconscious of man. It is an animal of the libido.

---

## Amplification Examples (Cross-Cultural Parallels)

### Germanic & Scandinavian Mythology
- The eight-legged horse **Sleipnir** (Wotan's mount) represents shamanic journeying between worlds
- German riddle: "Who are the two that go to the Thing? Together they have three eyes, ten feet and one tail, and thus they travel over the land" (referring to a horse and rider as psychopomp)

### Classical & Christian Symbolism
- Horses are connected with the **devil** through hoofed symbolism (cloven hoof = Satanic)
- In Mithraism and pagan religion, horses had connections to fertility and sacrifice
- The devil "rides" sleepers (nightmare = demon-ridden), so horses are said to be ridden by the devil
- **Centaurs** are wind-gods and represent libidinal nature
- **Pegasus** struck a fountain from the earth with his hoof (creative/generative power)

### Vedic & Hindu Sources
The **Brihadaranyaka Upanishad** describes the sacrificial horse as cosmologically structured:
- Dawn is the head; the sun is the eye
- The wind is the breath; fire is the open mouth
- The year is the body; sky is the back; earth is the belly
- The poles are the flanks; intermediate space is the belly
- The directions are the sides; seasons are the limbs
- Days/nights are the feet; stars are the bones
- The ocean is the kinsman/cradle

**Psychological meaning**: The horse contains the whole world; it is a time-symbol and world-symbol expressing cosmic order and libido's infinite potential.

## Psychological Meaning

### As Libido Symbol
The horse represents:
1. **Animal instinct and sexual drive** - the unregenerate libido
2. **Prophetic/clairvoyant power** - horses in mythology can see ghosts and speak prophecies (connecting past and future)
3. **Mantic abilities** - horses possess knowledge of hidden things (in Iliad, horses hear the words of Achilles' corpse and speak; they prophesy evil)
4. **The double nature** - simultaney bearing the rider (consciousness) while remaining a creature of instinct
5. **Wind and swiftness** - spiritual or energetic mobility

### The Horse as Psychopomp
Horses lead the way to the underworld (Charon's horse; the Valkyries' horses; Nordic shamanic tradition). They are guides between conscious and unconscious worlds.

## Dream Context

When horses appear in dreams, they typically signal:
- **Instinctual energy** rising into consciousness
- **Sexual or libidinous impulses** demanding attention
- **Spiritual journeys** or transitions between psychological states
- **The need to integrate animal nature** with consciousness
- **Loss of control** over instinctual drives (being "ridden" by the horse rather than riding it)

Jung notes: "One can therefore take it as certain that man's domestication cost him the heaviest sacrifices. An age which created the Stoic ideal must doubtless have known why and against what it was set up" (CW 5, para 422).

## Sacred Symbolism

### The Horse Shoe & Hoof Marks
- **Horseshoes** are apotropaic (ward off evil/sorcery), connected to fertility and blessing
- **Hoof-marks** establish sacred boundaries and mark possession
- In fertility rites, horses were used to consecrate ground and determine ownership
- The horse's foot is a **phallic symbol** (generative, creative power)

### The Quaridriga (Four-Horse Chariot)
In Dio Chrysostom's mystical quadriga:
- The highest god drives the chariot round in a circle
- Four horses represent the four elements
- The outermost horse moves swiftly (fire/time)
- The inner horses move progressively slower, merging with substance
- The horses dissolve and merge with "the strongest and most fiery, which now becomes the character" (the pure libido)

**Interpretation**: This is an astronomical symbol of time and also expresses the transformation of libido through the four elements, converging toward pure creative force.

## Libidinal Transformation

Jung emphasizes the connection between horse speed and **wind symbolism** (tertium comparationis = libido-symbol). The horse's velocity represents:
- The flow of libido through time
- Spiritual aspiration and seeking
- The drive toward transformation
- Unconscious forces that carry consciousness forward

The horse as the wind-symbol also connects to the **Aeolian/wind element** in Persian mysticism and the libido's capacity to "blow where it will."

## Variants & Related Forms

- **Three-legged horse** (Yama's mount in Buddhist cosmology) - destructive-creative libi
do at the point of world dissolution
- **White horse** (Mother Holle, fertility goddess in German folklore) - the nurturing/maternal aspect of libido
- **Black horse** (Hecate's companion) - the dark, devouring maternal libido
- **Fiery/golden horses** (solar symbolism, Helios) - consciousness, spiritual aspiration

---

## Key Quote from Jung

"The horse, as a symbol of the animal component in man, has numerous connections with the devil. The devil has a horse's hoof and sometimes a horse's form. At critical moments he shows the proverbial cloven hoof, just as, during the abduction of Hadding, Sleipnir suddenly looked out from behind Wotan's mantle" (CW 5, para 422).

The horse is the vehicle (psychopomp) through which instinctual libido travels and transforms. It represents what must be integrated, not repressed.
