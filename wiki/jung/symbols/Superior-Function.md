# Superior Function (Symbol)

## Core Interpretation

The superior (dominant) psychic function that must be sacrificed for transformation and spiritual advancement. In Augustine's case, his intellect had to be sacrificed for his feeling to develop. In Tertullian and Origen, similarly, the superior function (intellect) was sacrificed to achieve the paradoxical truth of Christianity. This sacrifice alone permits the inferior function to access the highest value.

## Key Meanings

- Dominant function
- Must be sacrificed
- Transformation requirement
- Consciousness barrier
- Spiritual obstacle
- Ego stronghold

## Psychological Meaning

- Superior becomes obstacle
- Sacrifice enables growth
- Paradox accessible only through sacrifice
- Inferior contains highest value
- Ego-death prerequisite
- Function differentiation

## The Capture Reflex (von Franz, via Qwarnström 2026)

The superior function's typical move is not just to neglect its opposite but to actively **seize and administer it on its own terms** — "like an eagle seizing a mouse." An introvert who intellectualizes away a real pull toward relationship ("she's just my anima projection") is using the superior function to pre-empt ever genuinely meeting the inferior one. This is the failure mode: the superior function annexes rather than sacrifices. See [[One-Sidedness]] for the clinical elaboration, worked through Coriolanus (dominant Introverted Thinking, refusing to cede ground to Extraverted Feeling until it breaks him).
