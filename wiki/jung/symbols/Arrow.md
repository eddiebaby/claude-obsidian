# Arrow (Symbol)

## Jung's Core Interpretation

The arrow is a symbol of piercing wound, masculine penetration, and the painful impact of desire and libido. It represents the sharp, cutting quality of conscious intention meeting the body/unconscious.

**Key meaning**: "The arrow has a masculine significance; hence the Oriental custom of describing brave sons as the arrows or javelins of their father" (CW 5, para 439).

---

## Amplification: Cross-Cultural Parallels

### Oriental & Vedic
- In Vedic tradition, arrows symbolize **brave sons** and paternal virility
- The Psalms declare (127:4-5): "As arrows in the hand of a mighty man, so are the children of youth... Happy is the man that hath his quiver full of them"
- Birth of sons announced by hanging a bow and arrow in front of the house (Chinese custom)
- Arrow-heads mark masculine fertility and the father's generative power

### Christian & Medieval
- **St. Sebastian** pierced with arrows: the martyr-saint whose body is riddled with arrows representing spiritual suffering and divine love
- Anna Catherina Emmerich (German nun, 1774-1824): received stigmata and complained of constant arrow-like piercing in her heart
- Medieval mystics interpreted the arrow-wound as the **soul's longing for God**, expressed through erotic/bodily pain

### Mythological
- **Cupid's arrows** - love/desire wounding the heart
- The arrow of Apollo and Artemis - divine, piercing, destroying the unintegrated
- **Prometheus wounded** - the arrow of divine punishment
- **Orestes pursued** by the Erinyes with arrows of remorse

---

## Psychological Meaning: The Wounded Soul

Jung emphasizes that the arrow-wound is not merely physical but **spiritual**:

### As Inner Conflict
The arrow represents:
1. **Repressed desires sticking in the flesh** - "the wounding and painful shafts do not come from outside, through gossip, which only pricks us on the surface, but from the ambush of our own unconscious. It is our own repressed desires that stick like arrows in our flesh" (CW 5, para 438).

2. **Unacknowledged erotic impulses** - The arrow is often described in connection with love/desire. When repressed, these become painful, piercing thoughts that torture consciousness.

3. **Guilt and self-judgment** - The arrow of one's own conscience, internalized. Gossip, jealousy, and "uncharitable slanders" become unbearable when they activate unconscious guilt.

### The Mystic Dimension
In St. Theresa of Avila and other Christian mystics, the arrow-wound is transformed into **spiritual ecstasy**:
- The soul's pain is exquisite precisely because it is unfulfilled desire for union with God
- The arrow becomes the vehicle of transformation: suffering becomes sanctification
- "The wounding and painful shafts do not come from outside... but from the ambush of our own unconscious"

## Dream Context & Compensation

When arrows appear in dreams, they often signal:
- **Repressed sexual or aggressive impulses** demanding integration
- **Anxiety about being wounded** by others or by one's own desires
- **The need to penetrate** some psychological barrier or defend oneself
- **Spiritual yearning** disguised as bodily pain
- **Divine punishment or judgment** (internalized super-ego)

Jung notes that dreams of arrow-wounds often compensate for **conscious underestimation of one's inner life**. The soul wounds itself with its own "arrows" (sharp thoughts, desires, judgment) when denied conscious expression.

## Variants & Transformations

### The Masculine Arrow
- Phallic symbol of penetration and creative power
- Represents the active, piercing aspect of libido
- When wielded by the hero (male dreamer), the arrow = conscious intention
- When wielded AGAINST the dreamer = unconscious impulses

### The Feminine Arrow (Rare)
- Artemis's arrows - divine, destructive, unpredictable
- Connected to the Virgin Huntress - the arrow defends her boundaries
- Represents the protective, destructive feminine rather than generative masculine

### The Arrow & The Lance
- Similar symbolism (penetrating instrument)
- The lance of Longinus pierced Christ's side
- The grail legends connect the lance to the king's wound (sexuality/fertility wound)

## Historical Examples: Saint Sufferings

Jung cites **Anna Catherina Emmerich** (1774-1824), German nun with stigmata:

She complained of constant "arrow-like piercing" in her heart. Jung interprets this not as supernatural visitation but as the eruption into consciousness of:
- Repressed sexual desire (she was a nun, hence celibate)
- Unfulfilled erotic longing converted into "spiritual" suffering
- The piercing pain is the body's way of speaking what consciousness denies: "I want, I desire, I suffer"

Jung's point: "Mysteries substitute a physical pain for a psychic pain which is not felt because repressed. The nun's interpretation of this pain is based, as usual, on a projection: it is always the others who secretly scheme against her, but her own interpretation of the pain is based on a projection of her repressed wishes" (CW 5, para 436).

---

## The Arrow as Compensation

In psychic development, the arrow-wound often appears as a **compensatory symbol**:
- Consciousness has become too one-sided, too rational, too defended
- The unconscious "shoots" the arrow of desire/feeling to force reintegration
- The wound is necessary: it breaks through defenses and makes consciousness aware of what it has excluded

The arrow is thus a necessary **instrument of individuation** - the wounding that leads to wholeness.
