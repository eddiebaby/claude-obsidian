# Prima Materia (Alchemical Symbol)

## Jung's Core Interpretation

The *prima materia* is the starting substance of the alchemical opus — the raw, undifferentiated, chaotic matter that must be transformed into the *lapis* (Philosopher's Stone). Psychologically it is the initial state of the unconscious before the work of individuation begins: dark, formless, containing everything in potentia.

The paradox: **the prima materia already contains the goal**. The lapis is latent within it. The work does not import anything new — it draws out what was always present but hidden.

---

## Its Many Names

The alchemists used hundreds of synonyms for the prima materia because they could not openly name what it was. The obscurity was intentional — the substance is psychic and cannot be pointed at directly:

| Name | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| *Serpens Mercurii* | The mercurial serpent = dragon |
| *Massa confusa* | The confused/chaotic mass |
| *Chaos* | Primordial undifferentiation |
| *Dragon* | The self-devouring Uroboros |
| *Nigredo* | The blackening, the beginning |
| *Caput corvi* | Head of the crow |
| *Vera prima materia* | The true first matter (= rust/verdigris) |
| *Mercurius* | In his beginning aspect |
| *Anima mundi* | The world soul before liberation |
| The sick king | The king awaiting renewal (fig. 149) |

Jung: "cannot proclaim aloud just what the prima materia or the lapis is" (CW 12, ¶402) — the alchemist must veil it in symbols.

## The Serpens Mercurii

From Part I of CW 12 (¶26), Jung gives the foundational definition:

The even numbers interpolated between the uneven numbers of Christian dogma are "personified by the *serpens mercurii*, the dragon that creates and destroys itself and represents the *prima materia*."

This is the axiom of Maria Prophetissa set in cosmological terms:
- The masculine, uneven, spiritual principle (Christianity)
- Compensated by the even, feminine, chthonic principle (alchemy)
- The serpent-dragon = the Uroboros = the self-consuming starting point

The prima materia is *serpentine*: it winds back on itself, it both creates and destroys, it is formless and yet contains all forms.

---

## Rust as Prima Materia

One of CW 12's most striking passages (¶207-208):

The *Rosarium philosophorum* quotes: "Our gold is not the common gold. But thou hast inquired concerning the greenness [*viriditas*, = verdigris/rust]... Therefore I say unto thee that whatever is perfect in the bronze is that greenness only, because that greenness is straightway changed by our magistery into our most true gold."

Rust (verdigris, the green sickness of metal) IS the *vera prima materia*. The imperfection, the leprosy, the decay — this is the basis for the philosophical gold. Jung's comment: "there is no light without shadow and no psychic wholeness without imperfection. To round itself out, life calls not for perfection but for completeness."

**Psychological meaning**: the prima materia is not something special to be sought outside. It is precisely the despised, neglected, decayed thing — the shadow material, the inferior function, the repressed content — that contains the gold.

## The Sick King

Figure 149 in CW 12 (18th-cent. MS) depicts "the sick king (*prima materia*), from whose heart the planet-children receive their crowns."

The king who must be renewed — who lies sick, dying, imprisoned — IS the prima materia. His sickness is the nigredo, the initial suffering of the unconscious projection. From his dying heart the six planet-sons (metals/functions) draw their life. His renewal is the production of the *filius philosophorum* = the renewed king = the Self.

**Pattern**: The prima materia is always the exhausted, suffering, incomplete state of consciousness at the beginning of a transformation. The "old king" must die so the new can be born.

## The Stone in the Waters of the Nile

The oldest recorded alchemical statement, from Ostanes (1st century BC), quoted by Zosimos (3rd century AD): "Go to the waters of the Nile and there you will find a stone that has a spirit [*pneuma*]. Take this, divide it, thrust in your hand and draw out its heart: for its soul [*psyche*] is in its heart." (CW 12, ¶405)

The prima materia is:
- Found in the most ordinary place (the Nile, everywhere)
- Has a spirit inside it (the imprisoned pneuma)
- Must be divided, opened, and its heart extracted (the opus)
- The soul IS the heart — it is a psychic substance, not a chemical one

Nietzsche's metaphor from *Zarathustra* points the same way: "an image slumbers for me in the stone." The alchemist sought to wake it.

## Psychological Meaning

The prima materia psychologically is:

1. **The raw unconscious** — the initial, undifferentiated mass of the psyche before any differentiation of archetypes or complexes. Totality in potentia.

2. **The rejected shadow** — whatever the ego has deemed worthless, inferior, or too chaotic to integrate. The prima materia is hidden in what is most despised.

3. **The starting point of individuation** — the initial confrontation with the unconscious, the "dark night of the soul," the depression or confusion that signals the beginning of the work.

4. **The body** — alchemy consistently points toward matter, the body, the concrete and chthonic. The prima materia is not abstract; it is embodied, smelly, dark, heavy.

5. **The anima mundi** — the world soul as yet unliberated, waiting in the elements for the alchemist who will recognize and redeem her.

> [!key-insight] The prima materia cannot be sought outside
> It is already present in the initial disorder and imperfection of the psyche. The work is always "with what you have" — the chaos of one's own life.

## Dream Context

When prima materia themes appear in dreams:
- Scenes of formless confusion, floods, mud, primordial darkness
- Encounters with raw, undifferentiated energy that is both terrifying and generative
- The "sick king" — powerful figures diminished, imprisoned, in need of renewal
- Decay, rot, dissolution — but which somehow feels necessary rather than final
- The serpent biting its tail, wheels within wheels, self-consuming processes
