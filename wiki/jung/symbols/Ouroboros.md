# Ouroboros (Symbol)

## Core Interpretation

The self-consuming dragon: serpent or dragon eating its own tail in an endless circle. The oldest documented alchemical symbol. In CW 5 it represents the eternal cycle of creation-destruction, the totality of the psyche. In CW 12 Jung reveals it is specifically the symbol of **Mercurius** — both the prima materia at the beginning of the opus and the completed lapis at the end.

---

## CW 12: The Alchemical Ouroboros

From ¶404, CW 12 (*Psychology and Alchemy*):

> "The dragon is probably the oldest pictorial symbol in alchemy of which we have documentary evidence. It appears as the *οὐρο βόρος*, the tail-eater, in the Codex Marcianus (fig. 147), which dates from the tenth or eleventh century, together with the legend: *νεν τὸ πᾶν* (the One, the All)."

The Codex Marcianus Uroboros (11th cent.) carries the legend *ἓν τὸ πᾶν* — "the One, the All." This is the alchemical watchword: the opus begins in unity (the undifferentiated prima materia), differentiates into multiplicity (the opus's stages), and returns to unity (the lapis).

The alchemists repeatedly reiterate "that the *opus* proceeds from the one and leads back to the one, that it is a sort of circle like a dragon biting its own tail." This is why the opus was called *circulare* (circular) or *rota* (the wheel).

---

## The Ouroboros as Mercurius

The Uroboros IS Mercurius in his earliest, most primal form:

- *Mercurius* stands at the beginning and end of the work
- He IS the prima materia → the caput corvi → the nigredo
- "As dragon he devours himself and as dragon he dies, to rise again as the lapis"
- He is metallic yet liquid, matter yet spirit, cold yet fiery, poison yet healing
- "Symbol uniting all opposites" (CW 12, ¶404)

The Uroboros thus has a double valence:
1. **Beginning**: the self-devouring dragon = the chaos of the prima materia = the nigredo = the starting undifferentiation
2. **End**: the self-completing circle = the opus returned to unity = the lapis = the Self

The same image means both the problem and the solution — because in alchemy they are the same substance at different stages of development.

## CW 5: Uroboros in Myth and Transformation

In *Symbols of Transformation*, the Uroboros appears in connection with:
- The eternal cycle of creation-destruction
- The dragon-mother who both creates and threatens to swallow the hero
- The undifferentiated state of the unconscious before the hero's emergence
- Ancient Near Eastern and Gnostic cosmologies of the eternal return

## Key Meanings

| Context | Meaning |
|---------|---------|
| Beginning of opus | Prima materia, nigredo, chaos |
| Throughout opus | The *circulatio*, the rotating wheel of transformation |
| End of opus | The lapis, the completed Self, *ἓν τὸ πᾶν* |
| Psychologically | The unconscious that precedes and follows consciousness; totality |
| Cosmologically | Eternal return, creation-destruction, the All |

## Dream Context

Ouroboros dreams signal:
- A major transformation beginning or completing
- The return to fundamentals — what has been split must be re-unified
- The Self becoming accessible — wholeness is near or has been achieved
- A self-sustaining psychic process that requires no external input
- Totality without rigidity: the circle that moves
