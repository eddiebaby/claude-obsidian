# Inferior Function (Symbol)

## Core Interpretation

The undeveloped (inferior) psychic function contains the highest value, though it is experienced as the greatest handicap. In Augustine, feeling was inferior yet held the key to spiritual transformation. The paradox: what appears as weakness is actually the gateway to the deepest truth. The inferior function must be liberated through sacrifice of the superior function.

## Key Meanings

- Undeveloped potential
- Contains highest value
- Experienced as weakness
- Gateway to truth
- Handicap disguised
- Treasure hidden

## Psychological Meaning

## Clinical Illustration (von Franz, via Qwarnström 2026)

Left uncultivated, the inferior function doesn't just sit dormant — it becomes "archaic," "un-adapted," prone to erupting as compulsion, mood, or resentment rather than a usable capacity. Fictional worked example: Shakespeare's **Timon of Athens**, whose dominant Extraverted Feeling (boundless, undiscriminating generosity) leaves Introverted Thinking so undeveloped that he cannot judge his flatterers' self-interest — the discarded function returns, personified, as Apemantus the cynic, whom Timon silences rather than heeds. When the imbalance finally breaks, the inferior function doesn't integrate gracefully; it erupts as [[Enantiodromia|enantiodromia]] — Timon's love flips wholesale into curses on "the gods, mankind, and life itself." The healthy alternative von Franz names is a **"mixtum compositum"**: a genuinely uncomfortable transitional state where the dominant function cedes real ground rather than either ignoring the inferior one or (per [[Superior Function]]) trying to seize and administer it.
