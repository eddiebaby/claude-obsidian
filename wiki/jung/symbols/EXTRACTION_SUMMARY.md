## Amplification Method: Jung's Technique

For each symbol, Jung demonstrates how to:

1. **Identify core psychological meaning** (e.g., horse = libido)
2. **Trace across cultures & epochs** (Germanic → Greek → Hindu → Christian)
3. **Show convergence** — when independent traditions use the same symbol for the same meaning, you've found an archetype
4. **Extract the compensation** — what conscious attitude does this symbol compensate?
5. **Identify dream context** — when does this symbol appear, and what is the dreamer avoiding?

Example from Horse:
- Germanic: Sleipnir carries consciousness between worlds → libido is the vehicle
- Vedic: sacrificial horse contains the whole cosmos → libido holds unlimited potential
- Classical: centaur as wind-god → horse is speed, invisible force
- Christian: devil rides horses (nightmares) → libido can possess if unintegrated
- **Convergence**: Horse = psychic energy that must be ridden, not repressed

## Key Jung Principles Illustrated

### 1. Symbols Are Polyphonic
- The arrow = wound AND penetration AND masculine power AND repressed desire AND spiritual ecstasy
- Multiple true meanings simultaneously; interpretation depends on the dreamer and context

### 2. The Dream Compensates
- If conscious = rational, separated → dream brings instinct, union, body (Horse)
- If conscious = passive, contained → dream brings sharp clarity, penetration, wound (Arrow)
- If conscious = undifferentiated → dream brings discrimination, separation (Sword)

### 3. Collective Unconscious Reality
- The fact that unconnected cultures produce identical symbolism proves something objective is present
- Not culture borrowing but direct access to collective patterns (archetypes)

### 4. Integration Not Conquest
- "The hero has much in common with the dragon he fights... Man and dragon might be a pair of brothers"
- The goal is not to slay and move on but to integrate, transform, combine forces
- Libido doesn't get "defeated"; it gets redirected toward consciousness

### 5. The Psyche Knows More Than Consciousness
- Dreams often know the answer before waking mind formulates the question
- The dream is ahead of consciousness; trust it over your opinions

## What Jung Emphasizes Throughout

1. **Symbols bridge conscious and unconscious** — they are the language the psyche uses to communicate across the threshold
2. **Collective unconscious is objective** — not personal fantasy but shared human inheritance
3. **The psyche has wisdom** — it produces symbols to guide development, often ahead of conscious awareness
4. **Integration is the goal** — not victory over the unconscious but union with it
5. **The dream always tells the truth** — it cannot lie; only consciousness lies to itself
