# Prima Materia

**Prima materia** ("first matter") is the base substance of the alchemical *opus* — the raw, undifferentiated starting material that the alchemist receives and must transform into the *lapis philosophorum*. It is simultaneously the most debased thing in the world and the hidden vessel of the highest value. Its paradox is central to alchemy's psychological meaning.

Primary CW 13 sources: [[jung-cw13-alchemical-studies]] Essays II–IV (pars. 88, 209, 255, 274).

---

## The Alchemical Definition

The prima materia is the starting point of the *opus alchymicum*: without it, no work can begin. But its nature is notoriously obscure — the alchemists called it "the secret of secrets" and deliberately kept its identity hidden. What is known from the texts:

- **Everywhere yet unrecognized**: "cheap as dirt and can be had everywhere, only nobody knows it" (par. 209)
- **Saturnine**: thrown out into the street, cast on the dunghill, found in filth. Saturn's metal is lead, and the prima materia is the leaden heaviness of the unconscious in its initial state.
- **Paradoxically precious**: "Despise not the ash, for it is the diadem of thy heart" (*Rosarium*, par. 207)
- **Mercurial**: the prime matter is frequently identified with [[Mercurius]] himself — who is at once the *prima materia* (beginning) and the *ultima materia* (the lapis, the end)

In [[Mercurius]] par. 255: as *Aqua mercurialis*, it is also the root-moisture (*humidum radicale*), the primordial water from which the work rises.

---

## The Saturn–Prima Materia Connection

The most important astrological identification of the prima materia is with **Saturn** (par. 274):

- Lead (Saturn's metal) = prima materia for the earlier alchemists
- Saturn = cold, dark, heavy, impure, melancholic
- The Arabic *Turba*: quicksilver = "water of the moon and of Saturn"
- Saturn says: "My spirit is the water that loosens the rigid limbs of my brothers" = the prima materia as Mercurius

Saturn is also the "father and origin of Mercurius" — which makes the prima materia the Saturnine origin of its own transformation agent. The opus begins in lead and ends in gold, but the agent of transformation (Mercurius) was already latent in the lead.

## Psychological Reading

Jung: "The alchemist's perplexity [about the prima materia] reflects his psychic background, which animates the darkness lying before him, so that he discovers in the projection the qualities of the unconscious. The *labor Sophiae* [labor of Sophia/Wisdom] is thus a process of coming to terms with the unconscious, which always sets in when a man is confronted with its darkness." (par. 209)

Psychologically:
- The prima materia = the **Shadow** and the unconscious contents the ego has not yet integrated — the "cheap, everywhere, unrecognized" starting material of inner work
- Its Saturnine character = the heaviness of depression, the "nigredo" state that begins the opus
- Its being "thrown on the dunghill" = how the ego initially treats the least valued parts of itself
- Its simultaneous preciousness = Jung's consistent teaching that the most neglected, disowned content carries the most transformative energy

The paradox "cheap as dirt, yet the philosopher's stone itself" = the paradox of individuation: what needs to be worked is precisely what one has dismissed or rejected.
