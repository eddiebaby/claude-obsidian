# Rex marinus

> **Latin:** *rex* ("king") + *marinus* ("of the sea") = **"King of the Sea."**

---

## Definition

The name given, in the 1593 alchemical compendium *Artis Auriferae*, to the sterile king at the center of the [[Visio Arislei]] (Vision of Arisleus) — the amplificatory template [[Reginald Ajuonuma|Ajuonuma]] (2023) uses to reinterpret the Oedipus myth. Rex marinus rules a barren land where "only like mates with like," so "nothing is begotten and nothing prospers." He seeks the philosopher Arisleus's counsel, is advised to mate his mentally-conceived children Gabricus and Beya, and — after their union kills Gabricus and provokes Rex marinus into banishing Arisleus and Beya — is finally renewed when Beya bears a new son (the *[[filius-regius]]*), resolving his sterility.

## Psychological Reading

Jung reads Rex marinus's sickness as sterility caused by the refusal or inability to unite with genuine difference ("only like mates with like") — the alchemical figure for a psyche too identified with its own conscious content to admit anything contradictory. This is the same one-sidedness Jung describes elsewhere as the general condition of the alchemical sick king: "The more distinctly an idea emerges and the more consciousness gains in clarity, the more monarchic becomes its content, to which everything contradictory has to submit" (1955-1956, para. 471). See [[Kings Renewal Parable]] for the full trope.

## In This Source

[[ajuonuma2023-alchemical-oedipus]] identifies Rex marinus with **Laius**. The evidence marshalled:

- Jungian analyst James Hillman (1987/1991): "He [Laius] could not generate, which was his reason for going to the oracle.... His kingdom was barren already before Oedipus, before the Sphinx" (p.127).
- Euripides records Laius and Jocasta's childless marriage lasting many years — mirroring Rex marinus's barrenness.
- Rex marinus's "only like mates with like" is read against Laius's love for Chrysippus (Laius takes his abductee to Thebes "to replace his wedded wife," per Kerényi 1959) as the mythic root of the sterility.
- Even Laius's one act of "momentary lust" with Jocasta is immediately followed by a wish for the resulting child's death — paralleling Rex marinus's need for outside counsel (Arisleus/the Delphic oracle) before his sterility can be resolved.
- Laius's pilgrimage to Delphi — cut short at the crossroads by Oedipus — is read, via Jung's own phrase for Rex marinus's plea, as "a cry for help from the depths of his unconscious" (Jung 1944, para. 436).
