# Faith in O

A concept [[Eckhard Frick|Frick]] ([[frick2025-spiritual-path-transformation|2025]]) borrows from psychoanalyst [[Wilfred Bion]] to name what Jungian transformation orients toward. **O** is ultimate reality — the Kantian *thing-in-itself*, "the formless infinite," what cannot be known but must be supposed. It is distinct from **K**, knowledge *about* something. Transformation is the movement from grasping-by-concept toward being-transformed-by-contact: **K → O**.

## K versus O

- **K** = kataphatic knowledge — positive, affirmative statements: "God is X," "the Self is Y." The mode of definitions, theories, and the analyst's interpretations.
- **O** = the ultimate reality those statements point at but cannot capture. In explicitly religious terms, O is "the transcendent mystery of God"; in analytic terms, the uncontrollable archetype / the Self that "never manifests itself entirely."

Bion's notation for the transformation, as Frick renders it: **Tα → Tβ = K → O** — the analytic process transforms the raw facts of a session (α) into representation (β), and ultimately transforms *knowing about* into an apophatic *experience of* O.

## Faith in O

Because O cannot be known, contact with it requires **faith** — Bion's technical, non-devotional sense: "the evolution of ultimate reality (O) has issued in objects of which the individual can be aware," and the analyst should cultivate "a positive discipline of eschewing memory and desire" to be available to it. For Frick, "faith in O is crucial for the patient and the analyst" precisely because the goal of the transformation process stays implicit, unconscious, and out of the ego's control.

## Apophatic / Negative Theology

O is approached the way negative (apophatic) theology approaches God — by **negation**, saying only what may *not* be said. Frick's circle diagram joins **A** (apophatic negation) — **O** (ultimate reality) — **K** (knowledge). The mystics Frick names as O's guides are Meister Eckhart and St John of the Cross, whose "dark night" is the suffering of God's felt absence — the transformation of kataphatic K into the apophatic experience of O. Bion ends *Attention and Interpretation* on exactly this note: what is sought "can be found only in the state in which there is NO memory, desire, understanding."

## Relation to Jung

Frick maps O onto the Jungian frame:
- **O ↔ the Self / the uncontrollable archetype.** The analytic couple orients toward it while working with dreams; it is never fully manifest ([[Self (archetype)]], the [[Individuation|ego-Self axis]]).
- **Faith in O ↔ [[Createdness]].** Jung's reading of the Ignatian "we are created, not creators" ("you don't create, you are created") is, in Frick's words, "Jung's way of expressing Bion's faith in O" — the humble acceptance of the unconscious's priority that creativity, and transformation, require.
- The two poles of transformation: an **active** component (the ego's agency, e.g. the dream-ego's rising agency in a [[Structural Dream Analysis|dream series]]) and a **passive/receptive** one (being *seized*, *ergriffen*, by the spirit). Faith in O is the receptive pole made conscious.
