# Lapis exilis

> **Latin:** *lapis* ("stone") + *exilis* ("small, meagre, worthless, insignificant") = **"the stone of no worth"** or "the insignificant stone." Given in full as *lapis exilis, in via eiectus* — "the worthless stone, cast out along the way."

---

## Definition

An alchemical synonym for the [[Lapis-Philosophorum|philosopher's stone]] that names it by what it looks like *before* recognition rather than by its ultimate value — the overlooked, discarded fragment that turns out to be the very thing needed to complete the work. [[jung-cw14-mysterium-coniunctionis]] treats it directly (§3, "Sulphur," paras. 147-153): "The *lapis exilis, in via eiectus* — the insignificant, rejected fragment — is the piece needed to round consciousness out into wholeness (the *homo totus* / Chên-yên, the 'true man')." The name belongs to the same paradox-of-value family as the lapis's other self-deprecating epithets — cheap as dirt, found in filth (see [[In-Stercore-Invenitur]]), yet identical with the goal of the entire opus.

## Psychological Reading

The *lapis exilis* names the specific psychological trap of over-valuing what already looks valuable and dismissing what looks worthless. Jung's point in CW 14: the shadow material that "opposes and compensates the conscious, positive figure" is exactly what consciousness discards as insignificant — yet it is the missing piece, not an obstacle to it. Wholeness is rounded out not by adding more of what the ego already prizes but by recovering what was "cast out along the way" (*in via eiectus*) as beneath notice.

## In This Source

[[ajuonuma2023-alchemical-oedipus]] applies the term to **Oedipus** via Derrida's *[[Pharmakon|pharmakon]]*: the alchemists' paradox of "the stone that is no stone" is read against Oedipus's own double status — expelled, blinded, cast out of Thebes as valueless (a *pharmakos*, scapegoat), yet becoming, at Colonus, "other-than-man" and a source of blessing to the city that receives him. The paper also notes the *homo altus* ("lofty man") as a companion synonym Ajuonuma applies to the dying, apotheosized Oedipus — the same lapis-identity approached from the opposite direction (elevation rather than rejection), both converging on the same figure. See [[Lapis-Philosophorum]] for the fuller catalogue of lapis names this paper adds to.
