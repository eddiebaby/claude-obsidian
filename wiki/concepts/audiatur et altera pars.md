# audiatur et altera pars

Latin: "let the other side also be heard." A maxim of Roman law and natural justice — no case should be decided without giving the opposing party a fair hearing. Standing Latin-phrase concept page per vault convention.

## Definition

In its original legal sense, *audiatur et altera pars* is the principle of procedural fairness: a judge must hear both parties before ruling. The other side has a *right to be heard*, and a verdict reached without it is void. The phrase names, more broadly, the discipline of not deciding a contested question by listening to only one voice.

## Context in Jung ("The Transcendent Function")

Jung imports the legal maxim into the psyche as the **procedural precondition** for the [[Transcendent Function|transcendent function]]. The transcendent function arises only when a conscious position and its unconscious counter-position are held in genuine tension until a living third emerges (see [[tertium non datur]]). For that to happen, consciousness must give the unconscious position a *real hearing* — it must let "the other side" state its case fully, rather than censoring, overruling, or explaining it away.

This is the ethical and methodological hinge of the whole essay. The ego's habitual move is to sit as sole judge and dismiss the unconscious counter-position out of hand; Jung's requirement is the opposite — the conscious mind must suspend judgment and let the unconscious content argue for itself (through fantasy, image, affect, the dream) with the same standing a defendant is owed in court. Only after *both parties are heard* can the suspension between them do its transformative work. Where [[tertium non datur]] names the logical impossibility the transcendent function overcomes, *audiatur et altera pars* names the attitude that makes overcoming it possible: a fair, adversarial-but-respectful hearing of the opposites.

## Why It Matters

- It is the courtroom metaphor behind [[Active Imagination]]'s core rule — do not argue the fantasy-figure down; let it speak, take its position seriously, and reply as to a real interlocutor.
- It reframes the confrontation of opposites as *due process* rather than conquest: integration, not repression or one-sided victory, is the aim.
- Companion Latin formula to [[tertium non datur]] in the same essay — the two together give the transcendent function its procedure (hear both sides) and its result (a living third that formal logic would forbid).
