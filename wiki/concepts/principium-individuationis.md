# Principium Individuationis

**Principium individuationis** ("principle of individuation") is the Latin philosophical term for the ordering intelligence or force that produces individuals out of undifferentiated matter or soul. Jung borrows it from Schopenhauer and reinvests it: in alchemical psychology, it names the spirit imprisoned in the bottle — the deepest organizing principle of the psyche that drives toward wholeness.

Primary CW 13 source: [[jung-cw13-alchemical-studies]] Essay IV §1, par. 243.

---

## Schopenhauer's Use

In *The World as Will and Representation*, Schopenhauer uses *principium individuationis* (borrowed from medieval Scholasticism via Leibniz) to name the principle of space and time that divides the undivided Will into discrete individual things. Individual existence is therefore *maya* — an appearance produced by the principium. For Schopenhauer and Buddhism, the principium individuationis is itself the source of suffering: as long as one is individuated, one is separate, therefore subject to desire, loss, and death.

---

## Jung's Alchemical Reinvestment

Jung lifts the term out of this pessimistic frame and gives it the opposite valence. In the Grimm fairytale of the Spirit in the Bottle (par. 243):

> The spirit imprisoned under the oak is identified as the **principium individuationis** — "the spiritual quintessence abstracted from" the vegetative spirit of the tree (*spiritus vegetativus*). "The tree would then be the outward and visible sign of the realization of the self."

So where Schopenhauer treats individuation as the fall from the undivided Will into suffering appearances, Jung treats it as the teleological process by which the Self realizes itself in and through a particular person. The *principium individuationis* is not the fall but the goal.

---

## Why It Was Imprisoned

Who imprisoned the *principium individuationis* in the bottle? (par. 244):

- **Schopenhauer and Buddhism**: individuation = the source of all evil; suppression is liberation
- **Christianity**: natural man is tainted with original sin; unconstrained nature produces "a product not essentially different from an animal"

The bottle = the suppression of this principle by culture, morality, and religion. The imprisonment was "by deliberate intervention from outside, and was artfully confined and banished like an evil spirit." Yet the Master who imprisoned it is God himself — making the suppression theologically necessary.

Jung's synthesis: the containment *was* necessary — "without guilt there is no moral consciousness and without awareness of differences no consciousness at all." The imprisonment of the *principium individuationis* created the tension from which consciousness emerged. But the purpose of the opus is now to work with the imprisoned spirit, not leave it locked in the bottle forever.

---

## The Spirit in the Bottle = The Self Awaiting Realization

The *principium individuationis* in the bottle is the **Self** in a state of unconscious containment — not yet realized, not yet integrated. The alchemist's task (and the analysand's task) is not simply to release it (which would produce inflation or possession) but to work with it carefully under the heat of the analytical relationship until transformation occurs.

Key: "The alchemist always sealed the stopper with magic signs and set it for a very long time over the lowest fire, so that 'he who is within may not fly out.'" The *principium individuationis* must be held — not suppressed, not simply released — in order to be transformed. See [[Individuation]].
