# Mental Screen (vs. Visual Screen)

## Definition

[[Alexey Turchin]]'s two-track model of imaginal capacity, developed for [[Active Imagination]] (AIM) practice ([[turchin-active-imagination-lucid-dreaming]]): people experience internally-generated imagery on one of two distinct "screens," and — unlike a skill — the type does not appear to be trainable from one into the other.

## The Two Screens

- **Visual screen** — the imagery seen with eyes closed (classically: hypnagogic images, dreams). If it appeared with eyes open it would overlay perceived reality as a hallucination. More common in young, emotional females in Turchin's informal sample. Associated with relaxation rather than mental activation.
- **Mental screen** — an internal, low-resolution ("about 100×100 pixels"), "pale" representation that does *not* overlap ordinary visual perception, even with eyes open. This is the same faculty used when a reader pictures a scene while still clearly seeing the printed page. More common in older, "rational and mathematical" males in Turchin's sample. Associated with mental *activation*, not relaxation — mental-screen imagery gets brighter under stimulation (e.g., caffeine), not under calm.

Neither type is trainable into the other, per Turchin's observation and consistent with reports that spatial visualization ability resists training even in intensive (e.g., military) programs. **This is not the same distinction as [[Aphantasia]]** — mental-screen-only people are not aphantasic; they simply lack the visual-screen channel while retaining a full, emotionally real imaginal capacity through the mental-screen channel.

## The Framing Principle: "Consciousness Creates a Frame, Unconsciousness Fills It"

Turchin's summary formula for how both screens work: the conscious mind sets an outer frame or command ("imagine a house"), and the imagination — sourced from the unconscious — fills in everything the frame doesn't specify (size, color, height, style), the way a generative image model like DALL-E fills the space between a prompt's explicit constraints. A sufficiently open frame ("what is on the left of the shore?") becomes "a gate into the realm of unconsciousness" — arbitrary content, repressed subpersonalities, or long-standing memories can surface through it. This is functionally the same claim Jung, Hannah, and von Franz make about a genuine active-imagination image being *discovered rather than invented* (see [[Active Imagination]]) — Turchin's version simply describes the underlying cognitive-imagery mechanics rather than the archetypal-psychology framing.

## Validity Check

Turchin proposes a practical test for whether a screen's content is genuinely autonomous rather than willed: **stability against the viewer's own will**. Self-authored images repaint or transform easily on command; images "behind which something is" resist change, feel uncomfortable or impolite to alter, and behave as if a living presence is attached to them.

## Why It Matters

This gives a concrete, testable typology for any practice — active imagination, guided visualization, an app-based dream-figure dialogue feature — that assumes visual imagery as the default channel. Screening for screen-type before promising "vivid" results avoids the disappointment Turchin observed in mental-screen participants chasing bright visuals they structurally cannot get.

## Connections

- Distinguished from [[Aphantasia]] (true absence of imagery) — mental-screen-only is not aphantasia.
- Determines whether [[Active Imagination]] can function as an LD-equivalent substitute for a given person (see [[Lucid Dreaming]]).
