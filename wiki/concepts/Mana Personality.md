# Mana Personality

The Mana Personality is Jung's term for the specific inflation that arises after a person has successfully worked through their shadow and dissolved their anima/animus projections — but then *identifies* with the enlarged state that results, rather than recognizing it as a temporary threshold experience.

Primary source: [[jung-cw7-two-essays]], Essay 2, Part II, "The Mana Personality" (CW 7, paras 374-406). This is the essay's culminating concept.

---

## Background: What "Mana" Means

*Mana* is a Melanesian and Polynesian concept Jung borrowed to denote magical efficacy — a numinous power or force that attaches to objects, persons, or actions and makes them effective beyond normal means. A person with mana commands the room. Their words carry weight beyond their content. Others orient around them.

In Jungian terms, mana is the quality of an activated archetype: the sense of speaking from depth, of accessing a power larger than oneself, of carrying wisdom or healing force that others recognize.

---

## The Sequence: How the Mana Personality Arises

The Mana Personality arises at a specific moment in the individuation process:

1. **Initial state**: The person is identified with their [[Persona]] — living the social mask, unconscious of their deeper contents.

2. **Shadow integration**: Through analysis or life crisis, the person confronts their [[Shadow]] — the inferior, rejected, morally uncomfortable aspects of their personality. They own them. This is genuine psychological work.

3. **Anima/animus work**: The person withdraws their [[Anima and Animus|anima/animus]] projections. They stop unconsciously projecting their contrasexual inner figure onto real people. They gain a new quality of presence, clarity, and inner stability.

4. **The threshold**: At this point, the person has genuinely grown. They have more energy, more perspective, less reactivity. Others sense this. The person themselves feels the change.

5. **The inflation**: Instead of recognizing this enlarged state as the result of a *relationship* established with unconscious contents, the person identifies with it. They feel they *are* the Wise Old Man, the Great Healer, the Enlightened Teacher, the Powerful Shaman — rather than a person who has made contact with those archetypal forces.

This is the Mana Personality inflation.

---

## Clinical and Behavioral Signs

The Mana Personality shows in specific, recognizable patterns:

**In analysts and therapists**: The patient-turned-analyst who cannot be with anyone without analyzing them. Who experiences others primarily as problems to solve or students to teach. Who has lost the ability to receive care, wisdom, or challenge from others.

**In spiritual practitioners**: The meditator or seeker who, having had a genuine opening experience, begins to see themselves as having arrived. Who teaches from a position of permanent superiority. Who is subtly (or not so subtly) unavailable for honest feedback.

**In leaders and intellectuals**: The person whose genuine intellectual or moral development has been short-circuited by the applause it attracted. Who now performs wisdom rather than seeking it.

**Common core**: *The identification with an archetypal role rather than the maintenance of a human relationship with archetypal forces.*

## The Specific Archetypes of the Mana Personality

Jung identifies two primary forms:

### In Men: Identification with the Wise Old Man

The Wise Old Man (Senex, Magus, Teacher, Prophet, Healer) is the archetype that lies beyond the anima in men. After successfully integrating the shadow and withdrawing anima projections, a man may encounter the Wise Old Man as an inner figure — and then mistake himself for that figure.

Signs: grandiosity about one's own understanding; inability to be taught; a subtle contempt for those who have not "done the work"; oracular pronouncements delivered from an assumed position of superior depth.

### In Women: Identification with the Great Mother

The Great Mother (the Nurturing Source, the Healer, the Wise Woman, the Terrible Mother) is the archetype that lies beyond the animus in women. After integrating shadow and withdrawing animus projections, a woman may identify with this overwhelming archetypal force.

Signs: compulsive nurturing that annihilates boundaries; the inability to allow others to suffer (or to learn through their suffering); a possessive quality to care; the projection of need onto others who have not asked for help.

## Why the Mana Personality Traps

The trap is subtle because the material basis is real. The person who has worked through shadow and anima genuinely is more psychologically developed, more present, less reactive, more insightful than before. Others genuinely do experience them as carrying more weight. The mana is not entirely illusory.

The error is about attribution. The mana does not *belong* to the ego. It belongs to the [[Self (archetype)]] — the deeper center that transcends both the ego and the anima/animus. The ego's relationship with the Self produces mana; identification with the Self produces inflation.

Jung: the Wise Old Man and the Great Mother are not endpoints. They are the threshold of the Self. Stopping at the threshold and claiming to be the gatekeeper is the Mana Personality error.

---

## The Resolution: The Self

The resolution of the Mana Personality inflation is the encounter with the [[Self (archetype)]] as something genuinely other than the ego — not something the ego has *become* but something the ego *faces*. 

The Self archetype appears as:
- A numinous center that does not coincide with the ego
- An ordering principle that has its own purposes
- Something that humbles precisely because it is larger than anything the ego can claim

This is why the appearance of [[Mandala Symbolism]] in dreams and fantasies marks a transition from the Mana Personality inflation: the mandala is the Self, and the Self is not centered on the ego.

When the inflation resolves, the person discovers they can relate to wisdom, healing, or power without claiming to *be* them. They can speak from depth when depth is present and be ordinary when depth is absent. The capacity for genuine humility — not false modesty but accurate self-assessment — returns.

---

## The Mana Personality and the Persona

An interesting structural parallel: the [[Persona]] is the inflation of the outer-facing surface; the Mana Personality is the inflation of the inner encounter. Both are cases of identification with a partial function rather than the whole person. Both are resolved by deepening: from persona to shadow/anima, from anima to Self.

The sequence maps the whole course of individuation in CW 7:

1. **Persona inflation** → dissolution of persona-identification → encounter with shadow
2. **Shadow integration** → encounter with anima/animus
3. **Anima/animus integration** → encounter with Mana Personality
4. **Mana Personality resolution** → encounter with the Self

---

## Relation to Inflation More Generally

Psychic inflation — the ego swelling beyond its proper size — can occur at multiple points in the individuation process. The Mana Personality is its specific form at the *advanced* stage: it affects people who have done substantial inner work, not beginners. This makes it particularly treacherous, because the standard defenses against inflation ("I haven't really done the deep work yet") are no longer available.

See [[Ego]] for the general dynamics of ego inflation vs. Self.
