# Ten Core Qualities of Dreams

## Definition

[[Ole Vedfelt|Vedfelt's]] list of ten traits that dreams reliably display, each **cross-validated from two directions** — a psychotherapeutic tradition and a neuroscience finding both point at it. The list is the integrative payload of his [[Cybernetic Dream Theory|cybernetic model]]: it is meant to be the common ground the warring dream schools can actually agree on, because each quality is where a school's clinical insight and a brain-research result coincide.

## The Ten

1. **Dreams deal with matters important to us.** REM-deprivation produces dysfunction; dreams cluster around the dreamer's "intensity of overriding concerns" (Domhoff), central conflicts, trauma, and life-stage preoccupations. Cybernetically, "importance" = the system moving to a higher level of self-organization; positive feedback on *resources* is therefore a prime dreamwork lever.
2. **Dreams symbolize.** They render experience as metaphor / "experiential gestalts" (Lakoff & Johnson). Symbol-comprehension is tied to the imaginative brain (mPFC/vmPFC, right-hemisphere "feeling of knowing"). Metaphoric thinking is a *learnable skill* trained by dreamwork (the Dorothy case).
3. **Dreams personify.** They process information too vast for verbal consciousness by casting it as characters — dream figures show facial expression, prosody, and "theory of mind" as complex as waking life (Nielsen; McNamara). Personified subsystems form from early relationships and stay active in waking life.
4. **Dreams are trial runs in a safe place.** Jung's "dream as classical drama … anticipatory combination of probabilities." Uncoupled from motor action, the dream is a safe simulation space — a "stage play with characters" (Rumelhart), building mental models and evaluating outcomes. (See [[Dream Dramatic Structure]].)
5. **Dreams are online to unconscious intelligence.** For ~25% of waking life attention is inward ("default network"); dreaming and dreamwork "go online" to the unconscious intelligence that creates the dream. Altered/imaginative states accessed via free association, drawing, role-play, body-work feed the process.
6. **Dreams are pattern recognition.** The psyche is an open context-sensitive system using "goodness-of-fit" matching. In dreamwork you match dream patterns against aspects of the dreamer's life (past/present/future) to find the best fit — *as opposed to fixed interpretations*. Different schools emphasize different contexts (Freud: childhood; Jung: future potentials; Hall: current behaviour).
7. **Dreams are high-level communication.** They proactively communicate developmental possibilities and correct one-sided or unhealthy attitudes — encouraging/reassuring dreams (reward system) and warning/corrective dreams (amygdala). Dreams near sessions comment on the therapeutic process, including transference (the Anders case).
8. **Dreams are condensed information.** Freud's "contamination," Jung's "stenographic language," Calvin Hall's compression: the dreaming brain is hyperassociative (vmPFC "privy to virtually any activity in body and mind"). Condensation is a *creative synthesis* (Koestler's "bisociation") that must be **unfolded** in a creative process.
9. **Dreams are experiences of wholeness.** They combine all experiential modalities (visual, bodily, motor, emotional, cognitive) — "ultimate consciousness" arises from numerous brain sites at once (Damasio's "global workspace"). Vedfelt's method attends equally to all modalities: the [[Supramodal Space]].
10. **Dreams are psychological energy landscapes.** Life's competing meaningful themes form an "energy landscape" of sharp peaks (dominating, tightly-packed patterns) and low hills (flexible patterns). "Big Dreams" (Jung) are intense peaks tied to major transitions; between them lie many "soft hill" preparatory dreams. Dreamwork seeks "optimal harmony" in this landscape.

## Why It Matters

The list functions as a **checklist for complete dreamwork**: a dream tool or practice that only asks "what does this symbol mean" (quality 2) misses personification, resources/energy-peaks, prospective communication, and wholeness across modalities. Vedfelt's recurring warning — the schools over-focus on *problems* — makes qualities 1, 5, and 10 (importance, unconscious intelligence, energy landscapes) the corrective: surface the dream's **resources**, not only its conflicts, or the dreamer's motivation collapses.
