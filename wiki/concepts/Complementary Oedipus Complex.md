# Complementary Oedipus Complex

> **One-line definition:** George Devereux's (1953) term for a parent's own filicidal fantasies and hostile behaviours toward a child — the parent-side mirror of Freud's Oedipus complex, which the child's complex may *constellate in response to*, rather than the reverse. Psychoanalyst J.M. Ross (1982) names the paternal version the **"Laius complex."**

---

## Origin

Coined by [[George Devereux|George Devereux]] in "Why Oedipus Killed Laius — A Note on the Complementary Oedipus Complex in Greek Drama" (*International Journal of Psycho-Analysis*, 34, 1953, 132-141). The core reframe: Freud's Oedipus complex describes the *child's* incestuous/patricidal wishes toward the parents. Devereux points out that in the myth itself, the causal sequence runs the other way first — **Laius seeks to kill the infant Oedipus before Oedipus does anything at all.** The parent's fantasy and action precede and provoke the child's.

Devereux's diagnosis of Laius: he "appears to have retained throughout life a propensity for unconsidered violence" (1953, p.134). But Devereux locates the deeper root of Laius's culpability not in the attempted infanticide but in his earlier "hybris" — his abduction and rape of Chrysippus, son of King Pelops, during his exile from Thebes. This makes Laius "a violator of good manners" (p.135) independent of, and prior to, any oracle-driven fear of his son.

Psychoanalyst **J.M. Ross** (1982) works the same material from the parental side and names it the **Laius complex** — describing the myth of Oedipus as, equally, "the myth of Laius, the father" and calling it "the forgotten story."

## Why It Matters

Without this complementary lens, the Oedipus myth's opening violence (Laius's exposure of the infant on Cithaeron) reads as an isolated, oracle-forced act. With it, the myth becomes intelligible as a **two-generation causal chain**: Laius's own crime (against Chrysippus) and his dread of dethronement generate the conditions — sterility, filicidal intent, exposure — that produce the adult Oedipus who unknowingly kills him. Literary theorist Silke-Maria Weineck (2010) argues that psychoanalytic culture since Freud has systematically "overidentif[ied] with the filial at the expense of the paternal" — i.e., has read Oedipus and ignored Laius. Devereux's and Ross's terms are the technical correction to that bias.

## In This Source

[[ajuonuma2023-alchemical-oedipus]] uses the complementary Oedipus complex / Laius complex as the psychoanalytic warrant for reinserting Laius's backstory into the myth before applying Jung's alchemical amplification. Devereux's evidence is also load-bearing for the paper's separate claim that Laius and Oedipus are **consubstantial** (both prone to violence, both marked by names denoting difficulty walking, both implicated in the same "dubious love-affair" pattern — Laius with Chrysippus, Oedipus with Jocasta).

## Relationship to Jung

Devereux and Ross are psychoanalytic (broadly Freudian-adjacent) theorists, not Jungians — but their complementary-complex reframing is what makes Ajuonuma's subsequent *Jungian* move possible: only once Laius is restored as a full causal agent can his corruption be read, via the alchemical [[Kings Renewal Parable]], as the *prima materia* from which Oedipus's renewal grows. The complementary Oedipus complex is thus the psychoanalytic precondition for the paper's alchemical-Jungian thesis, not itself a Jungian concept.
