### 2026-10-13 sweep: S&P 500 sleeve + macro (web-sourced; prices are 10/13/26 closes from stockanalysis.com / Yahoo unless noted; not terminal-checked; research only, no orders)

**Macro:** There has been no FOMC meeting since the 9/16 hike: 12-0 to **3.75-4.00%**. The statement itself gives no explicit forward guidance. The "another hike" signal comes from the SEP median of **4.1% for end-2026** (one more 25bp) and 4.1% for 2027, up from 3.8% and 3.6% in June. The minutes, released 10/7, say "most participants" judged another hike by year-end likely appropriate. They name crude and refined-fuel prices, pushed up by geopolitical events, as the risk that could broaden inflation, and staff called small-business credit "somewhat tight." The data since then cut both ways:
- *Weaker labor:* Sept payrolls **+29k** vs ~90k expected, unemployment 4.2%, Jul+Aug revised down 60k, average hourly earnings +3.0% y/y (BLS, 10/2).
- *Energy-led headline inflation:* Sept CPI is due **10/14**. Consensus is 3.7% y/y headline and ~2.5% core (unverified). WTI ~$94 and Brent ~$107 on 10/12 (unverified).

**Next FOMC is 10/27-28 (no SEP).** Futures price a hike there at ~17-19% (CME FedWatch via secondary sources, unverified), down from 42.5% on 9/11 and 22.1% on 10/2. A 12/8-9 hike is priced at ~75% (Kalshi/Polymarket average, unverified).

*Rates:* The 10y went from 4.96% (9/21) to **5.21% (10/13)**, +25bp in three weeks. Early October saw ~5.35% intraday. Sources call that a "19-year high," but one says "highest since 2002". These conflict, so read it as since-2007 (unverified). The 2y is at 4.79% (Treasury).

*Equities:*
- **SPX 7,805 (10/13): +0.5% since 9/21, +14.0% YTD** (vs 6,845.50 on 12/31/25, FRED).
- **R2000 2,783 (10/13): −3.2% since 9/21, +12.1% YTD** (vs 2,481.91). It is −9.3% from the 8/14 record close of 3,068 (unverified) and ~0.8% above correction territory (2,761.6). The R2000 has lost the YTD lead over SPX it held in July (+21% then).

**R2000 interest-expense trap: tighter, not looser.** I found no new sell-side update to the 31% interest/EBITDA figure. The latest is still ~31% (Apollo/Bloomberg via Benzinga). The floating-rate share of debt is ~30-32% for the R2000 vs ~6-7% for SPX (unverified). But every input moved the wrong way:
- the long end is +25bp;
- a December hike is ~75% priced (BofA rule of thumb: −2% R2000 operating earnings per 25bp);
- the minutes flag tight small-business credit.

The only thing pulling hike odds down is labor weakness. That is a growth scare, not a tailwind. The sleeve rule stands: catalyst-driven names only, never rate-beta. Nothing in this sweep argues for adding rate-sensitive small-cap exposure before CPI and the FOMC.

Per call commentary (secondary) that is down ~36-37% y/y. RPO was $22.16B, +8% y/y: the first single-digit growth since FY23, and *down* sequentially from $22.27B.
