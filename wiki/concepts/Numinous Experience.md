# Numinous Experience

The phenomenology of overwhelming sacred encounter: the direct, involuntary experience of a reality felt as utterly other, profoundly significant, both terrifying and fascinating. The term "numinous" originates with Rudolf Otto (1917); its phenomenological groundwork was laid by William James (1902). Jung adopted both traditions and made numinosity a defining property of archetypes.

## Three Thinkers, One Territory

### William James (1902): The Four Marks of Mystical Experience

James identified four criteria that define genuine mystical states (Lectures XIII-XIV of *Varieties*). These are the foundational empirical description of the territory:

1. **Ineffability**: The state defies expression in language. It is apprehended directly or not at all; it cannot be transferred to one who has not had it. "Its quality must be directly experienced; it cannot be imparted or transferred to others."

2. **Noetic quality**: Despite being ineffable, the state carries the felt quality of knowledge: insight, illumination, revelation. It is not merely an emotion; it feels like the grasping of something real and important.

3. **Transiency**: The state does not last. Minutes to a few hours, then it fades. Memory is imperfect, but when the state recurs it is recognized, and the conditions that favor it can sometimes be cultivated.

4. **Passivity**: The mystic's will is in abeyance. Even when the state has been prepared by deliberate practice, when it arrives the person feels grasped, not grasping. A superior power seems to take hold.

These four marks constitute the minimal empirical definition. They are taxonomically neutral: they apply across traditions (Christian, Islamic, Buddhist, Hindu, Neoplatonist, shamanic) and to spontaneous secular experiences as well as formal religious ones.

### Rudolf Otto (1917): The Numinosum

Otto introduced the term "numinous" in *Das Heilige* (The Idea of the Holy) to name the non-rational, non-moral core of religious experience. His analysis:

**Mysterium tremendum et fascinans**: The numinous is a mystery that simultaneously terrifies and fascinates.

- **Tremendum** (trembling): the element of awe, dread, majesty, overpoweringness. The numinous object is perceived as utterly beyond the subject's control or comprehension. The phenomenological residue in more familiar experience: the "creepy," the "uncanny," the sense of the presence of something vast.

- **Fascinans** (fascination): the element of attraction, compulsion, enchantment. The numinous simultaneously repels (tremendum) and draws irresistibly. The person cannot look away.

- **Mysterium** (mystery): the wholly other. The numinous is not merely unknown; it is categorically different, beyond the ordinary categories of experience. "The 'wholly other': that which is quite beyond the sphere of the usual, the intelligible, and the familiar, which therefore falls quite outside the limits of the 'canny.'"

Otto also identifies **majestas** (absolute overpoweringness) and **energicum** (urgency, vitality, will, force) as sub-elements of the tremendum. The combination makes the numinous experience distinctively charged.

### Jung: Religion *Is* the Observation of the Numinosum (CW 11, para 6)

In the opening pages of the 1937 Terry Lectures ("Psychology and Religion," CW 11), Jung does something more radical than apply Otto's vocabulary to archetypes — he redefines religion itself in Otto's terms, making the numinosum not one feature of religious life among others but its defining mark:

> "Religion... appears to me to be a peculiar attitude of mind which could be formulated in accordance with the original use of the word *religio*, which means a careful and scrupulous observation of what Rudolf Otto aptly termed the *numinosum*, that is, a dynamic existence or effect, not caused by an arbitrary act of will. On the contrary, it seizes and controls the human subject, who is always rather its victim than its creator." (para 6)

This is the methodological hinge for everything Jung writes about religion afterward, including *Answer to Job*. Religion is not assent to doctrine; it is the disciplined, careful *observation* of an autonomous psychic seizure. The believer does not produce the numinous experience — it produces him. This passivity clause (echoing James's "passivity" mark above) is doing real work for Jung: it is what allows him to treat religious experience as clinical data rather than metaphysical claim. See [[jung-cw11-psychology-religion]] for the full lecture-series argument this definition opens, including the extended dream-series case study (the "Church dream," the quaternity dreams, and the World Clock vision) that Jung offers as direct empirical evidence of the numinosum erupting from the unconscious independent of the dreamer's conscious belief.

### Jung: Numinosity of the Archetypes

Jung took both James and Otto and applied their descriptions to the activity of archetypes. For Jung, archetypes are numinous by definition: when an archetypal content erupts into consciousness, it carries the quality of the numinous. The ego is overwhelmed (tremendum), fascinated (fascinans), and feels itself to be in contact with something wholly other than itself (mysterium).

**Key Jungian formulation**: The collective unconscious is the psychological carrier of numinosity. It is not God in a metaphysical sense, but it functions identically to James's "More" and Otto's numinosum in experience: it is wider than the ego, impersonal, felt as other, transformative when contacted.

**The archetype-numinosum identification**: An archetype that activates strongly in the psyche (in a dream, in active imagination, in an overwhelming emotion) produces numinous experience. The intensity of the numinosity is a rough measure of the archetype's activation. This is why:
- The [[Self (archetype)]] is the most numinous archetype (it is the totality of the psyche, the "God-image" in psychological terms)
- Encounters with the Shadow, Anima/Animus, or Great Mother in dreams can have a numinous quality that distinguishes them from ordinary dream material
- The individuation process regularly involves numinous experiences at its turning points

**Inflation as numinous pathology**: When the ego identifies with an activated archetype rather than encountering it as other, the result is inflation. The person experiences grandiosity or possession rather than transformation. The numinous is not safe; it requires the ego to remain related but not identified.

## The Phenomenological Sequence

Numinous experience typically follows a pattern (synthesized across James, Otto, and Jung):

1. **Eruption**: Something breaks through the ordinary threshold of consciousness. It is not summoned voluntarily; it arrives.
2. **Awe and terror**: The ego registers the overwhelming otherness of what has appeared. Tremendum.
3. **Fascination**: Simultaneous pull. The person cannot disengage. Fascinans.
4. **Noetic flash**: A sense of meaning, revelation, or insight accompanies the state. It feels like understanding something essential.
5. **Passivity**: The will is suspended. The person is a recipient.
6. **Fading**: The state passes. Memory of it is imperfect but recognizable on recurrence.
7. **Transformation**: In genuine numinous experience, something in the personality is permanently altered. This is what James calls conversion and Jung calls the movement of individuation.

> [!note] Vocabulary note
> James (1902) did not use the word "numinous" — it is Otto's term from 1917. James describes the same territory via the four marks and the "More." When reading Jung on the numinous, he is drawing on both: James's empirical phenomenology of mystical states, and Otto's more differentiated vocabulary of tremendum/fascinans/mysterium. The concepts are compatible, not competing.
