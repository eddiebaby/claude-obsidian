# Leviathan

The great sea monster of Jewish tradition; in Jung's *Aion*, the supreme image of the primordial unconscious in its most terrible and most nourishing form simultaneously. Leviathan is the *pharmakon athanasias* — the medicine of immortality — whose flesh constitutes the eucharistic food of the end times.

Source: [[edinger-aion-lectures]] (Lecture 13, pp. 95-96); [[jung-cw9ii-aion]] (par. 178).

---

## Biblical Appearance

Leviathan appears alongside Behemoth in Job 40-41 — Yahweh's own exhibit of his power, produced after making his entrance from the whirlwind:

**Behemoth** (Job 40:15-19, ASV): "Behold now, behemoth, which I made as well as thee; he eateth grass as an ox... his strength is in his loins... He moveth his tail like a cedar... He is the chief of the ways of God."

**Leviathan** (Job 41:19, ASV): "Canst thou draw out leviathan with a fish-hook? Or press down his tongue with a cord?... Will he make a covenant with thee?... That thou shouldst take him for a servant?... Wilt thou play with him as with a bird?... Behold, the hope of him is in vain."

These are theriomorphic aspects of Yahweh — the God-image displaying itself in its most grandiose, terrifying, non-human forms. William Blake painted both: Behemoth as a grass-eating mammal, Leviathan as the monster of the deep (CW 13, plate B2). Blake perceived their archetypal reality directly.

God made both "as well as" Job — they are co-creatures, not mere animals. Their enormity is the measure of the God who made them.

## The Pharmakon Athanasias

In Jewish legend, Leviathan is the *pharmakon athanasias* — the **medicine of immortality** — the Messianic fish whose flesh will nourish the righteous at the end of days:

> "In Jewish tradition... the *pharmakon athanasias* [the medicine of immortality] is the flesh of Leviathan, the 'Messianic fish'... The Talmud Sanhedrin says that the Messiah 'will not come until a fish is sought for an invalid and cannot be procured.'" (Jung, par. 178; Apocalypse of Baruch adds Behemoth as well)

The messianic banquet: God created a male and female Leviathan. Had they copulated, their offspring would have destroyed the whole world. God therefore slew the female, salted and preserved her flesh for the righteous at the end of days. The male lives on until the final age, when he will be caught and slaughtered, and his flesh served at the banquet (Raphael Patai, *The Messiah Texts*).

---

## Structural Parallel to the Eucharist

The connection is explicit: just as the Mass is the transubstantiated body of Christ (the Messianic fish = Ichthys), the messianic banquet is the flesh of Leviathan. Both are eucharistic — ritual consumption of the divine animal body that transforms the consumer.

| Christian Eucharist | Messianic Banquet |
|---------------------|-------------------|
| Body of Christ (Ichthys) | Flesh of Leviathan |
| Eaten in the Mass now | Eaten at the end of days |
| Piscina: believers as fish in a fish-pond | Righteous eat the fish |
| Baptism into the fish-pond | Admission to the banquet |

The early Christian identification of Christ with the fish (Ichthys) is the present form; Leviathan is the eschatological form. The same archetype, two temporal registers.

## Psychological Significance

Leviathan is the archetype of the primordial unconscious at maximum potency — both maximally dangerous and maximally nourishing.

The pattern:
1. **Full force**: Male + female Leviathan copulating = offspring would destroy the world. The unconscious at full, undifferentiated potency overwhelms consciousness entirely (psychosis, inflation).
2. **The female killed and preserved**: The unconscious content bounded, differentiated, held in suspension. The dangerous energy is not destroyed but contained.
3. **The preserved flesh served at the banquet**: At the end of the process, the dangerous content becomes the highest nourishment. The messianic banquet is the teleiosis of the work.

This is the Tobit three-step (capture → extract → transform) on a cosmic scale. Leviathan is the ultimate version of the [[Book-of-Tobit|healing fish]].

The double valence — cold-blooded, primordial concupiscence (the lowest) simultaneously the eucharistic body of the Messiah (the highest) — is the core paradox of fish symbolism in *Aion*:

> "The image of the fish basically has two meanings. It is an image of the cold-blooded, undifferentiated, primordial, infantile psyche, the original concupiscence. The other side of its meaning, in its identification with Christ and the whole aeon, is as a symbol of the Self. So it is the highest and lowest simultaneously." (Edinger, p. 93)

Leviathan is where these two meanings are held at maximum intensity. The greatest danger and the greatest nourishment in a single image.

---

## Destruction of the God-Image

The wider context in *Aion* par. 178 is the destruction of the God-image and its consequences:

> "The destruction of the God-image is followed by the annulment of the human personality." (par. 170)

Materialism, atheism, the substitutes offered for religion — they repress the fish question into the unconscious and destroy the continuity of historical tradition. The Leviathan/fish symbolism is Jung's answer to what happens when the God-image is destroyed: the primordial psyche (Leviathan, the Messianic fish) remains in the unconscious, unavailable, unintegrated — and without that center, the personality disintegrates.

The task of Jungian analysis is the reconstruction of the God-image in the individual. The fish story in all its forms (Tobit, Ichthys, Leviathan, the fish-pond) is about that reconstruction.

---

> [!key-insight] The highest food is locked in the most dangerous form
> Leviathan could not be allowed to reproduce because full unconscious potency would overwhelm everything. The female must be killed — the psychic content bounded — before its flesh can nourish. The highest transformation product (teleiosis, the messianic banquet) requires the most dangerous starting material (the primordial sea monster). What threatens most, properly contained, nourishes most.
