# Psychological Types

## What It Is

Jung's systematic framework for understanding individual psychological differences, introduced in *Psychological Types* (1921, CW Vol. 6). The typology has two axes:

1. **Attitude** — the general direction of libido movement (introversion vs. extraversion)
2. **Function** — the psychological process that orients perception or judgment (four functions)

The typology was not merely classificatory: Jung developed it to explain why Freud and Adler reached opposite conclusions from the same clinical data. Freud's theory is extraverted (the decisive factor comes from the object — the outer person, the instinct-releasing stimulus); Adler's is introverted (the decisive factor is the subject's drive for superiority). Both are right within their own typological framework. Neither is universally valid.

---

## The Two Attitude Types

### Extraversion

The extravert's libido flows *outward* — toward the object, toward people, toward events in the environment. The object is what determines. The extravert adapts by fitting his inner life to outer conditions.

Biological analogy: high fertility, broad propagation. The extravert relates to many; his normality is social adjustment.

Pathology of excess: neglect of the subjective factor. The body, inner needs, and one's own psychological standpoint become invisible. Normality becomes mere *adjustment* (fitting current conditions) rather than *adaptation* (obedience to universal laws of life).

### Introversion

The introvert's libido moves *inward* — toward the subjective factor, toward the inner world, toward primordial images. The subject's inner response is what determines. The introvert adapts by applying a subjective view *interposed* between himself and the object.

Biological analogy: self-protection, depth over breadth.

Pathology of excess: when the *ego* (not the *Self*) usurps the subjective standpoint, the introvert falls into an unconscious compensatory enslavement to the very object he is trying to dominate. The object becomes terrifying; paranoid safeguards multiply. Typical neurosis: **Psychoasthenia** (extreme sensitivity + chronic exhaustion).

Jung's key point (p. 478): "Self and world are commensurable factors; hence a normal introverted attitude is just as valid, and has as good a right to existence, as a normal extraverted attitude. But if the ego has usurped the claims of the subject, a compensation naturally develops."

Both types are equally valid and equally frequent — distributed randomly across class, sex, and culture.

## The Four Functions

Jung divides psychic activity into two pairs based on how the function operates:

### Rational (Judging) Functions

These functions *evaluate* — they impose order on experience by reference to a criterion.

**Thinking**
Orients by logical inference and conceptual criteria. Asks: "Is it true or false? Does it follow?" The criterion is objective (extraverted thinking) or subjective/primordial-image-based (introverted thinking).

**Feeling**
Orients by value — pleasant/unpleasant, acceptable/repugnant. Feeling is *not* emotion; it is a judging function that evaluates the worth of things. The criterion is objective social value (extraverted feeling) or deep inner value-standards (introverted feeling).

Note: Thinking and Feeling are *antagonistic* functions. They cannot both be primary in the same person, and Feeling cannot serve as the auxiliary to Thinking.

### Irrational (Perceiving) Functions

These functions simply *register* — they do not evaluate; they perceive what is given.

**Sensation**
The function of concrete sensory reality. Registers what *is*, via the sense organs. Extraverted sensation is bound to the physical object as given; introverted sensation registers the subjective intensity of the sensory excitation.

**Intuition**
The function of unconscious perception. Transmits images of *possibilities, relations, and conditions* that sensation cannot detect. Extraverted intuition sniffs out possibilities in the environment; introverted intuition perceives archetypal images and patterns — the "mystical dreamer."

Note: Sensation and Intuition are antagonistic (both perceiving functions). Thinking can pair with either; Feeling can pair with either.

---

## Superior, Auxiliary, and Inferior Functions

Every person develops *one* function to primary (superior) status:
- It is conscious, differentiated, under voluntary control.
- It constitutes the dominant orientation of the personality.
- "The superior function is always the expression of the conscious personality, its aim, its will, and its achievement."

The function *opposite in kind* is correspondingly repressed and remains **inferior**:
- Archaic, autonomous, contaminated by unconscious contents.
- Responsible for compulsive behavior, moods, lapses, resentments.
- "The inferior functions belong to the things that happen to one."

The **auxiliary function** occupies second place — partly conscious, partly differentiated. It must be of opposite kind (rational/irrational) to the superior function but not antagonistic. Valid pairings:

| Superior | Valid Auxiliaries |
|---|---|
| Thinking | Sensation or Intuition |
| Feeling | Sensation or Intuition |
| Sensation | Thinking or Feeling |
| Intuition | Thinking or Feeling |

Classic pairings in practice:
- **Practical intellect**: Thinking + Sensation
- **Speculative intellect**: Thinking + Intuition
- **Artistic intuition**: Intuition + Feeling
- **Philosophical intuition**: Intuition + Thinking

## The Eight Types

Each of the four functions can operate in either attitude, yielding eight distinct types. These are idealized portraits ("Galtonesque family-portraits") — no one is a pure type.

### Extraverted

| Type | Core Orientation | Shadow Side |
|---|---|---|
| **ET (Extraverted Thinking)** | Logic + objective data; formula and law | Repressed feeling erupts as moralizing, sentimentality, resentment |
| **EF (Extraverted Feeling)** | Social value, harmony, fashion | Loses warmth when extreme; hysteria; performativity |
| **ES (Extraverted Sensation)** | Concrete reality is everything; "fullness of actual living" | Judging functions repressed; superstitious morality |
| **EN (Extraverted Intuition)** | Restless pursuit of possibility; entrepreneur/catalyst | No follow-through; abandons situations once potential exhausted |

### Introverted

| Type | Core Orientation | Shadow Side |
|---|---|---|
| **IT (Introverted Thinking)** | Subjective ideas; primordial images; philosophical depth | Ideas become mythological and untrue when severed from reality |
| **IF (Introverted Feeling)** | Deep inner values, unexpressed; "still waters run deep" | Blank/cold exterior; misunderstood; impersonal influence on others |
| **IS (Introverted Sensation)** | Subjective intensity of inner sense experience; aesthetic | Ungrounded; others experience him as odd; unconscious = extraverted intuition |
| **IN (Introverted Intuition)** | Archetypal visions; mystical dreamer or fantastical artist | Incommunicable; "voice crying in the wilderness"; unconscious = extraverted sensation (compulsive, hypochondriacal) |

## Typology and Individuation

The typology is not merely descriptive — it is a map of what must be *integrated* in the individuation process.

1. **The inferior function is the gateway to the unconscious**. The undeveloped function carries the archaic, instinctual, collective contents that the superior function has excluded. Dreams and fantasies present the inferior function in "archaic, animal" form.

2. **Individuation requires confronting the inferior function** — but not directly. Jung's clinical warning: attempting to develop the inferior function directly from the unconscious risks compulsive transference (the analyst becomes the patient's standpoint). Development should go *via the auxiliary function* first.

3. **The Anima/Animus is the fourth function personified**. In [[jung-cw9ii-aion]], Jung establishes that the anima in a man corresponds to his most inferior and undeveloped function — she is how the unconscious speaks to him through the function he least controls.

4. **Type falsification produces neurosis**. When a child is forced (by parental or social pressure) into an attitude that contradicts their natural type, neurosis follows. Therapy requires "a development of that attitude which corresponds with the individual's natural way."

## Relation to Other Concepts

- **[[Shadow]]**: Carries the inferior function's contents — the "dark side" of the dominant type. The introverted thinker's shadow is extraverted feeling.
- **[[Anima and Animus]]**: The fourth (most inferior) function personified. The anima is the man's feeling function (if he is a thinking type), appearing in dreams as a contrasexual figure.
- **[[Active Imagination]]**: The waking method for engaging the inferior function and unconscious contents. The [[Active Imagination|transcendent function]] is the bridge the active imagination creates between the two attitudes.
- **[[Individuation]]**: The individuation journey requires integrating the shadow (inferior-function contents) and the anima/animus (fourth function), moving toward wholeness.
- **[[Libido Transformation]]** (CW 5): The libido "dammed up" by type-rigidity is the energy that presses for transformation via the inferior function.

## Literary Application: One-Sidedness in Shakespeare

[[Sofie Qwarnström|Qwarnström's]] 2026 essay reads *Coriolanus* and *Timon of Athens* as mirror-image case studies of typological [[One-Sidedness|one-sidedness]]: Coriolanus (dominant Introverted Thinking, inferior Extraverted Feeling) starves his own capacity for felt relation exactly as he starves the plebeians he governs; Timon (dominant Extraverted Feeling, inferior Introverted Thinking) floods himself with undiscriminating generosity, having silenced the judging function that would let him see his flatterers clearly. Both endings enact [[Enantiodromia|enantiodromia]] directly. See [[One-Sidedness]] for the full treatment, and the updated [[Superior Function]] / [[Inferior Function]] symbol pages for von Franz's clinical mechanics (the superior function's tendency to "seize" rather than sacrifice its opposite).

## Cross-Reference: Transcendent Function

The **transcendent function** is Jung's term for the psychic function that bridges the gap between conscious (superior-function) orientation and unconscious (inferior-function) contents. It arises when the tension of opposites is held without premature resolution. See [[Active Imagination]] (where it appears as the outcome of the waking method) and [[jung-cw8-structure-dynamics-psyche]] (¶131–193 — the dedicated essay).

CW 6 does not develop the transcendent function as a standalone concept. The text mentions "transcendent" only in passing, in reference to theosophical thinking's habit of inflating experience into "transcendental and world-embracing ideas" — a pathological use, not the technical term.
