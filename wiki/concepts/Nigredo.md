# Nigredo

**Latin**: *nigredo* — "blackness," from *niger* (black).

In alchemical tradition, *nigredo* is the **first stage of the opus alchymicum** — the initial state of dissolution, putrefaction, and blackening that the *prima materia* must undergo before transformation can begin. It is the darkness before the dawn of the work.

---

## The Four Stages of the Opus

Alchemical tradition (particularly in its mature 16th–17th-century form) organized the transformation into four colour-coded stages:

| Stage | Colour | Process |
|-------|--------|---------|
| **Nigredo** | Black | Putrefaction, dissolution, death, prima materia |
| **Albedo** | White | Purification, washing (*ablutio*), moonlight, silver |
| **Citrinitas** | Yellow | Sunrise, transition (sometimes omitted in later texts) |
| **Rubedo** | Red | Completion, lapis philosophorum, sun, gold |

Nigredo is the necessary beginning — without blackening, no whitening; without death, no resurrection.

---

## Symbolism

The nigredo state is represented by:
- **The black crow** (*corvus*) — appears in both the *Verus Hermes* poem and alchemical illustrations
- **Putrefaction** (*putrefactio*) — rotting, dissolution of the original body
- **The black sun** (*sol niger*) — the sun "extinguished" or hidden in matter
- **Death and burial** — the *prima materia* must "die" before it can be transformed
- **The dung-heap** — Mercurius is said to be "found in the dung-heaps"
- **Ialdabaoth** — the lion-headed Gnostic archon = Saturn = child of chaos = initial dark state of Mercurius

---

## Psychological Reading

Jung reads nigredo as the encounter with the **Shadow** — the first, inescapable confrontation with the unconscious. The "blackening" of consciousness is the recognition of one's own darkness.

Key formula (par. 279, CW 13): "**The lowest is the symbol of the highest**" (*Anfang und Ende / Reichen sich die Hände* — "Beginning and end reach each other's hands"). The darkest, most degraded state of Mercurius contains within it the seed of the lapis.

The *Verus Hermes* poem (1620) gives Mercurius's first-person account of nigredo:
> "A weakling babe, a greybeard old, / Surnamed the Dragon: me they hold / In darkest dungeon languishing / That I may be reborn a king... / A fiery sword makes me to smart, / Death gnaws my flesh and bones apart... / To a black crow am I akin, / Such be the wages of all sin. / In deepest dust I lie alone, / O that the Three would make the One!"

This moves from nigredo (black crow, dungeon, death) through the appeal for trinity/unity to the emergence of the "Hero of peace" — i.e., the lapis in its role as healer.

---

## Connection to the Dark Mercurius

The dark Mercurius = the initial nigredo state. He is simultaneously the lowest and the highest — found in sewers and dung-heaps, yet "undoubtedly akin to the godhead" (par. 269). This paradox is inherent to the alchemical understanding: what is most degraded contains the most potent transformation.
