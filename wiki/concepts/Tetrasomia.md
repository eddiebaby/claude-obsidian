# Tetrasomia

**Tetrasomia** (Greek, "four bodies") is the Greek alchemical term for the reduction of a **quaternity of opposites to unity** — the union of four base metals (lead, tin, iron, copper), each carrying a planetary/psychic charge, into the single substance of the opus. Jung uses it as the entry point for tracing the tree symbol's fourfold structure back through Egyptian, Gnostic, and biblical sources to its psychological meaning: the quaternity as **the archetype of a totality image divided into four**.

Source: [[jung-cw13-alchemical-studies]], Essay V, "The Philosophical Tree," §3 (pars. 358–368). Discussed within [[Philosophical-Tree]].

---

## The Astrological Quaternio

The names of the planets indicate two dyads — one benevolent (Jupiter, Venus), one malefic (Saturn, Mars) — a structure that "often constitutes an alchemical quaternity" in itself. Zosimos describes the transformation needed to prepare the tincture: an earth formed from two bodies, watered by a water formed from two natures, until the sun burns the resulting clay into stone, whose burning yields the tincture. **The synthesis depends on unifying a doubled dyad** — the same structure Jung finds in the royal marriage and the cross-cousin marriage.

The fourfold Mercurius (known since antiquity as *quadratus*) is the arcane substance through whose transformation the lapis is produced. The love-magic of Astrampsychos invokes Hermes by his four cardinal forms: ibis (East), dog-headed baboon (West), serpent (North), wolf (South) — Hermes as *all-seeing*, present at every quarter of the heavens.

## The Egyptian Root: The Four Sons of Horus

The fourfold Mercurius/Hermes derives from the **four sons of Horus**, attested as early as the Pyramid Texts (4th–5th dynasties): Hapi (North), Tuamutef (East), Amset (South), Qebhsennuf (West) — guardians of the four Canopic jars, watching over the dismembered body of the dead in the underworld. Horus begot his sons with his mother Isis — an incest motif that "begins far back in Egyptian antiquity" and continues into Christian tradition and late-medieval alchemy. The four sons stand on a lotus before their grandfather Osiris: Mestha (human head), Hapi (ape), Tuamutef (jackal), Qebhsennuf (hawk) — one quarter human, three quarters animal.

The quaternity's function for the dead man was **preservation of wholeness**: four men carry the coffin, four Canopic jars, four sacrificial animals, formulas repeated four times. "The four parts (i.e., the wholeness) of the body" had to be kept intact.

## Ezekiel's Cherubim and Daniel's Beasts

**Ezekiel** (chs. 1, 10): four cherubim, each with the faces of a man, a lion, an ox, and an eagle — structurally identical to the four sons of Horus, one quarter human and three quarters animal. Each cherub has a wheel (later interpreted as *Merkabah*, the chariot); above them, on a crystal firmament, sits the throne of "him who had the appearance of a man." Ezekiel 9:4: the prophet marks a cross on the foreheads of the righteous — **the sign of God, who himself has the attribute of quaternity**; the cross is the mark of his protégés.

**Daniel's vision** (ch. 7): four beasts — lion (with a man's heart given to it), bear, leopard, and a fourth "dreadful and terrible" beast with ten horns. Only the lion retains the positive, human quarter; the rest are beasts of prey — psychologically, "functions that have succumbed to desire, lost their angelic character, and become daemonic." The fourth beast, monstrous and devouring ("shall devour the whole earth"), is read as **the daemonization of the human quarter when a monstrous lust for power dominates** — "a psychological process which can be observed only too often both individually and collectively... It has recurred countless times in the history of mankind."

---

## Christian Continuation

Via Daniel and Enoch, the quaternity of God's sons entered Christian ideology early: the three synoptic gospels plus John, assigned the cherubim's emblems, became "as it were the pillars of Christ's throne"; in the Middle Ages the tetramorph became the Church's riding-animal. Gnostic speculation appropriated the theme fully — the Valentinian "second tetrad" derives Jesus's fourfoldness from (1) the spiritual (Achamoth), (2) the psychic (the world-creator), (3) the body, and (4) the divine saviour.

## KEY: The Cross as Quaternity = Self

Paulinus of Nola: Christ "extended on the four arms of the wood of the cross... reached out to the four quarters of the world, that he might draw together unto life the peoples from every shore." **Par. 364 KEY**: "In the spontaneous symbolism of the unconscious the cross as quaternity refers to **the self, to man's wholeness**." The sign of the cross indicates the healing effect of wholeness, or of becoming whole.

Jung's closing judgment (pars. 367–368): the tetrasomia's long prehistory — reaching back through the Pythagorean *tetraktys* into Egyptian antiquity — shows an **archetype of a totality image divided into four**, producing the same figures, meanings, and values autochthonously wherever it appears. "It is not the task of empirical psychology to speculate about the possible metaphysical significance of this archetype" — only to note that dreams and fantasies generate it over and over, independent of doctrine.

---

## Relation to the Fourfold Quaternio

The tetrasomia (Essay V) and the [[Fourfold-Quaternio]] (*Aion*, Chs. 13–14) are two independent expressions of the same underlying pattern, developed in different books at different times, and they converge on the identical historical material: the four sons of Horus, Ezekiel's cherubim, the four Evangelists. Where *Aion*'s Fourfold Quaternio builds a full vertical structure (Anthropos → Man → Serpent → Lapis → Rotundum) with the ego occupying the mid-position, the tetrasomia in Essay V is used more narrowly — to establish that **the fourfold reduction to unity is itself the archetype of wholeness**, grounding the tree symbol's own quaternity (the four-branched tree, the four rivers of Eden, the four colors of alchemical blossoms).
