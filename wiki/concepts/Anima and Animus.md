# Anima and Animus

The anima (in men) and the animus (in women) are the contrasexual archetypes in Jung's analytical psychology. They personify the unconscious as a whole in its relation to the ego, and they serve as the bridge between the personal ego and the deeper [[Collective Unconscious]].

Source: [[jung-cw9i-archetypes-collective-unconscious]] (CW 9i), Section I: "Concerning the Archetypes, with Special Reference to the Anima Concept." Supplemented by well-established Jungian scholarship.

---

## Basic Definitions

**Anima** (Latin: soul): The feminine inner figure in a man's psyche. Represents his unconscious feminine qualities, his feeling life, his capacity for relatedness and Eros. Historically appears as goddess, muse, femme fatale, witch, wise woman. In negative form: seductiveness, moodiness, irrational depression.

**Animus** (Latin: spirit/mind): The masculine inner figure in a woman's psyche. Represents her unconscious masculine qualities, her logos (principled thinking), her capacity for assertion and independent judgment. Historically appears as hero, father-figure, wise old man, demon-lover. In negative form: opinionated rigidity, destructive criticism, power-drives.

---

## Why Contrasexual?

Jung's argument: each person develops one-sidedly in their psychological gender adaptation, leaving the contrasexual dimension unconscious. In a culture where men are expected to be rational and assertive, their feeling and relational capacities go undeveloped and drop into the unconscious, where they personify as a feminine figure. The converse holds for women in relation to logos and assertion.

This is a psychological claim, not a biological determinism. The specific content of any individual's anima or animus depends heavily on biography, culture, and individual development.

---

## The Anima as Mediator

The anima (and by extension the animus) does not just represent contrasexual traits. It is the primary mediator between ego-consciousness and the collective unconscious. When the unconscious "speaks" to a man - in dreams, moods, intuitions, sudden inspiration - it often does so through an anima figure. This makes the anima both a guide into the depths and a danger: if the man is unconsciously identified with his anima, he loses the stable center of the ego and may be led astray by moods, fantasies, or irrational attractions.

---

## Stages of the Anima (and Animus)

Jung sketched four levels of anima development (from primitive to differentiated):

1. **Eve**: Pure instinct; biological woman; the basic attraction to the feminine
2. **Helen**: The romanticized, aesthetic feminine; collective anima of an era (the "femme fatale" of the fin de siecle)
3. **Mary**: The spiritualized, devotional feminine; love that elevates
4. **Sophia**: Wisdom; the feminine as guide to the deepest psychic truths

Development of the animus follows a parallel fourfold schema from brute force to spiritual mediation.

---

## Von Franz: Four Stages in Detail (Man and His Symbols)

Von Franz gives the most clinically useful elaboration of the four-stage scheme in *Man and His Symbols* (Part 3). Source: [[jung-man-and-his-symbols]].

### Anima: Four Stages

| Stage | Embodiment | Characteristic |
|-------|------------|----------------|
| **Eve** | Biological woman; the mother | Pure instinct and vitality; the primordial feminine |
| **Helen** | The "femme fatale"; the romantic ideal | Collective aesthetic projection; the anima of an era |
| **Mary** | The Virgin; spiritualized Eros | Love that elevates; purified from sexuality without denying feeling |
| **Sapientia** | The Mona Lisa; wisdom | Transcendent guidance; bridge to the Self |

Global equivalents of the developed anima: **Kwan-Yin** (China), **Shakti/Parvati** (India), **Fatima** (Islam) — each a culturally specific form of the fourth-stage anima as guide to spiritual depth.

**Negative anima figures** — the anima at each stage has a shadow face:
- Seductive-destructive: Queen of Night, Sirens, Lorelei, the Siberian "forest woman"
- The **"poison damsel"** motif in world legend: the woman whose embrace kills (related to the anima's capacity to draw the man away from life entirely)

**Anima as mood**: At its most primitive and unrecognized, the anima appears not as a figure but as undifferentiated *mood* — inexplicable depression, suicidal undertow, impotent withdrawal. The man cannot trace these states to any actual event. They are the anima unrecognized.

**Positive function — "radio tuned to the Self"**: When integrated and functioning consciously, the anima is the man's bridge to the unconscious. She filters and transmits the symbolic messages of the deeper psyche that consciousness alone could not generate. The specific example: a 45-year-old psychotherapist dreams of his own church, with a priest and a nun who functions as guide; he is carrying a book of 16 (4×4) ancient pictures. The dream message: the inner feminine mediates the encounter with wholeness, not the rational function.

### Animus: Four Stages

| Stage | Type | Example Figures |
|-------|------|----------------|
| **Physical power** | Brute strength; the athlete | Tarzan; the hero as muscle |
| **Planned action** | The "man of action"; romance-hero | Hemingway, Shelley; the adventurer-artist |
| **The Word** | The bearer of authoritative speech | Lloyd George; the preacher; the orator |
| **Meaning** | The conveyor of spiritual significance | Gandhi; the sage; the wise guide |

**Negative animus** — the shadow face:
- "Obstinate cold hidden sacred conviction": the animus that has never been questioned substitutes rigid opinion for living thought. It speaks in collective absolutes: "one always..." / "you ought to..." / "it's always been done this way..."
- Death-demon: in fairy tales, the animus in its negative form appears as Bluebeard, the devouring husband, the figure who drives his family toward illness or psychic destruction
- Waspish effeminate remarks (in a man): von Franz's note that a man with an unconscious anima can produce cutting, slightly hysterical observations; the negative anima voicing itself through his speech

**Positive animus in creative life**: the integrated animus gives a woman focused creative will — the inner figure who provides structure, initiative, and the capacity to act from conviction rather than compliance. Example: a woman of 45, encouraged by her analyst to draw, discovers unexpected creative capacity — the animus freed from defensive function.

## The Anima and the Kore (Section IV)

In the essay on the Child Archetype (with Kerényi), the Kore (maiden, Persephone) is identified as the anima in its youthful, naive form - the figure encountered as "other" both by the older woman (the Mother) and by the man (for whom she appears as the anima). The Kore thus bridges the Mother Archetype and the anima: the maiden is what the mother was, and the anima is what the man encounters in the outer world and then discovers within.

---

## Projection and Withdrawal

The anima and animus are almost always encountered first through projection onto real persons of the opposite sex. A man's intense and apparently disproportionate fascination with or aversion to a woman is often partly projection of his own unconscious anima. The task of individuation is to withdraw the projection - not by denying the real qualities of the other person, but by recognizing the inner figure that was activated.

Withdrawal of projection does not kill the relationship; it changes its character from unconscious compulsion to conscious relatedness.

## Aion's Treatment: The Syzygy (CW 9ii Ch III, paras 20-42, directly read)

Aion's Chapter III ("The Syzygy: Anima and Animus") is the most precise treatment Jung published in a single chapter. Key points grounded in direct reading:

**The projection-making factor**: "What, then, is this projection-making factor? The East calls it the 'Spinning Woman' - Maya, who creates illusion by her dancing." The hints from the East "point unmistakably to the mother" - the mother-imago and the anima are structurally related but not identical. (para 20)

**Anima is NOT the mother**: "Nor is she [the anima] a substitute figure for the mother. On the contrary, there is every likelihood that the numinous qualities which make the mother-imago so dangerously powerful derive from the collective archetype of the anima, which is incarnated anew in every male child." (para 27) The mother's power comes FROM the anima archetype, not the other way.

**Eros vs. Logos**: "The animus corresponds to the paternal Logos just as the anima corresponds to the maternal Eros. But I do not wish to intend to give these two intuitive concepts too specific a definition. I use Eros and Logos merely as conceptual aids to describe the fact that woman's consciousness is characterized more by the connective quality of Eros than by the discrimination and cognition associated with Logos. In men, Eros, the function of relationship, is usually less developed than Logos." (para 29)

**Animosity**: "In both its positive and negative aspects the anima/animus relationship is always full of 'animosity,' i.e., it is emotional, and hence collective... Very often the relationship runs its course heedless of its human performers, who afterwards do not know what happened to them." (para 31)

**When animus and anima meet**: "When animus and anima meet, the animus draws his sword of power and the anima ejects her poison of illusion and seduction. The outcome need not always be negative, since the two are equally likely to fall in love (a special instance of love at first sight)." (para 30)

**Animus in women**: "In women, on the other hand, Eros is an expression of their true nature, while their Logos is often only a regrettable accident." The animus expresses itself in women as "opinions" rather than reflections - *a priori* assumptions "that lay claim to absolute truth" (para 29). The animus is a psychopomp, "a mediator between the conscious and the unconscious" - just as the anima gives a man relationship, "the animus gives to woman's consciousness a capacity for reflection, deliberation, and self-knowledge." (para 33)

**The marriage quaternio** (para 42, directly read): The complete structural picture at the end of Ch III. Shadow integration is "the first stage in the analytic process." Recognition of anima/animus requires "a relation to a partner of the opposite sex, because only in such a relation do their projections become operative." The third element (anima in a man) is partly transcendent. "The missing fourth element that would make the triad a quaternio is, in a man, the archetype of the Wise Old Man, which I have not discussed here, and in a woman the Chthonic Mother." These four form "a half immanent and half transcendent quaternity, an archetype which I have called the marriage quaternio." The Self is "a God-image, or at least cannot be distinguished from one."

Source: [[jung-cw9ii-aion]], Ch III, paras 20-42 (directly read)

## CW 7 Treatment: The Definitive Early Account (Essay 2)

[[jung-cw7-two-essays]] Essay 2 contains Jung's most extended pre-Aion account of the anima and animus. Key contributions beyond what later volumes assume:

**Anima as counter-image to the persona**: The anima's character is directly determined by the persona it compensates. A rigidly rational, achievement-focused masculine persona produces an anima that is volatile, irrational, and emotionally overwhelming. The specific coloring of a man's anima reflects what his persona has systematically excluded.

**Anima as moods**: At its most primitive, the anima does not appear as a figure at all — it appears as *moods*. The sudden, inexplicable drop into depression, the irritable fog with no apparent cause, the wash of fantasy that pulls attention away from the actual situation: these are the anima in its undifferentiated state. The man cannot locate their source and cannot lift them by an act of will.

**Animus as opinions**: The animus's most primitive manifestation in women is not as a figure but as *opinions* — sweeping, impersonal, dogmatic pronouncements delivered with absolute conviction yet disconnected from the actual situation. "That's always the way it is." "Men are fundamentally like this." These ready-made judgments pre-empt genuine response and genuine thinking.

**The threshold of the Mana Personality**: After the anima/animus is integrated, there is a specific danger — the [[Mana Personality]] inflation. The person who has done this work feels enlarged; if they identify with that enlarged state rather than locating it in the [[Self (archetype)]], they become the Wise Old Man (men) or the Great Mother (women) in an inflated sense.

Source: [[jung-cw7-two-essays]], Essay 2, Parts I-II (CW 7, paras 202-406)

## Edinger's Four States of Anima/Animus Relation (Aion Lectures, Lect. 4)

From [[edinger-aion-lectures]]: Edinger distinguishes four developmental states of the ego's relation to anima/animus — more clinically actionable than Jung's four stages of anima development:

| State | Description | Phenomenology |
|-------|-------------|---------------|
| **Infantile** | Original symbolic incest (mother-son / father-daughter) | Passive eros; seeks to be "caught, sucked in, enveloped"; man's ego is secretly in the mother's lap |
| **Projected** | Anima/animus experienced as projection onto a real person | Remote (celebrity adulation, collective projection) or nearby (actual relationship); nearby projections are corrective |
| **Possessed** | Anima/animus dominant but not projected | Anima-possessed man: resentful, inappropriately soft; animus-possessed woman: opinionated, brittle, inappropriately hard |
| **Conscious** | Anima/animus as filtering *functions* | No longer subject to possession; contrasexual element = conduit for unconscious contents; continuous attention to unconscious required |

The fourth state does not "finish" the work; it requires ongoing attention. Jung: "Though the effects of anima and animus can be made conscious, they themselves are factors transcending consciousness and beyond the reach of perception and volition. Hence they remain autonomous despite the integration of their contents."

## The Three Outcomes of Coniunctio (Aion Lectures, Lect. 4)

When anima and animus meet in projection (two people fall in love), three outcomes are possible:

1. **Concrete coniunctio** — marriage, family, joint life; libido channels into building an actual shared existence
2. **Concrete separatio** — one abandons the other; devastating but potentially transformative; the abandoned person realizes the other was not the essential thing and begins inner anima/animus connection. "When half-gods go, the gods arrive" (Ariadne, abandoned by Theseus, then met by Dionysus)
3. **Progressive development** — the individuals gradually discover their in-loveness was based on anima/animus projection; capacity for conscious object love develops; they love the partner as they actually are while maintaining connection to the inner image

The third possibility is the individuation outcome from a relationship. See [[Ego-Self-Axis]] for the structural parallel.
