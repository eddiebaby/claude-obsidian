# Azoth

**Azoth** is the supreme alchemical name for [[Mercurius]] in his aspect as totality — the beginning and the end combined, the alpha and omega of all three alphabets at once. Jung treats it as a term that encodes the universal in a single word.

Primary CW 13 source: [[jung-cw13-alchemical-studies]] Essay IV §6, par. 271.

---

## The Name and Its Construction

The *Aurelia occulta* gives the etymology directly (par. 271):

> "The philosophers have adorned [him] with the name **Azoth**, which is compounded of the A and Z of the Latins, the alpha and omega of the Greeks, and the aleph and tau of the Hebrews."

The word fuses the first and last letters of three sacred alphabets simultaneously:
- Latin: **A** + **Z**
- Greek: **α** (alpha) + **ω** (omega)
- Hebrew: **א** (aleph) + **ת** (tau)

The result is a portmanteau of endings: *Az-oth* collapses both terminals of every major written tradition into a single syllable. Mercurius is "the A and O that is everywhere present."

---

## What the Name Claims

Azoth is not a synonym for quicksilver. It names [[Mercurius]] in his highest, most universal aspect — corresponding not just to Christ but to the triune divinity in general (par. 271). The claim embedded in the word is that Mercurius spans the entire range of being: nothing exists outside him, since the first and last letter encompass everything between.

This parallels the Christian identification of Christ as "the Alpha and Omega" (Rev. 1:8, 22:13). The alchemists, characteristically, made the claim universal by not confining it to one language or tradition — the Jewish, Greek, and Latin traditions all point to the same totality.

---

## Psychological Reading

Psychologically, Azoth = the [[Self (archetype)]] in its most inclusive aspect: the ordering principle that is both the origin and terminus of the individuation process, encompassing the full range of psychic opposites. The tripling of alphabets (Latin + Greek + Hebrew) reflects the alchemical drive toward universality: the Self is not a culturally local symbol but an archetype that recurs across traditions under different names.

The formulation also encodes the [[Uroboros]] idea: beginning and end meeting, the circle of totality. The uroboros *says* the same thing in image form that Azoth says in linguistic form.
