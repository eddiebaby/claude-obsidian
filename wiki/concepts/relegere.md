# relegere

Latin verb (infinitive): "to go over again," "to read/gather/consider carefully," "to treat with scrupulous, repeated attention." From *re-* (again) + *legere* (to gather, read, choose).

Context: [[zeng2023-i-ching-jungian-application]] (Zeng, *JAP* 68/5, 2023), citing Jung's 1950 foreword to the I Ching (CW 11, ¶982).

---

## Definition

*Legere* — the verb underlying English "legible," "lecture," "collect," "select" — carries the sense of gathering or picking through something attentively (as one gathers grain, or picks out letters to read). Prefixed with *re-*, *relegere* means going back over that gathering process again: re-reading, re-considering, treating something with the sustained, repeated care of a careful reader rather than a single glance.

Cicero's *De Natura Deorum* (II.72) derives ***religio*** from this verb, contrasting the "religious" (*religiosi*, those who "read over again," diligent and scrupulous) with the "superstitious" (*superstitiosi*, those who merely repeat rituals without understanding). This derivation stood as the classical, pre-Christian etymology of *religio*, later contested by Lactantius's *religare* ("to bind") derivation.

---

## Use in the Source

Jung invokes *relegere* directly, in parentheses, as the root that makes his etymological claim work: "this is the original meaning of the word *religio* — a careful observation and taking account of (from *relegere*) the numinous" (Jung, 1950/1975, ¶982; quoted in [[zeng2023-i-ching-jungian-application]]).

The choice of derivation is not incidental to Jung's argument. If *religio* meant "binding" (Lactantius), the emphasis would fall on obligation, submission, institutional constraint — the very things Jung's psychology of religion is generally suspicious of when they substitute for lived experience. By instead grounding *religio* in *relegere* — careful, attentive, repeated consideration — Jung reframes "being religious" as a disposition of sustained regard toward numinous material (a dream, a hexagram, any spontaneous psychic content) rather than adherence to doctrine. This is precisely the stance his own three-decade I Ching practice models: not superstitious repetition of a ritual, but *relegere* — going over the material again, carefully, each time it appears. See [[religio]] for the fuller argument and [[I Ching Divination]] for the practice this etymology is invoked to justify.
