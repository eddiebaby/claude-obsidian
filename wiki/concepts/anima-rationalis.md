# Anima Rationalis

**Anima rationalis** ("rational soul") is the Scholastic-theological term for the immortal soul given by God specifically to man, the element that distinguishes human beings from animals. In Jung's reading of CW 13, it appears as one pole of a fundamental duality — the *two sources of illumination* — whose other pole is the mercurial life-soul (*spiritus vegetativus*, the *lumen naturae*).

Primary CW 13 source: [[jung-cw13-alchemical-studies]] Essay IV §4, par. 263.

---

## Scholastic Background

The medieval tradition distinguished three levels of soul:
1. **Anima vegetativa** — the vegetative soul; growth, nutrition, reproduction; shared by plants, animals, humans
2. **Anima sensitiva** — the sensitive soul; sensation and locomotion; shared by animals and humans
3. **Anima rationalis** — the rational soul; reason, will, intellect, moral consciousness; unique to humans; immortal

The *anima rationalis* was understood as directly infused by God at the moment of individual creation — it cannot be derived from matter or from the parents, only from divine act. This is why animals do not have it: it requires the specific divine intervention that makes a person a person.

---

## Jung's Use: The Two Sources of Illumination (par. 263)

Jung identifies a "fundamental duality" in the alchemical texts that structures the entire problem of human knowing:

> "A fundamental duality — the immortal *anima rationalis* given by God to man (distinguishes man from animals) vs. the mercurial life-soul connected with the *inflatio*/*inspiratio* of the Holy Spirit. 'This fundamental duality forms the **psychological basis of the two sources of illumination**.'"

| Source | Soul | Mode |
|--------|------|------|
| Divine revelation | *Anima rationalis* | God-given; direct; immortal; distinguishes man from animal |
| Nature / Mercurius | Mercurial life-soul (*spiritus vegetativus*, *lumen naturae*) | Infused through spirit's breath; connected with Holy Spirit but also with nature |

The *anima rationalis* is the soul that knows through grace and reason — the capacity for theological truth, moral consciousness, and the distinction between human and animal existence. The Mercurial soul is the soul that knows through nature, through the *lumen naturae*, through the body's own inherent light.

---

## The Duality and Its Tension

Paracelsus (Essay III) named these two sources explicitly: "There are two kinds of knowledge: an eternal and a temporal. The eternal springs directly from the light of the Holy Spirit, but the other directly from the Light of Nature." Yet both ultimately come from God: "If Father and Son are one, how then can I honour two lights?"

The tension between the *anima rationalis* and the mercurial life-soul runs through all of Jung's alchemical work:
- The *anima rationalis* tends toward **transcendence**, moral clarity, and the Christian hierarchical worldview
- The mercurial life-soul tends toward **immanence**, nature, the body, paradox, and paganism

Alchemy's bet — and Jung's — is that both must be honored. The [[cognitio matutina]] (morning knowledge) is the knowledge of the *anima rationalis* when it has integrated rather than suppressed the mercurial life-soul.

---

## Distinction from *Anima* (Jungian)

The *anima rationalis* is not the same as Jung's concept of the [[Anima and Animus]]. The Jungian *anima* is the feminine personification of the unconscious in a man — a figure encountered in projection and active imagination. The *anima rationalis* is the Scholastic rational soul — the immortal, conscious, God-given aspect of human nature, grammatically feminine in Latin but not functionally "feminine" in Jung's sense.

The distinction matters: when alchemical texts call Mercurius *anima* (hence feminine, *foemina*, *virgo*), they are using the soul-term for the Mercurial life-soul, not the *anima rationalis*. The two uses should not be conflated.
