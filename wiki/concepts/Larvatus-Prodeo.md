# Larvatus Prodeo

**Latin**: *larvatus prodeo* — "[only] masked do I go forward" / "I come forward wearing a mask."

A private motto [[Rene-Descartes]] recorded of himself, cited by von Franz ([[vonfranz-dreams-1998]]) as the key to his lifelong pattern of never disclosing himself outwardly. The word *larva* in classical Latin means both "mask" and "ghost" or "specter" — the same double meaning that makes the phrase resonate with the ghostly apparitions of Descartes's first 1619 dream.

---

## Context

Von Franz invokes the phrase in her interpretation of the first dream, where Descartes — whirled around by the wind, unable to stand upright, ashamed of his weakness — represents a man who "avoided any risk of disclosing himself" both outwardly and inwardly. She reads *larvatus prodeo* as confirmation that Descartes's public philosophical persona was consciously adopted as a mask, worn over an inner life he kept carefully hidden, and connects this masking to his failure to integrate the "shadow-figure," Mr. N., whom he passes without greeting in the dream.

The word choice is telling on its own: to describe himself with a term that also means "ghost" suggests Descartes sensed, at some level, that his public face and his haunted inner world were the same coin's two sides — the *larva* he wears outward is the same *larva* (specter) that pursues him inward as the ghosts of the dream.
