# Structural Dream Analysis

## Definition

[[Christian Roesler|Christian Roesler's]] narrative research method (*International Journal of Dream Research*, 2018; *Journal of Analytical Psychology*, 2020) that gives Jungian dream theory an empirical backbone. SDA focuses on the **relationship between the dream-ego and the other figures, and the extent of the dream-ego's activity** — i.e., dream-ego *agency*.

## How It Works

Five major dream patterns, ordered by the dream-ego's degree of agency:

1. **No dream-ego present** — the dreamer merely observes, "as if watching a movie."
2. **Dream-ego threatened** — attacked/injured, escaping or protecting itself (linked to weak ego structure / narcissistic disorder).
3. **Performance requirement** set by another figure — the classic examination dream; initiative lies with others.
4. **Mobility dream** — the ego moves toward a destination (agency from disorientation up to successful arrival).
5. **Social interaction dream** — the ego works to make contact/communicate (from being ignored to creating desired connection).

A dreamer's series is typically **dominated by one or two repetitive patterns** tied to their psychological problems. Over *successful* therapy the patterns move from passive/threatened (1–3) toward active/in-control (4–5); unsuccessful therapies show no such shift. About half of transformative cases featured a baby/young child needing care (the [[Archetype|child archetype]]).

## Why It Matters

It validates two design choices for tracking dreams over time: **(1)** track the dream-ego's agency across the series, and **(2)** treat the *series*, not the single dream, as the unit of meaning. Tested on the Freudian specimen case **Amalia X** (93 dreams), it "empirically confirmed" the passive→active movement and found "no evidence of censorship" and data that "refute the hypothesis of wish fulfilment."
