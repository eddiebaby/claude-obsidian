# Structural Dream Analysis

## Definition

[[Christian-Roesler|Christian Roesler's]] narrative research method (*International Journal of Dream Research*, 2018; *Journal of Analytical Psychology*, 2020 and [[roesler2026-jung-dream-theory-empirical-research|2026]]) that gives Jungian dream theory an empirical backbone. SDA focuses on the **relationship between the dream-ego and the other figures, and the extent of the dream-ego's activity** — i.e., dream-ego *agency*. It follows Jung's subjective-level method: figures and forces in the dream are read as personifications of parts of the dreamer's own psyche.

## How It Works

**Six dream patterns** (updated 2026-07-08 from an earlier 5-pattern summary — the 2026 paper is the fuller, later, authoritative version), ordered by the dream-ego's degree of agency, each with numbered sub-patterns of its own:

| Pattern | Dream-ego's position | Sub-patterns |
|---|---|---|
| **1. No dream ego present** | Dreamer only observes, "as if watching a movie" | — |
| **2. Threatened** | Threatened or pursued by another figure | 2.1 destroyed/killed · 2.2 overwhelmed, no coping strategy · 2.3 flees · 2.4 defends itself, threat remains · 2.5 successfully defends itself |
| **3. Performance requirement** | Confronted with a task set by others (exam, find/give something) | 3.1 fails, subjected to others' control · 3.2 prepared but obstructed, unsolved · 3.3 copes successfully through own activity |
| **4. Mobility** | On the way somewhere, following its own initiative | 4.1 locked up, can't break out · 4.2 wants to move, lacks means · 4.3 moves but stalls on obstacles · 4.4 in motion, obstacles, destination unreached · 4.5 reaches the destination |
| **5. Social interaction** | Trying to make satisfying contact (including sexuality) | 5.1 ignored · 5.2 attempted, fails overall · 5.3 succeeds |
| **6. Autonomy** | Establishes or defends its own autonomy | 6.1 flooded by others' affection · 6.2 aggressive toward others, asserting separateness · 6.3 alone and content · 6.4 has resources to spare, helps others |

This typology alone categorizes **over 90% of dreams** seen in clinical practice. Agency rises across the six patterns *and* within each pattern's own sub-patterns.

A dreamer's series is typically **dominated by one or two repetitive patterns** tied to their psychological problems. Over *successful* therapy, patterns move upward — but **nonlinearly**: a mid-therapy regression to Pattern 2 is common and marks the ego, now secured by the therapeutic relationship, directly confronting the central complex ("the path of the hero"), followed by a rapid rise to Patterns 5–6 at termination. Unsuccessful or absent therapy shows no such shift — confirmed against a 208-dream series from a person who never received psychotherapy. About half of transformative cases in the original case series featured a baby/young child needing care (the [[Archetype|child archetype]]).

## Empirically Confirmed (2026 paper)

Three hypotheses, tested against 150 Jung-Institut Stuttgart case reports plus named single cases, **all confirmed**:

- **H1** — successful therapy shows upward movement through the dream patterns across the series.
- **H2** — dream-ego agency increases over the course of successful therapy.
- **H3** — initial psychopathology severity is reflected in the dominant dream pattern at the start of therapy.

One case showed a **downward** movement in patterns that independently located the exact session (25) where the therapeutic relationship collapsed and treatment was terminated as a failure — the dream data tracked the rupture without being told about it.

## Why It Matters

It validates two design choices for tracking dreams over time: **(1)** track the dream-ego's agency across the series, and **(2)** treat the *series*, not the single dream, as the unit of meaning. Originally tested on the Freudian specimen case **[[Amalia-X|Amalia X]]** (95 dreams — the "best investigated case in the history of psychotherapy research"), which "empirically confirmed" the passive→active movement, found "no evidence of censorship," and produced data that "refute the hypothesis of wish fulfilment." Her closing dream is a textbook Pattern-6 autonomy image: she walks on her own hair (her presenting complaint was hirsutism) into her analyst's office, says goodbye, and leaves on her own.

## On Compensation vs. Confrontation

The 2026 findings support Jung's subjective-level method but complicate strict [[Dream Compensation|compensation theory]]: rather than simply balancing a one-sided conscious attitude, the dream's function "could be called not so much compensating... but more confronting consciousness with the actual reality of the psychological situation" (Roesler 2026). This deepens, rather than reverses, the completion-over-compensation qualification below.

**No fixed symbol meanings were found.** The same image (a snake) threatened one dreamer (moralistic superego vs. sexual desire) and helped another. Meaning tracks the dreamer's actual complex structure, not a dictionary entry — the same discipline [[Amplification]] and [[James Hillman|Hillman's]] anti-reduction stance already insist on.
