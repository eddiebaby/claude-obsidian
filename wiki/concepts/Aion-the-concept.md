# Aion (Concept)

*Aion* (Greek: αἰών) is a psychic organism — a word that has accumulated a cluster of related meanings over millennia of usage, all circling a central human experience. Edinger's first lecture in *The Aion Lectures* traces this cluster as preparation for understanding what Jung's book *Aion* is about.

Source: [[edinger-aion-lectures]] (Lect. 1); [[jung-cw9ii-aion]] (title and framework).

---

## The Greek Semantic Cluster

Ancient Greek had three words for time:
- **Chronos** — linear, quantitative, measurable time; "scientific" time
- **Kairos** — the right moment; the fullness of time; the special content of a particular time (Christ's "My time [*kairos*] is not yet come")
- **Aion** — diffuse; a long period; age, epoch; life span; eternity; inner water; destiny; totality

*Aion* links etymologically to *aionios* (without beginning or end, eternal or forever) and *aidios* (eternal). Both have *aion* as root.

**The cluster** (Figure 1, Edinger's diagram):
```
Primordial Water of Creation
        |
      Snake
        |
    Inner Water
        |
    Inner Partner
   /              \
Life Span of      Life Span of
the Collective    the Individual
   \      |      /
    \  [AION]  /
   /            \
Epoch            Age
   \            /
  Eternity    Time
```

All of these meanings belong to the single word *aion*. The word is a "psychic organism" — at its core is some fundamental human experience, and the entire cluster is present implicitly in any given usage.

## Key Ancient Uses

**Homer**: *aion* is parallel to psyche, soul, or life. In the *Iliad*: "When his psyche and *aion* have left him, send death to bear him away." The *aion* as a separate inner reality, almost a double of the soul. Also: the inner water wasted away in tears — "His sweet *aion* was ebbing away." The *aion* as a limited, precious inner substance.

**Aeschylus**: *aion* can mean a generation; guilt is passed through the third *aion*. Also: "The *aion* that has grown with me, inspireth of the gods, breatheth upon me persuasion" — the *aion* as life partner, source of inspiration.

**Plato (*Timaeus*)**: The ideal being was everlasting; since full eternity cannot be given to a creature, the demiurge resolved to have a *moving image* of *aion* — and this image we call *chronos*. Time is the moving image of eternity.

**Plotinus**: "If eternity, *[aion]*, is life at rest, unchanging and identical and already unbounded... then time must exist as an image of eternity." Psychologically: eternity = totality *outside* time; time = that same totality spread out in a temporal sequence. This corresponds to Jung's remark in *Mysterium Coniunctionis*: "The one-after-another is a bearable prelude to the deeper knowledge of the side-by-side."

**Biblical Greek (LXX and NT)**: *Aion* appears in both Testaments. Yahweh promises Abraham land "for an *aion*" (Gen. 13:15) = "for all time." "Neither in this *aion* nor in the *aion* to come" (Matt. 12:32) = "neither in this age nor the next." The Lord's Prayer: "For thine is the kingdom and the power and the glory to the *aions*" (Matt. 6:13).

**Bollingen Stone**: The inscription begins with "Time is a child playing a board game." Jung noted the Greek word that starts the inscription is *aion* — "The *aion* is a child playing a board game."

## Aion as Deity: The Mithraic God

In late syncretism, *aion* became the name of a deity. The Mithraic god Aion (frontispiece of Jung's *Aion*, a Roman statue, 2nd–3rd century CE):

- Lion-headed, body coiled by a serpent
- Holds a scepter and a key (the monarch of the heavens whose portals he opens)
- Wings symbolic of the rapidity of his flight
- The zodiacal signs engraved on his body (celestial and terrestrial phenomena)
- Sometimes identified with Destiny

Franz Cumont: "At the pinnacle of the divine hierarchy [in Mithraic theology was] boundless Time... He creates and destroys all things; he is the Lord and master of the four elements... Sometimes he is identified with Destiny."

The Mithraic Aion is Boundless Time, the total deity. In Gnosis, the primordial *Aion* emanates a series of thirty *aions* (the Pleroma). The Gnostic *aions* are syzygies (male/female pairs); they arise from the primordial Abyss and Silence as Mind (*nous*) and Truth (*Aletheia*). This framework is what Jung's Chapter 2 ("The Syzygy") presupposes.

## Psychological Significance

Edinger's synthesis: the word *aion* is at the convergence of:
- Personal time (one's life span, the limited "inner water")
- Collective time (a generation, an era)
- Historical time (epochs, ages)
- Cosmic time (the astronomical aeon, a Platonic month of 2,000 years)
- Eternity (totality outside time)

Psychologically, this means *aion* points toward the **Self** — which is precisely what Jung's *Aion* is about. The Self is what stands at the junction of personal and cosmic, time and eternity, individual and collective. The book is about how the Self has expressed itself across the Christian aeon.

> [!key-insight] Words are psychic organisms
> Edinger opens his lectures by insisting that "all words are psychic organisms; each has at its core some fundamental human experience." Tracing the etymology and usage of *aion* is itself an act of amplification — the Jungian method applied to language. The cluster that unfolds from this one word is not accidental; it marks a real node of psychic experience around which human consciousness has repeatedly circled.
