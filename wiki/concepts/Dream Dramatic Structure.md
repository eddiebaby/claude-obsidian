# Dream Dramatic Structure

## Definition

Jung's observation (CW 8, *On the Nature of Dreams*, ¶561–565) that dreams tend to unfold like a four-act drama: "There are a great many 'average' dreams in which a definite structure can be perceived, not unlike that of a drama" (¶561). It is the single most encodable scaffold for guided interpretation, used by both Jung and [[Marie-Louise von Franz|von Franz]].

## How It Works

The four phases, in Jung's own terms (¶561–564):

1. **Exposition** (¶561) — statement of place, protagonists, the initial situation. *"I was in a street, it was an avenue"; "I was sitting with Father and Mother in a train compartment."*
2. **Development** (¶562) — the plot complicates; "a definite tension develops because one does not know what will happen."
3. **Culmination / peripeteia** (¶563) — "something decisive happens or something changes completely." (This is where the reversal sits — von Franz's *enantiodromia*, the turn into the opposite — **not** in the development phase.)
4. **Lysis** (¶564) — "the SOLUTION or RESULT produced by the dream-work." Some dreams lack a lysis, "and this can present a special problem."

Jung: "This division into four phases can be applied without much difficulty to the majority of dreams met with in practice — an indication that dreams generally have a 'dramatic' structure" (¶565).

von Franz weights the **lysis** as most important: it often reveals what is being [[Dream Compensation|compensated]] and the direction the Self is pointing — Jung confirms the lysis is "the solution 'sought' by the dreamer… since the dream is compensatory" (¶564). A dream with no lysis (no resolution) is itself a statement.

## Why It Matters

Segmenting a dream into the four acts is the first analytic step after raw capture — it organizes the amplification work and focuses attention on the resolution. It is also the cleanest mapping onto guided UI steps in a dream app.

## Examples

- [[2026-06-25-montana-inheritance]] Part 1: *exposition* (Montana plains, the two houses), *development* (can't finish his house; Jason finishes the larger one), *culmination* (Jason donates it; Jesse turns to Scott), *lysis* (planning the train trip to the walking town — an integrated future, but note the dreamer never completes his own structure).

## Connections

- The lysis is read alongside the [[Dream Compensation|compensation]] question (¶564, ¶566).
- Primary-source extraction and adjacent rules: [[CW8 Dream Methodology]] §4.
- Operationalized as Step 0 of [[Working with Dream Symbols]].
- Von Franz applied this same structure to fairy tales in [[vonfranz-interpretation-fairy-tales]] (ch. 3, "A Method of Psychological Interpretation"). Her four-stage procedure for fairy tales (exposition → peripeteia → crisis → lysis) is identical to the dream method — she was explicit about this equivalence. The extended worked example is "The Three Feathers" (Grimm KHM 63), where the *lysis* (Simpleton inheriting the kingdom) reveals the Self's compensation of a one-sided extraverted attitude. The *enantiodromia* — the fool revealed as hero — is the characteristic crisis move in both fairy tales and compensatory dreams.
