# Scaiolae

The **Scaiolae** are Paracelsus's four "spiritual powers of the mind" (*spirituales mentis vires*) — a fourfold, quaternary system of mental faculties that align with the four elements and constitute the wholeness of the *homo maximus*. They are archetypes in Jung's sense: psychic structures that are mortal as natural functions but immortal as archetypal principles. Jung identifies them — via Ruland's interpretation — as the precursors of his own four psychological functions.

Primary source: [[jung-cw13-alchemical-studies]] Essay III §C (pars. 206–212). Primary figure: [[Paracelsus]].

---

## The Four Scaiolae

Ruland defines the Scaiolae as "spiritual powers of the mind" (*spirituales mentis vires*): qualities and faculties that are fourfold, corresponding to the four elements. They are:

- The **four wheels of the fiery chariot** that swept Elijah up to heaven
- Originate in the mind of man, "from whom they depart and to whom they are turned back" (*a quo recedunt, et ad quem reflectuntur*)
- "In them is nothing of mortality" — whoever lives "by reason of the Scaiolae" is immortal

Ruland's psychological interpretation maps the four Scaiolae onto:
1. ***Phantasia*** — intuition (in Jung's system)
2. ***Imaginatio*** — sensation (or feeling, depending on which axis)
3. ***Speculatio*** — thinking
4. ***Agnata fides*** (inborn faith) — feeling

This is an early Renaissance intuition of the **four psychological functions** (thinking, feeling, sensation, intuition) dressed in alchemical-theological language. Jung notes the mapping is "unmistakable" — every archetype is a *fascinosum* that "excites and grips the imagination" and "is liable to clothe itself in religious ideas (which are themselves of an archetypal nature)."

## Multiple Interpretive Registers

Every archetype can be read through multiple lenses, and the Scaiolae exemplify this:

| Lens | Four Scaiolae = |
|------|----------------|
| **Psychological** | Four functions: *phantasia, imaginatio, speculatio, agnata fides* |
| **Elemental** | Four elements: fire, air, water, earth |
| **Seasonal** | Four seasons |
| **Spatial** | Four quarters of heaven |
| **Religious** | Four articles of Christian faith: baptism, belief in Christ, Last Supper, love of neighbour |
| **Social** | *Scaioli* = "lovers of wisdom" (Paracelsus) |

The four Scaiolae = "the traditional quadripartite man and express his all-encompassing wholeness." The *homo maximus* (Adech / Anthropos) is "the basis and cause of all division into four: four elements, seasons, directions, etc."

## The Quaternity of the Homo Maximus

The Scaiolae belong to the **invisible** *homo maximus* — Dorn's *invisibilis homo maximus* — whose four mental powers constitute an archetypal quaternary. This is paradoxical: Dorn in other writings says the *lumen naturae* is mortal, yet here "the natural powers of the mind are immortal and belong to the *Archa* — the principle that existed before the world." (par. 208)

Jung's resolution: "These concepts of Paracelsus were the result not of rational reflection but of intuitive introspection, which was able to grasp the quaternary structure of consciousness and its archetypal nature. The one [mortal man] is mortal, the other [archetypal man] immortal."

The mortal man's four functions are empirical and temporal. The archetypal Scaiolae are eternal and belong to the *Archa* — the pre-cosmic ground. The individual exercises the functions, but the quaternary pattern precedes and survives any particular individual.

## Adech as Ruler of the Scaiolae

The *homo maximus* or Adech is the **quintessence** of the Scaiolae — "the ruler of the Scaiolae and/or their quintessence." Paracelsus calls him "that difficult Adech" who "hinders our intentions." He "rules over both the death and life of the Scaiolae."

This produces the central paradox of the Paracelsan psychology: the *homo maximus* is at once the goal the adept seeks AND the obstacle that thwarts him. He "permits the first times [= beginnings], but at the end he changes himself" — he allows the work to begin, then introduces distractions that prevent it reaching completion.

Jung reads this as the classical ego-Self tension:

> "Adech is almost hostile to the adept, or at least intent on frustrating him in some way. We have only to make the further assumption that this is what Paracelsus meant... 'He counts himself' among the Scaiolae, the 'lovers of wisdom,' and thereby 'implants himself' in the Scaiolae, the quaternity of the Original Man." (par. 212)

The ego that identifies with or "implants itself" in the Scaiolae has placed itself in the "immediate vicinity of Adech" — it has approached the Self/Anthropos. But Adech deflects the *propositum* (purpose, intention) while leaving the *modus* (procedure, method) intact. The work always remains the same procedure; only the ego's private goals are frustrated.

## The Alchemical City / Paradise

The quaternity of the Scaiolae has architectural equivalents that represent its cosmic scope:

- **Paradise** with its four rivers
- **The eternal city (Metropolis)** with its four gates
- **The *domus sapientiae*** (house of wisdom) — alchemical
- **The squared circle** — the union of the circle (spirit, eternity, wholeness) and the square (matter, four elements, embodiment)

All of these figures express the same thing: the Self/Anthropos/homo maximus as a fourfold, centred totality — a city one can "dwell in" if one is approximated to Adech through the alchemical opus.

## Psychological Reading

The Scaiolae are the earliest Renaissance prototype of Jung's theory of the four functions. The key parallels:

- **Quaternary structure**: four functions cover the totality of psychic orientation (no fifth function in either Paracelsus's or Jung's systems)
- **Archetypal roots**: the functions are not learned but innate (*agnata fides*); they precede individual development
- **Immortality**: in Jungian terms, the functional types are archetypal configurations that belong to the collective unconscious — they are "older than" any individual who instantiates them
- **Wholeness**: the *homo maximus* as the basis of the quaternary = the Self as the ground of the four functions. One cannot individuate by using only one function; the differentiation and integration of all four = psychological wholeness = approximation to Adech

> [!key-insight] The Scaiolae and typology
> The Scaiolae demonstrate that the fourfold structure of consciousness is not a modern psychological invention but a persistent archetype. The fact that Ruland arrived at *phantasia, imaginatio, speculatio, agnata fides* — in an alchemical-theological context — and that this maps onto Jung's four functions is an example of the archetypal pattern forcing itself through whatever symbolic language is available. The specific names change; the quaternary structure does not.
