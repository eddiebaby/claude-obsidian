# Anima Mundi

**Anima mundi** ("soul of the world") is the Platonic and Neoplatonic concept of a single animating soul that pervades, organizes, and gives life to the entire cosmos. In alchemy, it is one of the most important names for [[Mercurius]] in his water/soul aspect — and for the *aqua permanens* as the divine substance that underlies matter.

Primary CW 13 sources: [[jung-cw13-alchemical-studies]] Essay IV §§3–4 (pars. 245, 253, 262–263).

---

## Origin: Plato's *Timaeus*

The *Timaeus* (34b–36d) describes the Demiurge creating the world-soul before the world-body: the soul is woven from Sameness, Difference, and Being, then stretched through the cosmos as the organizing intelligence behind all motion and life. Everything that lives participates in this soul.

The Stoics developed this into the *pneuma* — a fiery, rational breath pervading all things. The Neoplatonists (Plotinus, Iamblichus) made it an intermediate principle between the One (transcendent) and matter (the lowest level), mediating between spirit and body.

---

## In Alchemy

The alchemists inherited the *anima mundi* from the Platonic tradition and identified it with their arcane substance. Several specific uses in CW 13:

**As the alchemical retort** (par. 245): The hermetically sealed glass vessel — the *vas Hermeticum* — is identified with the *anima mundi* "which according to an old alchemical conception surrounds the cosmos." The bottle that contains the spirit *is* the world-soul that surrounds all things.

**As Mercurius's soul-aspect** (par. 262): Mercurius designated *anima* (hence feminine: *foemina*, *virgo*) leads to identification with the *anima mundi*. Avicenna: "He is the spirit of the Lord which fills the whole world and in the beginning swam upon the waters... the spirit of Truth." Another text: the "supracelestial spirit which is conjoined with the light, rightly called *anima mundi*." The alchemists linked the *anima mundi* to both Plato's world-soul in the *Timaeus* and the Holy Spirit present at Creation.

**As imprisoned in matter** (par. 253): The *anima mundi* is the divine essence that has become trapped in the heaviness of matter — exactly as in the Gnostic myths of the Anthropos, the divine light-particles imprisoned in created substance. The alchemical opus is the process of liberating or purifying this trapped soul.

## The Two Loci: World and Individual

The *anima mundi* stands in a structural parallel to the individual *anima*:

| Level | Term | Function |
|-------|------|---------|
| Cosmic | *anima mundi* | Organizes and animates the world |
| Individual | *anima* | Organizes and animates the individual psyche |
| Alchemical | *aqua permanens* / Mercurius | The purified, liberated world-soul |

Jung's psychological reading: the *anima mundi* is the archetype of the world as psyche — the recognition that the cosmos itself is animated by the same principle that animates the individual. When an alchemist labors to release the world-soul from matter, he is projecting the individuation process onto nature: his own unconscious vitality seeks liberation from its imprisonment in unconscious matter.

The [[Lumen-Naturae]] is the *anima mundi* as light — the natural light of the world-soul, which Paracelsus claimed was a second, equally valid source of knowledge alongside divine revelation.

---

## Psychological Significance

The *anima mundi* concept carries a crucial implication: the psyche is not merely personal. The world itself has a soul, and the individual soul participates in it. This is the archaic background behind [[Participation-Mystique]] — the primitive experience of being undivided from nature — and it is also the underpinning of Jung's claim that the collective unconscious is not "mine" but shared.

When the alchemist discovers the *anima mundi* in his retort, he has encountered not just his own unconscious but the impersonal, world-level layer of psychic reality. The *anima mundi* is thus the psychological equivalent of the [[Self (archetype)]] extended beyond the individual to the collective and cosmic level.
