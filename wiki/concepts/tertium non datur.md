# tertium non datur

Latin: "a third is not given" / "no third possibility exists" — the classical **law of excluded middle**: for any proposition, either it or its negation is true; there is no third option. Standing Latin-phrase concept page per vault convention.

## Definition

*Tertium non datur* is one of the traditional laws of classical logic (alongside the laws of identity and non-contradiction): given a binary opposition (A or not-A), reality must fall on one side or the other — a middle, third term is logically excluded. In ordinary use it names any situation framed as a strict either/or with no room for a synthesizing alternative.

## Context in Jung ("The Transcendent Function," ¶189)

Jung invokes the phrase to state precisely what the [[Transcendent Function|transcendent function]] is *not*, in order to define more sharply what it *is*. Describing the confrontation between a conscious position and its unconscious counter-position, he writes that the resulting tension "creates a living, third thing — not a logical stillbirth in accordance with the principle *tertium non datur* but a movement out of the suspension between opposites, a living birth that leads to a new level of being, a new situation."

The move is deliberate and somewhat paradoxical: formal logic says that when two positions are genuinely opposed, no third term can exist between them (a thing cannot both be and not-be in the same respect). Jung's claim is that the psyche is not bound by this logical constraint — held long enough in genuine tension, without either side being suppressed or allowed to win, two opposed psychic positions generate a third position that is not a "stillbirth" (a merely logical, lifeless non-entity forced into existence by contradiction) but a "living birth": a new attitude that actually contains and supersedes both original positions rather than splitting the difference between them. The transcendent function is therefore an *energic* (psychological) event, not a *logical* one — its third term is real in the way a chemical compound is a real third thing distinct from either of its constituent elements, not a compromise average of them.

This is the essay's sharpest one-line statement of why the transcendent function counts as "transcendent" at all: it transcends what formal either/or logic would permit.
