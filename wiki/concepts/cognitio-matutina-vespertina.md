# Cognitio Matutina / Cognitio Vespertina

**Cognitio matutina** ("morning knowledge") and **cognitio vespertina** ("evening knowledge") are Augustine's two modes of knowing, drawn from his commentary on Genesis. Jung reads them psychologically as the difference between knowledge of the Self and knowledge of creatures — and uses them to close the Essay IV meditation on [[Mercurius]].

Primary CW 13 source: [[jung-cw13-alchemical-studies]] Essay IV §10, pars. 299–303.

---

## Augustine's Distinction

In *De Genesi ad litteram* and *The City of God*, Augustine distinguishes how the angels knew the created world on the first day of creation:

- **Cognitio vespertina** ("evening knowledge"): knowledge of creatures *in themselves*, in their creaturely nature — knowledge that is valid but partial, since it terminates in creation, not in the Creator. Called "evening" because the light is already fading.
- **Cognitio matutina** ("morning knowledge"): knowledge of creatures *as they exist in God's understanding*, i.e., referred back to their divine source. Called "morning" because it participates in the full light of the first day.

Augustine's first day of creation begins with **cognitio sui ipsius** — not knowledge of the ego, but self-knowledge that refers itself to God. Evening gives birth to morning: darkness becomes the womb of light.

---

## Jung's Psychological Reading (pars. 299–303)

Jung takes Augustine's cosmological distinction and reinvests it:

| Augustine | Jung |
|-----------|------|
| *Cognitio vespertina*: knowledge of creatures | Knowledge of the ego and its world; consciousness that "gradually darkens as at nightfall" |
| *Cognitio matutina*: knowledge in the divine | Knowledge of the **Self** — the center that transcends the ego; full light of the psyche |
| The self that knows on the first day | *Cognitio sui ipsius* = not ego-knowledge but **Self-knowledge** |

Par. 301 KEY: "When all visible lights are extinguished one finds... the light of the self." (Yajñavalkya) This directly parallels Augustine: when the *cognitio vespertina* exhausts itself, the *cognitio matutina* becomes visible — the light of the Self that was always present but hidden by ordinary consciousness.

---

## The Evening–Morning Dialectic

The transition from evening to morning is not a smooth continuum but passes through darkness:

- **Evening knowledge** ends in night — the nigredo, the darkening of consciousness, the moment when rational ego-knowledge runs out
- **The dark night** → the [[stella matutina]] rises (Lucifer as light-bringer, *phosphoros*)
- **Morning knowledge** → the Self's light, which is not the ego's light

The critical warning (par. 303): For those who do not orient toward the *cognitio matutina*, **Mercurius** as *lumen naturae* (the natural light that should guide toward the Self) becomes instead a perilous *ignis fatuus* — a will-o'-the-wisp leading into the swamp. And the psychopomp who should guide becomes "a diabolical seducer" — Lucifer as "the father of lies" whose voice, amplified "by press and radio," leads millions to ruin.

## Relationship to the Two Sources of Illumination

The *cognitio matutina / vespertina* pair maps onto the two sources of illumination already introduced in par. 263 (see [[anima rationalis]]):

| Source | Mode of Knowledge | Agent |
|--------|------------------|-------|
| Divine revelation | *Cognitio matutina* | *Anima rationalis* |
| *Lumen naturae* (natural light) | Mediating; can rise to morning or fall to *ignis fatuus* | Mercurius |
| Creaturely ego | *Cognitio vespertina* | Ordinary consciousness |

The alchemists' bet was that the *lumen naturae* — if properly worked — could produce the *cognitio matutina* from below, arriving at the same knowledge that divine revelation provides from above.
