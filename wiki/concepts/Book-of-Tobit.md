# Book of Tobit: The Healing Fish

The Book of Tobit (Apocrypha; included in Catholic Old Testament, absent from Protestant and Jewish Bibles) is "the analytic fish story *par excellence*" (Edinger). Jung uses it in *Aion* par. 174 as the definitive illustration of the healing function of the fish archetype. It provides a three-step template for the analytic process itself: **capture, extraction, transformation**.

Source: [[edinger-aion-lectures]] (Lecture 12, pp. 92-94); [[jung-cw9ii-aion]] (par. 174).

---

## The Story

**Tobit** lives in Nineveh. Blind and persecuted, he has prayed to die. He remembers he once deposited ten talents of silver with a man in Media — a considerable sum. He calls his son Tobias to retrieve it.

**Sarah** lives in Media. She has had seven husbands, each killed on their wedding night by the demon Asmodeus before the marriage was consummated. She too wants to die.

**Tobias** sets out for Media with a traveling companion who turns out to be the angel Raphael in disguise.

**The fish**: On the first night, camped at the Tigris River, a great fish leapt up and threatened to swallow Tobias's foot. Raphael told him to catch it and extract the **heart, liver, and gall** and set them aside.

In Media: Tobias recovered the silver, courted Sarah, married her. On the wedding night he burned the heart and liver of the fish — the smoke drove away the demon Asmodeus.

Return to Nineveh: Tobias applied the gall of the fish to Tobit's eyes. His sight was restored. Then Raphael revealed himself as an angel and departed.

---

## The Analytic Interpretation

**Tobit** = the old blind ego; in despair, cut off from its resources (the silver, a genuine psychic value, deposited far away in Media = the unconscious).

**Sarah** = the anima; possessed by a demonic complex (Asmodeus) that destroys every attempt at relationship before it can be consummated. Seven dead husbands = the repetition compulsion; the anima locked in the same destructive pattern.

**Tobias** = the hero-ego undertaking the journey to recover the lost value.

**Raphael** = the Self as psychopomp; appearing as a traveling companion (guide), revealing his true nature only at the completion of the work.

**The fish** = the libido from the unconscious; the curative energy locked in the cold-blooded, primordial stratum. It appears *threatening first* — it leaps up to devour — before revealing its healing capacity. Its specific organs carry specific cures: heart/liver (warm eros content) drives away the demon; gall (bitter discriminating substance) restores sight.

**Asmodeus** = the destructive complex holding the anima; driven away by warmth and eros, not by force.

---

## The Three Steps

These three steps constitute the analytic process itself:

1. **Capture** — making contact with the unconscious content. Not passive: the fish is threatening and Tobias must actively catch it. Active engagement with what frightens.

2. **Extraction** — differentiating the healing virtues from the raw material. Not the whole fish consumed raw, but specific parts extracted and applied to specific conditions. This is the analytic work: discriminating the content, not simply being overwhelmed by it.

3. **Transformation** — applying the extracted material to the living situation. The healing happens not in the catching or the extracting but in the *application*. Tobias must bring the fish material back to where it is needed (Nineveh, the blind father; Media, the possessed bride) and apply it concretely.

## Edinger's Personal Initial Dream

Very early in his own analysis, Edinger had what he understood to be his initial dream — an individual variant of the Tobit pattern:

> "In the dream a golden fish was jumping up through the floor and I was trying to catch it. The process of catching it was rather complicated, but I finally succeeded. The next task was to extract its blood. I got it out of the fish and into a beaker. I was boiling the blood, which was supposed to keep it permanently fluid if the process succeeded. The danger was that the blood might clot before the process was finished. An older man, whom I identified as a spokesman for tradition, told me the method would never work, the blood was sure to clot. However, I did not think so. I thought I had a good chance of succeeding and kept on boiling the blood." (pp. 93-94)

Three steps present: catching (laborious), extracting the blood, keeping the blood fluid through heat (transformation). The tradition figure doubts the process; the dreamer persists.

## The Fish's Double Valence

The fish carries two opposite meanings simultaneously — which is why Tobit can serve as the central image for all of *Aion*:

- **Lowest**: cold-blooded, primordial, undifferentiated; the original concupiscence; the infantile psyche in its most unconscious form
- **Highest**: symbol of Christ, the Self, the divine; Ichthys; the eucharistic body; the food of the messianic banquet (see [[Leviathan]])

"The image of the fish basically has two meanings. It is an image of the cold-blooded, undifferentiated, primordial, infantile psyche, the original concupiscence. The other side of its meaning, in its identification with Christ and the whole aeon, is as a symbol of the Self. So it is the highest and lowest simultaneously, and this corresponds to the fact that when it first appears to Tobias it is a threat until it has been dealt with, and then it becomes a most valuable curative remedy." (Edinger, p. 93)

The threatening fish and the curative fish are the same fish. The complex that was most frightening, properly caught and worked with, carries the highest value.

---

> [!key-insight] The fish you fear is the fish that heals
> The Tobit pattern: what appears as a threat from the unconscious (the leaping fish) is the curative content in its first, undifferentiated form. The three steps — capture, extract, transform — describe what analytic work asks the ego to do with what frightens it most. Avoidance preserves the threat; engagement begins the transformation.
