# Homo Maximus

**Homo maximus** ("greatest man") is Paracelsus's central arcane concept — the Primordial Man or Inner Man who is simultaneously the individual's highest potential and the universal cosmic pattern underlying creation. It is a Renaissance-alchemical version of the Gnostic [[Anthropos]] and the cross-cultural archetype of the divine human.

Primary CW 13 source: [[jung-cw13-alchemical-studies]] Essay III §1D, pars. 165–168.

---

## Paracelsus's Formulation

Paracelsus designates the Primordial Man by multiple secret names, each a facet of the same concept:
- **Homo maximus** — the greatest man; the cosmic pattern
- **Adech** — from Adam; the archetypal first man in his most interior form
- **Archeus** — the vital principle governing individual organisms
- **Idechtrum** — an obscure term; further specification of Adech
- **Protothoma** — the first/original human form

The *homo maximus* is explicitly cosmic:

> "The true man is the **star in us**. The star desires to drive man towards great wisdom."

> "For heaven is man and man is heaven, and all men are one heaven, and heaven is only one man." (par. 165)

The *homo maximus* is the human form of heaven: every individual contains the cosmic pattern, and the cosmos is structured as a gigantic human form. This is the macrocosm-microcosm identity at its most expansive.

## Cross-Cultural Parallels (par. 166)

Jung identifies the *homo maximus* as a version of a universal archetype, "always connected with the question of creation and redemption":

| Tradition | Term | Character |
|-----------|------|-----------|
| India | Purusha | Cosmic man; material of creation |
| Persia | Gayomart | First man; his body became the world's substance |
| Judaism | Adam Kadmon | Primordial Adam; cosmic template; Cabalistic |
| Judaism | Metatron | Chief angel; heavenly man in the Zohar |
| Bible | Celestial Man | Daniel 7:13; Ezra; Enoch; Philo's Logos-man |
| Gnosticism | Anthropos | The divine light-man imprisoned in matter |
| Alchemy | Filius philosophorum / Homo maximus | The completed alchemical man; product of the opus |

All of these express the same archetype: the idea of a cosmic, universal human pattern that precedes and exceeds any individual realization, yet is somehow present in every individual as their deepest potential.

## The *Homo Maximus* and *Homo Minor*

The tension that drives Paracelsus's system:
- **Homo minor** (lesser man): the mortal ego; the outer man; time-bound, body-bound, subject to death
- **Homo maximus** (greatest man): the inner eternal man; the immortal Self-pattern; the sidereal body

> "In the whole Ides there is but One Man, the same is extracted by the Iliaster and is the Protoplast." (par. 166)

The Iliaster (see [[Iliaster]]) = the stellar life-substance that both sustains ordinary life and, when developed through the alchemical work, connects the *homo minor* to the *homo maximus*. The retorta distillatio (*ex medio centri*) = the process of purifying the lesser man until union with the greater man becomes possible.

---

## The *Homo Maximus* as Inner Obstacle (par. 210)

Paradoxically, the *homo maximus* — called Adech by Paracelsus — is also experienced as **the inner obstacle**:

> "That difficult Adech who crosses [the ego's] purpose at every misguided step, who gives fate an unexpected twist, and sets it as a task the very thing it feared."

The *homo maximus* does not cooperate with the ego's plans. He deflects the *propositum* (purpose) without changing the *modus* (procedure): the general method of inner work always remains the same, but what that work produces is determined by the Adech, not by the ego's ambition.

This is Jung's clearest formulation of what the Self does in active individuation: it consistently disrupts the ego's projects in order to redirect energy toward what the psyche actually needs. The Self appears as "the difficult Adech" precisely because it cannot be incorporated into the ego's agenda.

---

## Psychological Reading

The *homo maximus* is the [[Self (archetype)]] in its most grandiose, cosmic formulation — the total person that the ego is not yet, but toward which individuation moves. Jung's careful distinction: this is not ego-inflation. The ego does not become the *homo maximus*; it enters into relationship with it. The *adech* remains always larger than the ego, always disrupting it, always indicating that "a wholeness, of which he is a part, wants to be transformed from a latent state of unconsciousness into an approximate consciousness of itself." (par. 221)
