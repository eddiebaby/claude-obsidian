# Reciprocality Principle

Edinger's coined term for Jung's insight in *Aion* para 355: the unconscious responds **inversely** to the conscious attitude. Where the ego is strong, the unconscious is weak in that quality; where the ego is weak, the unconscious holds the compensating charge. This is the clinical underpinning of transference, countertransference, and the analysis of symptoms.

Source: [[edinger-aion-lectures]] (Lecture 22, para 355, directly read).

---

## The Mathematical Analogy

Edinger deliberately frames this as mathematics because the inverse relationship is *exact*, not vague.

In mathematics, every number has a reciprocal. The reciprocal of 2/3 is 3/2. The reciprocal of 1/5 is 5. The key property: **when you multiply a number by its reciprocal, the product is always 1**.

> 2/3 × 3/2 = 1. Always.

The psychological application: represent the ego's relation to any quality as a fraction. If the ego carries 2/10 of the total aggression charge, the unconscious carries 10/2 = five times the ego's level. The *product* of these two fractions = 1 — which signifies the underlying wholeness, the Self as unity.

"Reciprocality can only exist when two terms exist. When one multiplies two reciprocals, the product is always one. I think that is psychologically significant." (Edinger, p. 164)

## Clinical Examples

**The victim ego**: A patient presents as fundamentally wounded, helpless, passive. Ego's aggression level: 2/10. The unconscious compensation: 10/2 — a full aggressor is constellated in the other pole. In the analytic relationship, the analyst begins to feel aggressive impulses toward the patient. If the analyst is alert, this is recognized as a countertransference signal: the patient's unconscious has constellated its reciprocal in the analyst.

The dog-chase analogy (from animal observation): "If one animal runs, a cat for example, a dog will chase it. If the cat stops running and turns around, the dog suddenly stops and the dog might start running." The animal that runs constellates the pursuer. The one who stops running breaks the reciprocal.

**The speaker who feels weak**: "If a speaker behaves like a weak, fearful victim at the lectern, it will not be long before the whole audience will be after him." The weak presentation constellates the reciprocal aggressor in the collective audience.

**Transference and countertransference**: A patient who is identified with the victim role (however mild and polite they appear) will constellation in the analyst the contrasting powers of health, power, and healing. "As long as those qualities are carried by the analyst, they won't help the patient at all. The task is to reverse those reciprocal fractions, to know how to hand back to the patient what has been constellated in the analyst." (Edinger, p. 165)

## The Uncertainty Relationship

Alongside the Reciprocality Principle, Edinger addresses a companion insight from Jung's para 355: the **uncertainty relationship** between conscious and unconscious observation.

Jung borrows Heisenberg's uncertainty principle: "the position and velocity of a subatomic particle cannot both be precisely measured at the same time... The very act of observation of subatomic particles alters their condition." Jung's application: "In the process of doing all this, one has put one's fingerprints on it. The reverse is also true. Both the observer and the observed influence each other."

This means:
- "There is no such thing as an absolutely objective piece of psychological data from the unconscious, because in order to have observed it, the observer has seen it, touched it, and brought it back."
- The unconscious is altered by the act of observation (making it conscious changes it)
- The ego is also altered by the Self's observation of the ego

The crucial implication: "There is an eye-of-God aspect to the Self which observes the ego, just as the ego, when it reaches a certain level, can observe and affect the Self." (Edinger, p. 165, citing *Mysterium Lectures*)

This is not a mystical claim — it is a structural observation about the reflexive relationship between the two poles of the psyche. Analysis is never a one-way study; it is a mutual alteration.

## Jung's Original Text

Para 355 of *Aion*:

> "The self is a true *complexio oppositorum*, though this does not mean that it is anything like as contradictory in itself. It is quite possible that the seeming paradox is nothing but a reflection of the enantiodromian changes of the conscious attitude which can have a favorable or an unfavorable effect on the whole. The same is true of the unconscious in general, for its frightening figures may be called forth by the fear which the conscious mind has of the unconscious. The importance of consciousness should not be underrated; hence it is advisable to relate the contradictory manifestations of the unconscious causally to the conscious attitude, at least in some degree."

Edinger names this the Reciprocality Principle because "the self is a *complexio oppositorum*" — a union of opposites — and that union is maintained through the reciprocal inverse relationship between the two poles.

## Broader Applications

**Collective level**: A collective ego that is overtly peaceful, overly compliant, and inflated with righteousness will produce a constellated unconscious of extraordinary aggression. Jung's reading of 20th-century totalitarianism: the cultural suppression of shadow material did not make it disappear — it amplified the unconscious reciprocal.

**Symptom reading**: Any symptom the ego resists most strongly carries the highest reciprocal charge. The ego's horror at a symptom is a clue: that charge belongs in the Self's total economy. This is NOT permission to act out the symptom. It is information about where to look.

**The individuation task**: To consciously develop the unlived reciprocal, so that the Self's total charge does not erupt in unconscious projection or symptom. "The task is to reverse those reciprocal fractions" — not by suppression but by conscious development. A man who is 2/10 assertive needs to consciously develop assertion, not to drive the unconscious aggressor further into others.

## Monoimos and Twoness

Monoimos the Arab (a 2nd-century Gnostic, quoted in para 347) states the same principle in different terms:

> "Seek him from out thyself, and learn who it is that taketh possession of everything in thee... and if thou shouldst closely investigate these things, thou wilt find Him in thyself, the One and the Many, like to that little point [kereia], for it is in thee that he hath his origin and his deliverance."

Edinger's gloss: "This tells us that we should make a discrimination between our own will and the unconscious. The ability to make that distinction is the crucial discovery in the process of an encounter with the Self. We first have to realize that we are not one but two; there is an Other inside." (p. 162)

The "twoness" — the discovery of the inner Other — is the prerequisite for the Reciprocality Principle to become visible. So long as one believes "I am my thoughts and feelings," there is no observer to notice the reciprocal. The moment one recognizes the autonomous Other (the unconscious), the reciprocal structure becomes observable.
