# Coincidentia Oppositorum

**Coincidentia oppositorum** ("coincidence of opposites") is the philosophical concept, associated especially with Nicholas of Cusa (1401–1464), that ultimate reality or God necessarily contains and reconciles all opposites. Jung adopts it as the central structural principle of the [[Self (archetype)]] and of [[Mercurius]] — the figure who most fully embodies it in alchemical psychology.

Primary CW 13 source: [[jung-cw13-alchemical-studies]] Essay IV §3, par. 256.

---

## Nicholas of Cusa

In *De Docta Ignorantia* (1440), Cusa argues that God is the *coincidentia oppositorum* — the being in whom all opposites that appear contradictory at the finite level coincide and are reconciled. Maximum and minimum, finite and infinite, one and many, are all unified in God. This is not illogical but *beyond* finite logic: the divine transcends the principle of non-contradiction.

Cusa's *coincidentia* was influential in Renaissance thought, especially in traditions (like alchemy and Hermeticism) that were seeking a unified vision of reality beyond scholastic dichotomies.

---

## In Alchemy: Mercurius as *Coincidentia Oppositorum*

Jung quotes the alchemical application directly (par. 256):

> "The concept of an all-encompassing God must necessarily include his opposite. The *coincidentia*, of course, must not be too radical or too extreme, otherwise God would cancel himself out. **The principle of the coincidence of opposites must therefore be completed by that of absolute opposition** in order to attain full paradoxicality and hence psychological validity."

This is Jung's important qualification: Mercurius is not a simple merger of opposites that eliminates the tension. The tension is essential. The *coincidentia oppositorum* works not by canceling the opposites but by holding them in a productive paradoxical unity:

| Opposite | In Mercurius |
|----------|-------------|
| Water | Fire |
| Good | Evil |
| Masculine | Feminine (*servus* and *virgo*) |
| Above | Below |
| Spiritual | Material |
| Living | Dead |
| One | Many |

The *Aurelia occulta* self-description (par. 267) lists these contradictions explicitly: "I am the highest and the lowest, the lightest and the heaviest... I am dark and light... I am known and yet do not exist at all."

---

## The Psychological Necessity of the Pair

Jung's addition — "the principle of the coincidence of opposites must be completed by that of absolute opposition" — is crucial. A god who simply combines everything without tension would have no psychological validity: it would be a mere concept, not a living symbol. What makes the *coincidentia oppositorum* psychologically operative is that the opposites *remain* genuine opposites — they are not dissolved but held in creative tension.

This is the structural definition of the [[Self (archetype)]]: not the erasure of opposites but their transcendent union that does not abolish either pole. The Self is the coincidentia of consciousness and unconscious, of ego and shadow, of masculine and feminine — not because these distinctions disappear, but because a center large enough to contain all of them has been realized.

---

## Connection to the Coniunctio

The [[Coniunctio]] (the alchemical union of Sol and Luna) enacts the *coincidentia oppositorum* in its most concentrated form: the masculine and feminine principles are united without either being destroyed. The product of this union is the [[filius-philosophorum]] — the hermaphroditic stone who is neither simply masculine nor simply feminine but embodies both.

The difference between the *coincidentia oppositorum* and mere synthesis:
- **Synthesis** resolves one opposite into the other (e.g., darkness absorbed into light)
- ***Coincidentia***: both remain genuine, held in a higher unity that neither reduces to the other

This is why [[Mercurius]] is not simply "the balance between good and evil" — he *is* both, without the one mastering the other. This paradox is the source of his psychic power and also of his dangerousness.

---

## The Uroboros as Image

The [[Uroboros]] (the serpent eating its own tail) is the visual symbol of the *coincidentia oppositorum*: beginning and end coincide, life and death are one gesture, the destroyer and the sustained are identical. It is the most elemental image of the *coincidentia* in the alchemical tradition.
