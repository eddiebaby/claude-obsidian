# Vessel Symbol (Vas)

The vessel (*vas*, alchemical retort) is the containing symbol of the Self — the psychic structure that holds the individual's sense of containment and meaning. It is closely identified with the *lapis philosophorum* ("there is no difference between the vessel and its content"), and in *Aion* it appears as the **Philosophical Pelican** and the **Rotundum** — both images of a circular, self-containing wholeness.

Primary source: [[edinger-aion-lectures]] (Lectures 23-24, paras 377-380, directly read). Text: [[jung-cw9ii-aion]] Ch 14.

---

## The Squaring of the Circle (Lapis Quaternio)

Zosimos writes (in an alchemical scholia to the *Tractatus aureus*): "the *vas* is often synonymous with the *lapis*, so that there is no difference between the vessel and its content." The vessel and what it contains are the same thing. This is also how the Self works: the Self is both the container of the psyche and the content (the psyche's totality).

The anonymous author of the scholion depicts the vessel as "the squaring of the circle": the four elements form a square (four corners), and within that square there is a small circle. The vessel holds the four elements in their tension, and the small inner circle is the lapis/rotundum — the unity within the differentiation.

This image encodes the threefold sequence:
1. **Original circle**: unconscious unity (the rotundum as prima materia)
2. **Square**: separatio into four elements; differentiation and conflict
3. **Final circle**: coniunctio; the re-achieved unity that now contains the four elements consciously

Psychologically: start in unconscious wholeness (original Self-identification, inflation) → differentiation through conflict and shadow work → conscious reintegration. The second circle is not the same as the first. "We start off in a condition of unconscious unity... The course of psychological development requires progressive differentiation of that unity, hopefully into four more or less conscious functions. But the functions, as long as they remain separate, are in antagonism with one another. To complete the process, the original unity has to be re-achieved on the level of consciousness." (Edinger, p. 176)

## The Philosophical Pelican (Figure 25)

The anonymous scholion on the *Tractatus aureus* depicts "the true philosophical pelican." Jung considers it "rather important" and returns to it in *Mysterium Coniunctionis* (CW 14, pars. 8ff.). The figure is simple but highly condensed.

**Structure of Figure 25 (as Edinger describes):**
- **A = SOURCE (Unconscious)** at center — the inner origin
- **B, C, D, E** at the four cardinal points — letters that flow from the source
- **Outer ring labeled "Consciousness"** on both sides

"A is the inside, as it were the origin and source from which the other letters flow, and likewise the final goal to which all the others flow back, as rivers flow into the ocean or into the great sea." (para 378)

The pelican is a mandala: a fourfold entity (B-C-D-E form a square when connected) flowing from and back into a central source. This is the same image as:
- The four rivers of Paradise (Pison/Gihon/Tigris/Euphrates) flowing from Eden and returning
- The Lapis Quaternio (four elements from rotundum, returning to rotundum)
- The Formula of the Self (A-B-C-D cycling)

The "philosophical pelican" is an apparently modest alchemical figure that encodes the entire structure.

## The Rotundum (Omega Element)

Zosimos calls the *rotundum* the "omega element" — the last, the end state — "which probably signifies the head." The rotundum is:
- The small circle at the center of the squared circle
- The bottom of the Lapis Quaternio (= original chaos)
- The same as the Anthropos at the top (beginning = end)
- The vessel's own self-containing circularity

The rotundum is "the unknowable beginning state or chaos, out of which unfold the four elements, which in turn unify into the lapis." (Fig. 24 caption) It is pre-differentiated, undivided — but it is also the *terminus* of the process, what remains after full circulatio.

## The Four Rivers as Philosophical Pelican

The four rivers of Paradise (from the Naassene interpretation in para 172/Edinger's chapter 19) are the same image as the philosophical pelican:
- Eden's brain at center = the SOURCE (A)
- Pison (eye/vision), Gihon (ear/hearing), Tigris (smell/nose), Euphrates (mouth/nourishment) = B, C, D, E flowing outward
- All four ultimately flow into the sea = return to source

"The head of the four rivers can be seen, and they flow out of eye, ear, nose and mouth, going to make up a mandala — exactly the same as the 'philosophical pelican' image which is simply abstracted a little more." (Edinger, p. 176) "Give me a drink" (John 4:10) — Christ speaking from the Euphrates / mouth level, offering the living water.

## The Projected Vessel

"Basically, the vessel represents the vessel of Selfhood in the individual psyche. In ordinary human life, this containing vessel is projected into relationships, into groups, into a church, or into other collective settings. The individual's sense of containment or fulfillment is dependent upon the connection to whatever that collective vessel is." (Edinger, p. 177)

Forms of projected vessel:
- **Church / religion**: the community holds the Self-vessel collectively; the individual participates in the container
- **Marriage or intimate relationship**: the partner becomes the vessel; withdrawal of projection is experienced as the marriage "dying"
- **Therapeutic relationship**: the analyst temporarily embodies the vessel; this is "all right as a temporary phenomenon, but it is not permanently satisfactory"
- **Group identities**: nation, political movement, ideology — all function as collective vessels

**The critical transition**: "Eventually the vessel must somehow find its way back into the individual." When collective containers fail or are no longer psychologically live — as is happening at the Aeon transition (see [[Pisces-Aeon]]) — the Self-vessel must be realized internally. This is the task individuation sets for the modern person: to become one's own vessel.

## Clinical Significance

The vessel/vas symbol appears in dreams and analysis as:
- Containers of any kind (cups, bowls, pots, vases, retorts, ovens, rooms, caves)
- Circular or self-enclosed structures
- Rivers that flow from and return to a source
- The four-chambered heart or brain
- The head (Zosimos: rotundum = the head = the self-containing sphere)

Edinger's gloss: finding the vessel means finding the individual locus of the Self that does not require collective projection. It is the inner equivalent of the church — a containing structure that holds the psyche through its transformations without requiring external support.

> [!key-insight] The Vessel and Scott's individuation
> The question "what contains me?" is central to any individuation work. If the answer is "nothing — I contain myself by will," that is inflation. If the answer is "trading gives me containment" or "studying Jung gives me containment," those are projected vessels. The task is to develop the vessel that is the ego's genuine conscious relationship with the Self — a living symbolic connection that holds through suffering, not just through success.

## CW 13: The Krater of Zosimos (pars. 96–117)

Jung's commentary on the Visions of Zosimos gives the Hermetic vessel its earliest and most concentrated treatment.

**The bowl-shaped altar** in the visions is "unquestionably related to the *krater* of Poimandres" (par. 96) — the vessel the demiurge sent down to earth filled with Nous (*mind*), so that those striving for higher consciousness could baptize themselves in it. Zosimos himself instructs Theosebeia: "Hasten down to the shepherd and bathe yourself in the *krater*, and hasten up to your own kind (*γένος*)." She had to go down to death/rebirth, then up to "the twice-born, or, in the language of the gospels, the kingdom of heaven."

**The krater as vas Hermetis** (par. 97): the wonder-working vessel is a font or *piscina*, a uterus of spiritual renewal or rebirth. The *benedictio fontis* in the Christian rite encodes the same idea. In the Isis narrative, the angel Amnael brings "a small, unpitched vessel filled with a translucent water" — the water of the art, the real arcanum. The connection is explicit: vessel = arcane substance = spring of life.

**Osiris and the dismembered body** (par. 97): the Nile water = Osiris's body in its fourteen dismembered parts. Lead = "the sealed tomb of Osiris, containing all the limbs of the god." This identifies the vessel with the tomb that holds and eventually resurrects the god — the vessel is the container of the divine body awaiting reconstitution.

**"Water is spirit"**: In the *aqua nostra* of the alchemists, water, fire, and spirit coalesce (par. 98). The *aqua permanens* as the vessel's content is simultaneously what the vessel contains and what the vessel *is* — just as the lapis and its vessel are identical.

**Dorn's formulation** (par. 115): the vessel must be made "by a kind of squaring of the circle." It is not a physical retort but "essentially a psychic operation, the creation of an inner readiness to accept the archetype of the self in whatever subjective form it appears." Dorn calls it the *vas pellicanicum* — through its help, the *quinta essentia* can be extracted from the prima materia.

**"The self is its own container"** (par. 115): "The vessel is the lapis itself and at the same time contains it; that is to say, the self is its own container. This formulation is borne out by the frequent comparison of the lapis to the egg or to the dragon which devours itself and gives birth to itself." The vessel and what it contains are identical — the same paradox as the uroboros.

**Christ as vessel** (par. 116): in the Epistle of Barnabas, Christ's body is called "the vessel of the spirit." Christ is the pelican who plucks out his breast feathers for his young. In Herakleon's Gnostic tradition: "I am a vessel more precious than the feminine being who made you." The vessel-symbol moves between the material and the divine without distinction.
