# In stercore invenitur

**Latin: "it is found in filth" (or "in dung").** An alchemical dictum Jung quotes (CW 16, 1946, ¶384): the precious thing — the *lapis*, the gold, the spiritual treasure — is found in the basest, most despised matter. [[Eckhard Frick|Frick]] ([[frick2025-spiritual-path-transformation|2025]]) uses it to state the core of "analytical alchemy": the physical and the spiritual are linked, as the alchemist links laboratory and oratory — one finds God (or the Self) precisely in the low, the bodily, the excremental.

## Meaning

The phrase encodes the alchemical/Jungian refusal of a purely upward, disembodied spirituality. The highest value is *hidden in* the lowest material, not above it. Jung: the alchemists linked the physical and the spiritual — "in stercore invenitur," found in filth. Frick pairs it with Jung's insistence (Zarathustra Seminar) that "no individuation can take place… without the animal… that one black spot, which is the earth, is absolutely indispensable on the bright shield of spirituality." Spirituality that tries to destroy the body "to be something imaginary" is "a lie."

## In the Transformation Argument

The dictum is one node of Frick's larger claim that transformation is **circular** and passes *through* the base rather than bypassing it:
- It rhymes with the [[Nigredo|nigredo]] — the blackening, the "dark night," the confrontation with the shadow — as the necessary lowest point of the opus.
- It grounds [[Faith in O]] in matter: O (the mystical, the formless infinite) is met in *K*'s failure and in the body's insistence, not in an escape from them.
- It is the antidote to **spiritual bypassing** ([[Discernment of Spirits]]): the treasure is not reachable by rising above the filth, only by going into it.
