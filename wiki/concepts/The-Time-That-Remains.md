# The Time That Remains

The paired figure to [[The Hour That Never Comes]] in [[Daniel Françoli Yago]]'s [[yago2026-hour-that-never-comes-time-that-remains|2026 essay]]: not suspended anticipation but the aftermath of collapse, when temporality no longer points to the future but to survival and remainder. "The fall doesn't always end when the body crashes to the ground." Patients keep moving, meeting obligations, while carrying a silent collapse within: "I no longer know what comes next," "it's as if I'm still walking, but inside I've already ended."

## Three Namings of the Same Wound

- **Peter Pál Pelbart** — not a timeline but "a labyrinth of time... not a circle of time, but a vortex" — a saturated present connecting neither to past nor future.
- **[[Mark Fisher]]** — the **cancellation of the future**: capitalism doesn't just dominate the present, it colonizes the imagination of time itself. Tomorrow becomes only an endlessly remixed version of today.
- **[[Byung-Chul Han]]** — "hyperactive stagnation": not sudden catastrophe but the narcissistic repetition of the same, continuously updated yet nothing truly happening. "The acceleration of time does not generate future, but repetitions."

## The Imaginal Figure

Not the hero, the orphan, or the pilgrim — **the wanderer among ruins**, something between the Tarot Hermit and Walter Benjamin's Angelus Novus: one who knows the building has collapsed yet keeps walking among the stones, collecting useless objects, repairing cracks no one else will notice. "This kind of time cannot be healed."

## Clinical Vignettes

- **A.**, 53 — stopped teaching after caring for his dying father; no acute distress. "I still wake up, but the days no longer come with me." Precise speech, no gravity, "like someone recounting a dream from which they have long since awakened."
- **T.**, 51 — lost her daughter, then her job of 27 years. Stopped crying, stopped dreaming — until one recurring dream returned: a dirt road littered with fragments of demolished houses; she walks calmly, picks things up, sits, walks on. Wakes with the same line every time: "There's nowhere left to get to, but I'll keep walking." Her own words for the work: "I think I'm trying to learn to live as if the world had ended, but I was still in it."

## Not Only Trauma's Consequence — Possibly Individuation's Condition

Yago's genuine extension: the collapse of linear time "can be understood not merely as a consequence of trauma, but as a specific condition of the individuation process." When continuity breaks — through loss, the suspension of a dream, or a failed continuity-project — what opens is not only emptiness but a field where the psyche's own temporality can finally surface: spiral rather than progressive (after Leda Martins's Afro-diasporic spiral time), the past insisting as recursive unfolding rather than clean repetition. Individuation here is not a return to normal function but "a symbolic traversal that becomes possible only when the ego yields to the irregular rhythms of the psyche."

## Clinical Stance

Do not attempt to rebuild the timeline. Accompany the pulse of what survives. Georges Didi-Huberman's fireflies — fragile, discontinuous, real, appearing and disappearing without warning — are Yago's closing image: "Analysis, at this point, does not turn on the light: it walks with the fireflies."
