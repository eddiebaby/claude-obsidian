# King's Renewal Parable

> **One-line definition:** Jung's recurring alchemical trope in which a corrupt, sick, or sterile king is renewed — through dissolution, immersion in his own darkness, and death — by or through his son; a father-son (rather than mother-son) template for rebirth, running in parallel to the hero's-journey material Jung treats in *Symbols of Transformation*.

---

## Origin and Scope

Jung discusses the sick/renewed king across three major works: *Psychology and Alchemy* (1944), *Mysterium Coniunctionis* (1955-1956), and — outside alchemy proper — *Answer to Job* (1952), where Yahweh is redeemed by his son Christ. [[Reginald Ajuonuma|Ajuonuma]] (2023) is the first source in this vault to extract the trope as a distinct interpretive template and apply it outside the Jungian corpus, to the Greek Oedipus myth (see [[ajuonuma2023-alchemical-oedipus]]).

The parable differs structurally from the mother-son rebirth narratives that dominate *Symbols of Transformation* ([[jung-cw5-symbols-transformation]]): here the psychological problem is posed and resolved across the **father-son dyad**, not the hero's separation from the maternal matrix.

---

## The King's Sickness

The alchemical king suffers from sterility, senescence, ignobility, or corruption — psychologically, a lack of "the dark, chthonic aspect of nature" (Jung 1955-1956, para. 427). Consciousness has become one-sided and tyrannical:

> "The more distinctly an idea emerges and the more consciousness gains in clarity, the more monarchic becomes its content, to which everything contradictory has to submit.... For these reasons, too, the king constantly needs the renewal that begins with a descent into his own darkness, an immersion in his own depths, and with a reminder that he is related by blood to his adversary." (Jung 1955-1956, para. 471)

The king's darkness is associated with "the secret, infernal fire" and the blackness of the [[Nigredo]] — the initial stage of the opus — and with Saturn as "dark, cold *maleficus*" (Jung 1955-1956, paras. 464-473). Saturn is also "connected with dubious love-affairs" (para. 140) — a detail Ajuonuma reads directly onto Laius's abduction of Chrysippus.

---

## The Renewal Process

The king is renewed through a sequence of fatalities: "dissolution and decomposition, extinction of his light in the darkness, incineration in the fire, and renewal out of the chaos" (Jung 1955-1956, para. 486). Early versions of the parable frame the goal as "a strengthening, rejuvenation or renewal of the initial state"; later versions clarify the goal as "transformation into a higher nature" (para. 367).

The reborn figure is named the ***puellus regius*** (kingly boy) — read by Jung as "an allegory of Christ" (para. 465) — or, in the fuller alchemical vocabulary Ajuonuma also draws on, the ***filius regius*** (royal son). See [[filius-regius]].

The alchemists called the developmental process itself **"the philosophical tree,"** whose goal is the production of the ***lapis*** (philosopher's stone), which they identified with Christ (Jung 1944, para. 451). Dorn's gloss: the root of the philosophical tree is "ascribed to Saturn" — the corruption of the old king is the *necessary soil* for the splendour of his son (Dorn 1659, as cited in Jung 1945/1954, para. 409). This is the parable's central psychological claim: **the son's transformation requires, and is grown from, the father's corrupted matter — not despite it.**

---

## Iconography: Splendor Solis

The classic pictorial statement of the parable is Figure 1 in the *Splendor Solis* (1582 alchemical manuscript, British Library Harley 3469): "the salvation of the drowning king (in the background) by his crowned son (in the foreground)." Cited by [[ajuonuma2023-alchemical-oedipus]] as the visual template for reading Oedipus's redemption of Laius.

---

## Answer to Job as the Non-Alchemical Instance

Jung's most famous application of this template outside alchemy is [[jung-answer-to-job]]: Yahweh's unintegrated shadow and moral one-sidedness are redeemed through his son Christ's incarnation and suffering. The structural parallel — a sick/incomplete divine or royal father redeemed through the son's descent and suffering — is the same trope Ajuonuma applies to Laius and Oedipus, making the Oedipus myth a Greek variant of the same archetypal pattern Jung already found in the Judeo-Christian God-image. See [[God-Image (Imago Dei)]].

---

## Ajuonuma's Application: Laius and Oedipus

[[ajuonuma2023-alchemical-oedipus]] uses the [[Visio Arislei]] — a specific, more granular version of this parable — as the amplificatory tool, mapping Laius onto the sterile ***[[rex-marinus|Rex Marinus]]*** and Oedipus onto the renewed royal son. The full mapping, and the argument that Oedipus becomes the Jungian [[Self (archetype)|Self]], is documented on the source page and on [[Visio Arislei]].
