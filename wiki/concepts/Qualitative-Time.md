# Qualitative Time

Jung's **early, later-abandoned** model of synchronicity: the idea that a moment in time itself possesses a *quality*, so that whatever is born or begun at that moment shares the moment's character. Documented by [[Jingchao Zeng|Zeng]] & [[Nathan Fraikin|Fraikin]] ([[zeng-fraikin2026-synchronicity-postjungian-astrology|2026]]) as the model post-Jungian astrology revived after Jung himself discarded it.

## The Model

From Jung's 1930 memorial talk for Richard Wilhelm (CW 15, ¶81–84): "If there are any astrological diagnoses of character that are in fact correct, this is due **not to the influence of the stars but to our own hypothetical time qualities**." The birth moment "contains a certain quality that exists in time"; the horoscope reflects the quality of the moment, not a causal stellar force. This is a Chinese/*I Ching*-flavoured intuition — the whole situation at an instant coheres in meaning — that Jung floated before he had the mature [[Synchronicity]] concept.

## Why Jung Abandoned It

In the 1952 *Synchronicity* essay, especially after his own **astrological experiment failed statistically** (couples' horoscopes vs. marriage aspects showed only chance, ¶901), Jung dropped qualitative time. He moved to a post-Kantian view: synchronicity is *beyond* the categories of time, space, and causality altogether (¶968), not the expression of a special quality *of* time. He said so explicitly in later letters (1958). Qualitative time makes synchronicities look **predictable and predetermined** from a moment (e.g. a birth chart); mature synchronicity makes them singular, rare, acausal, present-moment "acts of creation in time."

## The Revival in Post-Jungian Astrology

The paper's central diagnosis: astrologers recovered exactly the model Jung buried.
- **[[Liz Greene]]** affirms Jung's psychical synchronicity but "slips into the qualitative-time model" where the birth moment's magical timing predetermines later events — backed by Hermeticism and Paracelsus ("as above, so below"). This "recovers Jung's cautious removal of qualitative time."
- **[[Richard Tarnas]]** goes further, making synchronicity a cosmological axiom of formal-causal planetary order — which, like qualitative time, renders synchronistic events predictable across past and future, so "the abnormal becomes normal" and numinosity is lost.

## Why It Matters

Qualitative time is the fault line between **synchronicity as rare numinous experience** (Jung's mature, defensible claim) and **synchronicity as cosmological principle** (the metaphysical over-reach). The corrective the authors propose is Maggie Hyde's *Synchronicity II* — the astrologer's participatory, present-moment, numinous encounter with the symbol — not a quality inhering in the clock.
