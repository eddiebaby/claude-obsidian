# Iliaster (Paracelsus)

The **Iliaster** is Paracelsus's central arcane concept for the stellar life-substance — the cosmic principle that determines the duration and quality of life. It is one of his characteristic neologisms, a compound of Greek *ὕλη* (hyle, matter) and *ἀστήρ* (aster, star): roughly, "stellar matter" or the star-principle embedded in physical substance.

Primary source: [[jung-cw13-alchemical-studies]] Essay III §2 (pars. 170–171+). Primary figure: [[Paracelsus]].

---

## Etymology and Meaning

The Iliaster is one of Paracelsus's many hapax legomena — words that appear once and require comparison of multiple passages to decipher. Jung notes: "For instance, one can guess that 'Iliaster' or 'Yliastrum' is composed etymologically of ὕλη (matter) and ἀστόρ (star), and that it means about the same as the *spiritus vitae* of classical alchemy." (par. 160)

Analogous Paracelsian neologisms:
- **Cagastrum** = κακός (bad) + ἀστήρ (star) = the evil or pathological stellar principle
- **Anthos / Anthera** = embellishments of the alchemical *flores* (flowers = sublimation products)

The common root — the *astrum* or stellar principle — runs through all of Paracelsus's arcane terminology. His doctrine of the *corpus astrale* was not a new discovery but drew on the "Tractatus Aristotelis" (an old alchemical-astrological treatise), which held that "the planets in man have a more powerful influence than the heavenly bodies."

---

## Life as Mumia and Balsam

Paracelsus defines life in *De vita longa* as follows:

> "Life, by Hercules, is nothing other than a certain embalsamed Mumia, which preserves the mortal body from the mortal worms and from corruption by means of a mixed saline solution." (par. 170)

**Mumia** (from Egyptian mummification): the preserving substance within the body; originally the pulverized parts of Egyptian mummies used as medieval medicament. Paracelsus attributes incorruptibility to a special agent he calls **balsam** — a natural elixir keeping the body alive or, if dead, incorruptible.

The same logic applies to venomous creatures: "a scorpion or venomous snake necessarily had in it an alexipharmic, i.e., an antidote, otherwise it would die of its own poison."

---

## Balsam, Quinta Essentia, Iliaster

The Iliaster stands in a hierarchy:

1. **Four elements** — the substrate of all matter
2. **Quinta essentia** — the fifth essence; gold and pearls can be transformed into it
3. **Balsam** — the life principle, higher than the *quinta essentia*, "excels even nature herself"; equals *spiritus mercurii*; the pharmacological aspect of the Iliaster
4. **Iliaster** — higher than all four elements; determines the length of life; the stellar/cosmic life-substance proper

"The balsam is the life principle, the *spiritus mercurii*, and it more or less coincides with the Paracelsan concept of the Iliaster. The latter is higher than the four elements and determines the length of life. It is therefore roughly the same as the balsam, or one could say that the balsam is the pharmacological or chemical aspect of the Iliaster." (par. 171)

---

## Three Forms of the Iliaster

The Iliaster has three forms (par. 171):

1. ***Iliaster sanctitus*** — the sanctified or holy Iliaster
2. ***Iliaster paratetus*** — the prepared or perfected Iliaster
3. ***Iliaster magnus*** — the great Iliaster

The exact distinction between these three is not yet fully extracted from the text. They appear to be stages or aspects of the one principle rather than three separate substances — subordinate levels within a single hierarchy.

## Relationship to the Primordial Man

The Iliaster is directly connected to Paracelsus's concept of the Primordial Man (*homo maximus*, Adech). "In the whole Ides there is but One Man, the same is extracted by the Iliaster and is the Protoplast." (par. 168)

"Ides" or "Ideus" is "the gate through which all created things have proceeded," the "globule or materia" from which man was created. The Iliaster is the medium through which the Primordial Man is extracted — it is both the substance and the extracting principle.

This connects the Iliaster directly to the *spiritus mundi* of classical alchemy: the world soul diffused through matter, awaiting extraction through the alchemical *opus*. See [[Anthropos]], [[Mercurius]].

---

## Alchemical Context

Jung situates the Iliaster within the broader alchemical tradition. The *corpus astrale* idea — that man contains a stellar body more powerful than the visible stars — appears in the "Tractatus Aristotelis." The *lumen naturae* dwelling in man "has been brought by the stars." The medicine is found in the *astrum*: "in man, who is made in the image of God, can be found the cause and the medicine."

The Iliaster is therefore Paracelsus's version of the *anima mundi* or *spiritus vitae* — the cosmic life-principle that descends into matter and into man, and can be extracted and concentrated by the art. It differs from classical alchemy primarily in terminology and in its explicit connection to human longevity (*De vita longa* = "On Long Life").

## The Retorta Distillatio and the Centre (pars. 185-189)

Paracelsus's transformation of the Iliaster proceeds through *retorta distillatio* — "a distillation that was in some way turned back upon itself." The vessel: the Pelican, where distillate runs back into the belly of the retort. The purpose: to purify the human body until it unites with the *maior homo* (the inner spiritual man). Jung's assessment: "This was not an ordinary chemical operation — it was essentially a psychological procedure."

The distillation proceeds "from the midst of the centre" (*ex medio centri*). The **centre** is the alchemical key:

- Dorn: "Nothing is more like God than the centre, for it occupies no space, and cannot be grasped, seen, or measured." The centre = "an infinite abyss of mysteries."
- Michael Maier: the centre contains the "indivisible point," simple, indestructible, eternal. Gold = symbol of eternity.
- Fire originates at centre; carries everything upward; when it cools, everything falls back to centre
- Benedictus Figulus: "Visit the centre of the earth, / There you will find the global fire."

The **sidereal balsam** = the *corpus astrale* = the shining body in man = the Iliaster in its stellar form. It dwells in the heart "like the sun in the macrocosm." The egg's red sun-point (the chick in the yolk) = the same image. Mylius: the chick = bird of Hermes = the mercurial serpent.

Dorn's formula of the One: "For in the One is the One and yet not the One; it is simple and consists of the number four." The *infallibile medii centrum* = "the centre of natural wisdom, whose circumference, closed in itself, forms a circle: an immeasurable order reaching to infinity." The quaternity resolves into unity; the One is the midpoint.

> **Par. 189 — Key conclusion**: "From this context we can see that the *retorta distillatio ex medio centri* results in the activation and development of **a psychic centre, a concept that coincides psychologically with that of the self**."

The alchemical distillation from the centre = the psychological process of Self-development. The *spagirica foetura* (spagyric foetus) = the *filius philosophorum* = "the inner, eternal man in the shell of the outer, mortal man." The *filius* cures not only bodily defects but "the subtle and spiritual sickness in the human mind." See [[Self (archetype)]].

## Melissa, Iloch, Aniadus — The Coniunctio in the Spring (par. 190+)

At the end of the Iliaster process, Paracelsus introduces several key arcane terms:

**Melissa** (lemon balm): singled out for special honour. In ancient medicine considered a means of inducing happiness and purging "black, burnt-out blood" (Saturnine melancholy). In Paracelsus's arcane system: Melissa "unites in itself the powers of the 'supracelestial coniunctio'." It purges Saturnine heaviness from the body — prerequisite for the inner conjunction.

**Iloch**: appears after Paracelsus speaks of separating Saturn from Sol. Saturn = cold, dark, heavy, impure. Sol = the opposite. When the body has been purified by Melissa and freed from Saturnine melancholy, the *coniunctio* occurs with the "long-living inner, or astral, man" — and from this conjunction arises the **Enochdianus**.

**Aniadus**: constitutes the essence of the *Iloch*, that is, of the *coniunctio*. Dorn defines it as "the efficacy of things" — "the springtime condition." It comes about by the "exaltation of both worlds," and "in the true May, when the exaltations of Aniada begin, these should be gathered."

The sequence:
1. **Separation**: Saturn separated from Sol
2. **Purification**: body purged by Melissa from Saturnine melancholy
3. **Coniunctio**: purified body unites with the long-living astral man (= *maior homo* = the Iliaster's goal)
4. **Product**: Enochdianus (named after Enoch, the one who walked with God and was taken)
5. **Aniadus**: the springtime efficacy = the virtue of the conjunction = the *filius*'s life-force in its most potent form

The springtime / May imagery: the coniunctio occurs in the moment of highest natural vitality. The word "Aniadus" itself appears to be Paracelsus's coinage for the culminating state — "in the true May" recalls the sacred marriage of Sol and Luna under the most propitious stellar conditions.

**The "lightning of Saturn"** appears at the end of the process and separates from the lightning of Sol. What appears pertains "to longevity, to that undoubtedly great Iliaster." The process does not diminish the body's weight, only its "turbulence" — the result is "tranquillity of mind" (*tranquillitas animi*). This is the goal of the Paracelsan *opus*: not just a longer life but a tranquil one, freed from Saturnine heaviness.

> [!key-insight] Melissa and Saturnine melancholy
> The fact that Paracelsus's prerequisite for the inner coniunctio is the purging of Saturnine melancholy is psychologically exact: Saturn/lead = the heavy, depressive weight of unconscious matter. The *nigredo* is the Saturnine state. Melissa as the herb that purges black bile and enables joy is the bridge between the *nigredo* and the *albedo* — between the dark prima materia and the spring-bright coniunctio.
