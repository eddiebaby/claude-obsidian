# Anthropos (Primordial Man)

The **Anthropos** — the Primordial Man, First Man, or Cosmic Man — is a recurring archetype in Gnosticism, alchemy, Neoplatonism, and cross-cultural mythology: the idea that a pre-existent, cosmic-scale human figure underlies creation, has fallen into matter, and must be recovered. In Jung's reading, the Anthropos is the projected Self — the archetype of wholeness externalised as a cosmic figure — and it appears at the top of the Fourfold Quaternio as the origin point of the entire alchemical symbol-system.

Primary CW 13 source: [[jung-cw13-alchemical-studies]] Essay III §§1C-D (pars. 159-168); with Paracelsus as the bridge between ancient Gnosis and Renaissance alchemy. See also [[Fourfold-Quaternio]] for the structural position of Anthropos in the Quaternio.

---

## The Paracelsan Primordial Man (pars. 165–168)

In Paracelsus, the Primordial Man has several secret names: **Adech** (from Adam), **homo maximus**, **Archeus**, **Idechtrum**, **Protothoma**.

Key formulations:

> "The true man is the star in us. The star desires to drive man towards great wisdom." (*Paracelsus*)

> "For heaven is man and man is heaven, and all men are one heaven, and heaven is only one man." (*Paragranum*)

Man stands in the relationship of a son to the inner heaven — the Father-principle Paracelsus calls *homo maximus* or Adech. The Archeus is "similar to man and consists of the four elements and is an Archeus and is composed of four parts; say then, he is the great Cosmos."

The Iliaster is the medium: "In the whole Ides there is but One Man, the same is extracted by the Iliaster and is the Protoplast." Ides/Ideus is "the gate through which all created things have proceeded," the "globule or materia" from which man was created.

Adam Kadmon (Cabalistic) was identified with the *filius philosophorum*. Originally this figure was probably the **ἄνθρωπος φωτεινός** (man of light) who was imprisoned in Adam — the same figure Zosimos encounters in the "Meridian of the Sun" visions.

## Cross-Cultural Parallels

The Primordial Man is a globally distributed archetype:

| Tradition | Name | Characteristics |
|-----------|------|-----------------|
| **Indian** | Prajapati / Purusha | "The size of a thumb and dwells in the heart of every man"; cosmic creator |
| **Persian** | Gayomart (*gayō-maretan* = "mortal life") | Youth of dazzling whiteness; = alchemical Mercurius |
| **Jewish (Zohar)** | Metatron | Created together with light; the celestial man |
| **Biblical** | Celestial Man | In the visions of Daniel, Ezra, Enoch; in Philo Judaeus |
| **Gnostic** | Anthropos | One of the principal figures; connected with creation and redemption |
| **Alchemical** | *Filius philosophorum* | Son of the philosophers; = lapis = light-bringer |
| **Paracelsian** | *Homo maximus* / Adech | "The star in us"; inner Father = the great Cosmos |

"The ancient teachings about the Anthropos or Primordial Man assert that God, or the world-creating principle, was made manifest in the form of a 'first-created' (*protoplastus*) man, usually of cosmic size." (par. 168)

## The Anthropos in the Fourfold Quaternio

In [[Fourfold-Quaternio]], Jung places the Anthropos at the top of the chain:

**Anthropos** ↓ Man ↓ Serpent ↓ Lapis ↓ Rotundum → (back to Anthropos)

The Anthropos = the beginning and end; the Lapis = its material expression; the Rotundum = the undifferentiated substrate from which both arise. The entire quaternio is the Self understood at different levels of embodiment.

This structure recurs in Neoplatonic descent: the One → Nous (Anthropos) → Psyche → matter → (return to One). The alchemical *opus* re-ascends the chain.

## The Lapis-Christ-Anthropos Triangle

Three figures occupy the same symbolic position in three systems:

- **Christ** (Christian): God becomes man; redemption from above downward
- **Lapis** (alchemy): man extracts the divine from matter; redemption from below upward
- **Anthropos** (Gnosis): the Primordial Man fell into matter and must be recovered; a third path

Khunrath makes the equation explicit: "This [the *filius philosophorum*], the Son of the Macrocosm, is God and creature... that [Christ], is the son of God, the θεάνθρωπος, that is, God and man; the one conceived in the womb of the Macrocosm, the other in the womb of the Microcosm, and both of a virginal womb."

In Gnosticism, the Anthropos is always "connected with the question of creation and redemption." He creates (by falling) and must be redeemed (by extraction). This is the deepest structure of the alchemical narrative.

## The Upper Triad: Aniadus, Adech, Edochinum (par. 203)

In Paracelsus's system, the deathless Original Man appears under three parallel names that form an upper triad:

| Name | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Adech** | From Adam; the inner *homo maximus*; the astral man; manifestation of the macrocosm in the microcosm |
| **Aniadus** | The "efficacy of things"; the springtime condition; Ruland: "the regenerated spiritual man in us" |
| **Edochinum** | Variant of Enochdianus; Enoch "tasted not death"; the one who walked with God and was taken |

"The three different names are probably only amplifications of the same conception — that of the deathless Original Man, to whom the mortal man can be approximated by means of the alchemical opus." (par. 203)

When the *anima iliastri* is moved into the centre (outside the *capsula cordis* = the heart capsule), it "resounds only as Aniadus, Adech, and Edochinum. Whence comes the birth of that great Aquaster, which is born beyond Nature" (= supernaturally). (par. 201)

The Aquaster: Dorn — born from "the Aniadic mother, with the aid of Adech and through the power of imagination... the great vision, which impregnates the supernatural matrix so that it gives birth to the invisible foetus of longevity." (par. 204)

Union with the *homo maximus* → the *vita cosmographica*. In this life "time appears as well as the body Jesahach" (*corpus Jesahach* = *corpus glorificationis* = the resurrected body of the alchemists = *corpus astrale*). (par. 205)

## The Thousand Names (par. 203)

Because the *lapis philosophorum* and the *homo maximus* are the same archetype at different registers of projection, the lapis had innumerable names — all ultimately names for the Anthropos in his material aspect. Jung catalogues them:

> "elixir, panacea, tincture, quintessence, light, east, morning, Aries, living fount, fruit-tree, animal, Adam, man, *homo altus*, form of man, brother, son, father, *pater mirabilis*, king, hermaphrodite, *deus terrenus, salvator, servator, filius macrocosmi*, and so on."

"In comparison with the 'mille nomina' of the alchemists, Paracelsus used only about ten names for this entity." The extravagance of names reflects the archetype's inexhaustibility — it can be approached from any direction in the human symbolic imagination.

## "That Difficult Adech" — The Ego-Self Tension (pars. 209-212)

The Anthropos is not only the goal but the primary obstacle. Paracelsus repeatedly refers to "that difficult Adech" who "hinders our intentions," who rules over both death and life of the Scaiolae. See [[Scaiolae]].

> "Here the human mind is confronted with its origins, the archetypes; the finite consciousness with its archaic foundations; the mortal ego with the immortal self, Anthropos, purusha, atman, or whatever else be the names that human speculation has given to that collective preconscious state from which the individual ego arose. Kinsman and stranger at once, it recognizes and yet does not recognize that unknown brother who steps towards it, intangible yet real." (par. 210)

"The more it is bound by time and space, the more it will feel the other as 'that difficult Adech' who crosses its purpose at every misguided step, who gives fate an unexpected twist, and sets it as a task the very thing it feared." (par. 210)

This is Jung's proto-description of the **compensatory function of the Self**: the Self frustrates the ego's narrow purposes precisely in order to redirect it toward the opus — toward what the ego feared and avoided. The "unexpected twist" that fate gives = the Self's intervention.

Paracelsus: "If, therefore, I should count myself among the Scaiolae... in the manner of the Necrolii [= adepts]... it is hindered by that great Adech, who deflects our purpose but not the procedure." (par. 211)

Key distinction: Adech deflects the *propositum* (purpose, intention) but NOT the *modus* (method, procedure). The alchemical *opus* always remains the same general procedure — only the ego's private goals are frustrated. The work continues; the ego's projects are overturned.

## The Labor Sophiae and Coming to Terms with the Unconscious (pars. 209-210)

Finding the *prima materia* — the first matter for the opus — is the hardest step. It is "tantalizing": "cheap as dirt and can be had everywhere, only nobody knows it." The task is like "shooting an arrow through a thread hung up in a cloud." The prima materia is "saturnine," "thrown out into the street," "cast on the dunghill," "found in filth."

These epithets reflect not just the alchemist's bafflement but "his psychic background, which animates the darkness lying before him, so that he discovers in the projection the qualities of the unconscious." (par. 209)

The encounter with the prima materia triggers what Jung calls the **labor Sophiae** — "a process of coming to terms with the unconscious, which always sets in when a man is confronted with its darkness. This confrontation forced itself on the alchemist as soon as he made a serious effort to find the prima materia."

The *prima materia* is "saturnine" — it is the Saturnine shadow, the rejected, the repressed. The first encounter with the unconscious takes exactly this form: what was "thrown out into the street" and "found in filth" is precisely what the psyche has rejected. The alchemist and the analysand make the same discovery.

## The Secret Doctrine of the Anthropos (par. 210)

The Anthropos doctrine was dangerous: "since from that point of view Christ was a reflection — and only a reflection — of the inner Anthropos. Hence there were a hundred good reasons for disguising this figure in indecipherable secret names." (par. 210)

This is the theological radicalism of Gnostic and alchemical Anthropology: the inner man is PRIMARY; Christ is his projected image. To say this openly in the 16th century was to invite prosecution. Hence Paracelsus's elaborate arcane terminology — Adech, Aniadus, Edochinum, Scaiolae — all veiling the doctrine that the cosmic man is not external revelation but inner reality.

## Psychological Reading

Jung's psychological translation: the Anthropos = the Self projected onto a cosmic scale. The "fall" of the Primordial Man into matter = the descent of psychic contents into the unconscious or into matter. The "recovery" of the Primordial Man = individuation: the extraction of the Self from its material and unconscious embedding.

The Primordial Man who "dwells in the heart of every man" (Purusha, size of a thumb) is the inner Self that Jungian analysis tries to contact — small, central, permanent, and ordinarily invisible.

> [!key-insight] The Anthropos and the inner work
> When the Jungian process reaches its deeper registers, the Self begins to feel not merely personal but trans-personal — as if the individual's individuation were participating in something larger. The Anthropos archetype encodes this: to individuate is not merely to become more yourself, but to recover a portion of the Primordial Man who is simultaneously "you" and "all men."
