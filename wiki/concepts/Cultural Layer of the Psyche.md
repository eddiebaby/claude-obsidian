# Cultural Layer of the Psyche

A finer-grained model of the [[Collective Unconscious]], drawn by [[Divine Charura]] and [[Stephen Bushell]] (2023) from Jung's 1925 seminar notes (*Introduction to Jungian Psychology*, ed. McGuire, Princeton, 2012) and Tacey's (2012) gloss. Rather than a single collective layer beneath the personal unconscious, the psyche is treated as internally stratified: **family** and **tribal** layers sit closer to consciousness, resting upon a **cultural layer** that "undergirds the stability, customs and distinctiveness of cultures."

Source: [[JOAP-68-1-Charura-Bushell-Duoethnographic-Colonialism]] (*JAP* 68/1, 2023).

---

## Definition

"We contend that the cultural layer supports the family and tribal layer that are closer to consciousness, with the cultural layer containing processes, images and the psychic energies that undergird the stability, customs and distinctiveness of cultures." The example given: a client from the US, resident in the UK, doubted he would ever become fluent in British humour — humour, like shame, embarrassment, and attitudes toward death and dying, is conditioned by "the energetic patterns with which the cultural layer of the psyche has underpinned generations of peoples."

---

## Not Static

The cultural layer is not fixed. Like all psychic forces it is "prone to change as they meet with conscious manifestation on the one hand, and the irruption of deeper archetypal forces on the other." Movements such as Black Lives Matter are read as conscious reactions to "damaging and decaying cultural layer forces," themselves supported by "deep archetypal shifts" — i.e. change in the cultural layer is not purely social/political but has an archetypal dimension pushing from beneath it.

---

## Relation to Existing Vault Model

The existing [[Collective Unconscious]] page presents Jung's standard two-layer model from CW 9i: **personal unconscious** (individual, acquired) resting on the **collective unconscious** (inborn, universal). This paper's layered-psyche model is a refinement, not a contradiction: it inserts intermediate family and tribal layers between the personal and the fully universal/archetypal collective layer, with "cultural" naming the layer nearest the boundary between the collective-particular (a given culture's stability and customs) and the collective-universal (the archetypes proper). The layering is consistent with Jung's broader claim (CW 9i) that collective material is not homogeneous — national, racial, and family unconscious strata are acknowledged elsewhere in Jung's corpus; this paper operationalizes that stratification for clinical and social use.

---

## "The 'I' as Multidimensional Construction"

A structural corollary drawn out in the paper: "As new-born infants we already come into the world influenced by our genetic inheritances, and upon this, these layers then rapidly create a conditioning that become the ways in which I meet the world. The 'I' referred to in language in the singular, we discover to be a multidimensional construction based upon an untold number of layered conditionings." This grounds the paper's later claim that ego-formation itself has a "colonizing" character — see [[Colonizer Archetype]].

---

## Why It Matters for Colonialism/Race Work

The cultural layer is where Charura & Bushell locate both the [[Ethnic Shadow]] (split-off racial/colonial material specific to a people's history) and the mechanism of [[Cultural Complex|cultural complexes]] (Kimbles 2014) that transmit belonging/othering dynamics into individual attitudes "long before one is conscious" of them. It is the layer at which family-level experience (e.g. the grandmothers' stories in the source paper) meets collective-historical structure (colonizer vs. colonized).
