# Duoethnography

A qualitative research method in which two (or more) researchers put their personal, situated narratives into sustained dialogue with each other, so that neither voice can claim final authority over the material. First described by Norris & Sawyer (2003/2004) and further developed in *Duoethnography: Dialogic Methods for Social, Health and Educational Research* (Norris, Sawyer & Lund, eds., 2012).

Source: applied by [[Divine Charura]] and [[Stephen Bushell]] in [[JOAP-68-1-Charura-Bushell-Duoethnographic-Colonialism]] (*JAP* 68/1, 2023).

---

## Definition

Duoethnography combines elements of "autoethnography, autobiography, self-study, life history and more." Norris & Sawyer (2012, p. 12) describe duoethnographies as a form of "collaborative field testing." The defining epistemic commitment (2012, p. 15): by juxtaposing two personal, solitary voices, "neither position can claim dominance or universal truth." Good duoethnography, per Norris & Sawyer (2012, p. 12), should "invite reconceptualization of the past to bring about new insights into the old stories."

---

## Method as Applied in the Source Paper

Charura & Bushell's process ran over a two-year period, arising from a shared diversity-training encounter:

1. Monthly dialogue via Zoom, recorded
2. Email exchange to share reflections between sessions
3. Thematic analysis of the recorded dialogue for statements and patterns
4. Weaving the co-constructed narratives into a coherent joint text, retaining direct quotes from the dialogue in the published paper "to demonstrate the trustworthiness of our process, to offer insights which are of clinical relevance, and to advance an innovative theoretical perspective"

The authors describe this as producing "a transformative experience for each of us," and explicitly frame the method itself as a performance of their theoretical argument: because neither author's account is permitted to dominate, the dialogic form models the anti-[[Colonizer Archetype|colonizing]] stance the paper argues for theoretically.

---

## Why the Method Fits the Subject

Duoethnography's structural refusal of a single authoritative voice is not incidental to a paper about colonialism — colonization is, among other things, the imposition of one narrative's dominance over another's. Charura & Bushell's use of the method is therefore methodologically self-consistent: the racial asymmetry between the authors (Black British/Zimbabwean heritage and White British) is held in the dialogue rather than resolved into a single synthesized "objective" account.

---

## Relevance Beyond This Paper

Duoethnography is a general qualitative method (education, health, social research), not specific to depth psychology. Its Jungian-adjacent value, as this paper demonstrates, is as a structured two-voice dialogic form that can carry archetypal/shadow material (see [[Colonizer Archetype]], [[Ethnic Shadow]]) without one party's interpretation overwriting the other's — a discipline directly analogous to the requirement, in [[Active Imagination]], that the ego not overwrite the autonomous voice of the unconscious figure it is in dialogue with.
