# Diamond Body

The *chin-kang t'i* (diamond body) or *kin tan* (golden elixir) of the Chinese Taoist *Secret of the Golden Flower* — the incorruptible pneumatic body that grows in the individual through the meditation practice described in that text. Synonyms in the same tradition: "holy fruit," "breath-body," "spirit-body," "the true man," "the immortal body." Jung reads this as a symbol for the psychological reality he calls the Self — specifically the Self as experienced from the subjective side: not an abstract concept but a felt centre that is not the ego and cannot be destroyed by what destroys the ego.

Primary source: [[jung-cw13-alchemical-studies]] (pars. 68–69, 76–78).

---

## The Chinese Concept

In the *Golden Flower* text, the diamond body is what "grows" through the practice of circulating the light (*hui kuang*). The practitioner redirects the light of consciousness inward and downward, away from the usual centrifugal dissipation in the world of objects, and allows it to gather in the "germinal vesicle" — the seat of original spirit (*yuan shen*). Over time, a new centre crystallises: the diamond body.

The diamond is the hardest substance; it cannot be cut, burned, or dissolved. The diamond body is similarly impervious to the forces that batter the ordinary ego: emotional entanglements, violent shocks, the fear of death. This is not insensibility but a different order of existence — one in which those forces reach but do not destroy the centre.

Other names and their emphasis:
- **Holy fruit**: what ripens from the work; organic, temporal
- **Breath-body**: it is built of pneuma, not matter; it belongs to the spirit-world
- **Germinal vesicle**: the seed form; the not-yet-realised centre that contains the full potential

## "It Is Not I Who Live, It Lives Me"

Jung locates the psychological reality of the diamond body in this formulation (par. 76):

> "This fact could be best expressed by the words 'It is not I who live, it lives me.' The illusion of the supremacy of consciousness makes us say, 'I live.' Once this illusion is shattered by a recognition of the unconscious, the unconscious will appear as something objective in which the ego is included."

The shift is ontological, not merely psychological:
- **Before**: ego at centre; "I live" — the ego believes it is the agent, responsible, in control
- **After**: a larger reality at centre; "It lives me" — the ego recognises itself as contained within and moved by something that precedes and exceeds it

This is not passivity or dissolution of the ego. The ego remains; it just loses its illusion of sovereignty. It becomes a organ of the whole rather than the whole itself.

Jung compares the attitude toward the unconscious, once this shift occurs, to "the feeling of the primitive to whom the existence of a son guarantees continuation of life" — the son carries what the parent cannot fully carry any longer.

## Western Parallels

**Paul (Galatians 2:20)**: "Yet not I, but Christ liveth in me." This is Jung's primary Western analogue. The shift from "I live" to "Christ lives in me" maps directly onto the movement from ego-centrism to the recognition of the Self as the true centre.

**The pneumatic body (1 Corinthians 15)**: Paul distinguishes the *soma psychikon* (natural/soul body) from the *soma pneumatikon* (spiritual body). The diamond body is the soma pneumatikon — "put on like a garment" (Galatians 3:27: "as many of you as have been baptized into Christ have put on Christ"). It is a future dwelling, invisibly born in the individual.

**The Risen Body**: The Resurrection body in Christian theology — the *corpus glorificationis* — is incorruptible, luminous, not subject to ordinary physical laws. Psychologically: the diamond body is what rises in the individual when the prima materia of the old ego-identity undergoes its necessary death.

**The Alchemical Lapis**: The philosopher's stone (*lapis philosophorum*) is also incorruptible, produced from the lowest and most corrupt starting material, and transforms everything it touches. Structurally identical. See [[Lapis Philosophorum]].

**Nietzsche (par. 77)**: "In most loving bondage, free." The diamond body is not freedom from relation but freedom within relation — the paradox of being most fully oneself when most fully given.

## The Diamond Body as Felt Reality of the Self

The mandala is what the Self looks like in image. The diamond body is what it feels like from the inside.

From one angle: the diamond body is the subjective dimension of the Self-encounter — the felt sense that something objective and imperishable is alive in one. From another: it is the lived experience of having passed through participation mystique and arrived at genuine relatedness.

The mandala's protective circle corresponds to the diamond body's imperviousness. Both are symbols of a centre that holds against the forces of dissolution.

European patient mandala #8 (from the Golden Flower essay plates): "A child in the germinal vesicle with the form primary colours included in the circular movement." The child in the germinal vesicle = the diamond body in its seed form — the new centre being born within the circular field of psychic transformation.

---

## Death and the Diamond Body

Par. 68: "Death is psychologically as important as birth and, like it, is an integral part of life."

The diamond body neither denies death nor is destroyed by it. It is not immortality in the ordinary theological sense (survival of the personal ego). It is the recognition that what is most essentially alive in a person participates in something that exceeds the individual lifespan — in much the same way that the child's body continues something the parent's body held.

The attitude cultivated in the diamond body: "an attitude that is beyond the reach of emotional entanglements and violent shocks — a consciousness detached from the world" (par. 68) — not coldness but a freedom from compulsion that allows genuine engagement.

> [!key-insight] The diamond body is the Self from the inside
> The mandala expresses the Self in image. The diamond body expresses the Self as existential reality — the felt sense of being lived by something larger, of having a centre that participates in eternity. Both are symbols for the same psychological fact; the difference is the angle: the mandala is seen, the diamond body is inhabited.
