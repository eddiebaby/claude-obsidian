# Deconstructing Archetype Theory

[[Christian-Roesler|Christian Roesler's]] critical thesis (book 2023; [[roesler2025-process-of-transformation|JAP 2025]]) that **"archetype theory" is not one theory but four distinct, separable strands** conflated under a single word — and that only one survives contemporary evidence. The aim is not to abolish the archetype but to create clarity by pulling the strands apart.

## The Four Strands

| # | Strand | Claim | Roesler's verdict |
|---|--------|-------|-------------------|
| 1 | **Biological** | Archetypes are genetically transmitted, brain-rooted, preformed structures/contents that appear "autochthonously" without cultural influence | **Refuted.** What is innate are *capacities for relationship, communication, sociality* — not preformed images/contents. Genetic-pathway findings are misused as "proof" of Jungian preformationism. |
| 2 | **Anthropological** | Human universals; cross-cultural primary forms (the Great Mother, the hero myth); phylogeny–ontogeny homology; archaic→civilized maturity scale | **Refuted.** Anthropology, archaeology, religious studies show the assumed primary forms don't exist; parallels are explained by migration, contact, environment (Witzel 2012). The Great-Mother/hero universality "has practically collapsed." |
| 3 | **Transcendental (unity reality)** | Bridges mind and matter via quantum physics; the [[Wolfgang Pauli\|Pauli]]–Jung dialogue; [[Synchronicity]], [[Unus Mundus\|unus mundus]] | **Fruitful.** Generative for transpersonal psychology, consciousness studies, spiritually-integrated therapies. |
| 4 | **Process theory of transformation** | A force in the psyche drives integration toward wholeness ([[Self (archetype)\|the Self]]), preformatted in the unconscious; its universal form could map psychotherapy | **The defensible core of AP** — but only demoted from biological fact to a *clinical hermeneutics*. |

## The Surviving Core, Reframed

Roesler keeps strand 4 but changes its epistemic status: the archetypal transformation process is **"a template for what could be called a clinically applied hermeneutics"**, not a positivistic biological structure. Proper Jungian interpretation is "a flexible (pluralistic), comparative, and interdisciplinary exegesis that seeks out interpretive possibilities — not conclusions" and "a positing of meanings in relation to, and not the uncovering of 'the meaning'" (Barnaby & D'Acierno). Its opposite is **"vulgar Jungianism": the mechanical and reductivist allegorical rewriting of a text according to the master code of the archetypes.** Meaning is *produced* between at least two minds — hence therapeutic change requires two persons and the relationship is "much more than just a projection screen."

## Four Models Inside the Process Strand

Even strand 4 is internally plural — four models of the individuation/transformation process:
1. **Centring** (the Self as autonomous centring factor; mandalas).
2. **Sequence of stages** (shadow → anima/animus → wise old man/great mother → Self) — the most nomothetic, most contestable.
3. **Consciousness from an unconscious matrix** (hero emancipating from the Great Mother; Neumann, Campbell) — Roesler: brain development doesn't start from an unconscious matrix, and the Great-Mother-source is Eurocentric.
4. **Alchemy** (nigredo, separatio, mortificatio, dissolutio).

## The Universality Problem

Roesler's sharpest methodological charge: Jung "did not occupy himself with the material of the patient but rather held lectures, presenting his theory," and was "willing to force [his concepts] onto his patients." Case reports are "compromise formations" with low replicability. Even classic Jungian cases can be explained without archetypes (Merchant 2019). The remedy is a sceptical, adequately-methodologized research programme — exemplified by [[Structural Dream Analysis]], where the *only* cross-culturally stable regularities were the **patterns of dream-ego agency**, not any fixed symbol meanings (a genuinely archetypal child-in-danger motif appears at transformation turning points, but a snake threatens one dreamer and helps another).
