# Visio Arislei

> **One-line definition:** The "Vision of Arisleus," a king's-renewal parable preserved in the 1593 alchemical compendium *Artis Auriferae* ("The Golden Art") — Jung's primary text for the sterile-king/redeeming-children motif in *Psychology and Alchemy*, and the specific amplificatory tool [[Reginald Ajuonuma|Ajuonuma]] uses to reinterpret the Oedipus myth.

---

## The Story

Summarized by anthropologist Curtiss Hoffman (1999, pp.159-160), as quoted in [[ajuonuma2023-alchemical-oedipus]]:

> "The philosopher Arisleus journeys to the land of the King of the Sea, in which only like mates with like; consequently nothing is begotten and nothing prospers. Arisleus advises the king to mate together his mentally conceived son and daughter, Gabricus and Beya. During intercourse, Gabricus is swallowed into the womb of Beya, and dissolved into atoms. As a punishment, Arisleus and Beya are banished to a hothouse under the sea. There they remain imprisoned for 80 days until they are rescued by the androgyne Harforetus, a disciple of Pythagoras, who feeds them the fruit of the immortal tree which gives salvation. Beya gives birth to her own brother, resolving the king's problem."

## The Cast

- **The King of the Sea** — *[[rex-marinus|Rex Marinus]]* in *Artis Auriferae* — sterile because "only like mates with like": nothing of difference can unite in his land, so nothing is begotten. His sickness is the parable's inciting problem.
- **Arisleus** — the philosopher-counsellor the king seeks out. Jung equates him with **consciousness**: it is Arisleus's role to activate the latent potential represented by Gabricus and Beya (Jung 1944, para. 438). His redemptive role in the hothouse ordeal is "entirely passive," because "no one can complete the work except with the help of God" (paras. 449-450).
- **Gabricus and Beya** — the king's two mentally-conceived children. Their names derive from Arabic *kibrit* ("sulphur") and *al-baida* ("the white one") respectively (Ruska 1931, as cited in Jung 1944, para. 435, footnote 37) — the paired red-sulphur/white-mercury principles of Arabic alchemy, "from which all other metals emerge" (Thomas Willard 2015, p.271). Their mere existence signals that "the hidden state is one of latency and potentiality" (Jung 1944, para. 436).
- **Harforetus** — the androgynous disciple of Pythagoras, "the author of nourishment," who feeds Arisleus and Beya the fruit of the tree of life and ends their torment. A Hermetic figure, and thus identifiable with [[Mercurius]] (Jung 1943/1948, para. 278). His designation as "author of nourishment" marks him as a representation of the philosophical tree itself.
- **Pythagoras** — Arisleus's master, appearing to him in a dream in the hothouse. Jung identifies Pythagoras with **God** in this parable.

## The Movement of the Parable

1. **Sterility.** Rex Marinus's kingdom is barren because only like mates with like.
2. **Counsel and activation.** Arisleus advises the mating of Gabricus and Beya — mirrors the *coniunctio*.
3. **Death in union.** Gabricus is dissolved into Beya's womb during intercourse — a fatal ***[[coniunctio-oppositorum]]***, "punishment for the incestuous union of opposites" with his own sister (Jung 1944, para. 436).
4. **Banishment and ordeal.** Arisleus and Beya are banished to a hothouse under the sea, imprisoned 80 days, "exposed to every kind of terror" (para. 437) — Jung reads this as the hero-myth stage of "being swallowed up in the belly of the whale or dragon," calling the heat "the *ignis gehennalis*, the hell into which Christ descended in order to conquer death as part of his *opus*" (para. 440).
5. **Redemption from outside.** Harforetus, sent by the God-figure Pythagoras, feeds them the fruit of the tree of life.
6. **Resolution.** Beya gives birth to her own brother — a renewed, transformed son unlike either parent, the ***[[filius-regius]]*** — resolving Rex Marinus's sterility and renewing his kingdom.

## Why Jung Reads It Psychologically

The philosophical tree that bears fruit through the ordeal is the same tree the alchemists identify with Mercurius, itself identified with the *prima materia* and the *lapis* — see [[Lapis-Philosophorum]] and [[Kings Renewal Parable]]. The parable is, structurally, a complete statement of the individuation sequence in miniature: an unconscious, undifferentiated state (Rex Marinus's sterility) is activated by consciousness (Arisleus), passes through a death/dissolution ordeal (Gabricus's dissolution, the hothouse torment), and issues in a new, more complete configuration (the *filius regius*) that resolves what the original state could not.

## Ajuonuma's Mapping onto the Oedipus Myth

[[ajuonuma2023-alchemical-oedipus]] maps this cast directly onto the Oedipus cycle:

- Laius ↔ Rex Marinus (childless with Jocasta for years; his sterility rooted, on this reading, in his love for Chrysippus)
- The Delphic oracle of Apollo ↔ Arisleus (both are the counsellor/initiator figure; the paper notes it is "for the sake of Apollo" that Laius and Oedipus confront each other at the crossroads, supporting the equivalence)
- The crossroads killing ↔ Gabricus's dissolution in Beya's womb ("the quarrelling that precedes their union," Jung 1955-1956, para. 415)
- Laius and Oedipus, read via consubstantiality (Mercurius *duplex*, shared names/wounds/violence) ↔ Gabricus and Beya, whose names denote the paired sulphur/mercury principles
- Creon's banishment of Oedipus (with Antigone) ↔ Rex Marinus's banishment of Arisleus and Beya to the hothouse
- Colonus, sacred to Poseidon (water) and Prometheus (fire), site of the Furies' grove ↔ the undersea hothouse, characterized by the same water/fire elements and "every kind of terror"
- The Furies' transformation into the benevolent Eumenides, who receive and protect Oedipus ↔ Harforetus's rescue of Arisleus and Beya — both are an **enantiodromia** from persecution to salvation
- Oedipus's death as a blessing for Athens and a curse for Thebes ↔ the *filius regius*, who "bestows on men all health and prosperity ... but to the wicked, who abuse it, eternal punishment" (Waite 1678/1893, p.86)

See [[ajuonuma2023-alchemical-oedipus]] for the full argument and the resulting identification of Oedipus with the Jungian [[Self (archetype)|Self]].
