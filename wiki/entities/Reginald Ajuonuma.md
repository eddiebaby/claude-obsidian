# Reginald Ajuonuma

London-based analytical-psychology writer. Author of "The Alchemical Oedipus: Re-Visioning the Myth" (*Journal of Analytical Psychology*, 2023, 68(5), 807-827) — see [[ajuonuma2023-alchemical-oedipus]].

## Key Contribution

Ajuonuma addresses a real gap: Jung recognized the Oedipus myth's importance but never developed a sustained Jungian counter-reading to Freud's Oedipus complex. Ajuonuma supplies one, using two moves:

1. Reinserts the parts of the myth Sophocles' *Oedipus Rex* excludes — Laius's own crime (the rape of Chrysippus) and sterility, and the redemptive coda of *Oedipus at Colonus*.
2. Applies Jung's method of [[Amplification]] to the reassembled myth, using the alchemical [[Kings Renewal Parable|king's-renewal parable]] — specifically the *Visio Arislei* (see [[Visio Arislei]]) — as the amplificatory template.

The result: Oedipus reread not as the site of the incestuous complex Freud named after him, but as **Laius's redeemer** and an embodiment of the Jungian [[Self (archetype)|Self]] — "a combination of opposites." The paper's method is itself a demonstration that amplification, per Jung's own claim that "a great work of art is like a dream," extends beyond dream and patient material to literary/mythological texts.

## Method

Explicitly positions amplification against the criticism (Edward Glover, 1950) that it is "pedagogic rather than analytic" because it imports the analyst's own associations. Ajuonuma follows Jung in accepting the "personal equation" as long as the resulting interpretation "makes no pretence to be generally valid, but valid only for that area of the object which is being considered" (Jung 1921, para. 10) — and follows Caterina Vezzoli (2021) in treating amplification's mythopoetic openness as a safeguard against reductive, Freud-style closure of the tragedy.
