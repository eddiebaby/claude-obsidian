# Karl Kerényi

Hungarian classicist and mythologist (1897-1973). Co-authored *Essays on a Science of Mythology* (1949, with Jung) — Jung's principal formal collaboration with a classical scholar, developing the Child and Kore archetypes together. Already referenced in passing on the [[depth-psychology]] domain page's Key Figures list ("mythologist, collaborator on Child and Kore archetypes") prior to this ingest, which supplied the first full entity treatment.

## In This Source

In [[ajuonuma2023-alchemical-oedipus]], Kerényi is Ajuonuma's most-cited classical-mythology authority, drawn on for two works: *The Heroes of the Greeks* (1959) and *Oedipus: Two Essays* (in Kerényi & Hillman's *Oedipus Variations*, 1966-1968/1991).

Key contributions to the paper's argument:
- Notes that in the ancient epic *Oedipodeia*, Oedipus does not blind himself and remains king after Jocasta's suicide — evidence that Sophocles' *Oedipus Rex* is "but one version" of the myth, opening room for Ajuonuma's amplificatory reading of the fuller cycle.
- Argues Laius would not have needed the Delphic oracle's warning to want to harm the infant Oedipus — dethronement anxiety toward a son is a common Greek mythological pattern (paralleling Uranus's hostility to Kronos).
- Reads Oedipus's name ("swell-foot") as referencing both the birth-piercing of his ankles by Laius and an "ancient Daktyl nature" — a race of earthbound beings associated with the mother goddess Rhea — grounding Ajuonuma's chthonic-archaism link between Laius and Oedipus (alongside Lévi-Strauss's structural reading of both names denoting difficulty walking).
- With historian Pierre Vidal-Naquet, identifies Colonus (where Oedipus dies) as the threshold of Hades.
