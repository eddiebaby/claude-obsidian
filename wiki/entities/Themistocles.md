# Themistocles (524-460 BCE)

## Historical Overview

Athenian military strategist and statesman. Lived during the Persian Wars (5th century BCE). Rose from modest origins to become the architect of Athenian naval supremacy.

## Key Roles

**Battle of Marathon (490 BCE):** Fought under Miltiades; played a role in the defense

**Battle of Salamis (480 BCE):** Convinced the Athenian assembly to fight the Persian navy at the narrow strait of Salamis rather than retreat. His strategy—luring the larger Persian fleet into confined waters where Athenian triremes had tactical advantage—resulted in decisive Greek victory and saved Athens from Persian domination.

**Post-war politics:** Became the leading figure in rebuilding Athens and strengthening its naval power. Later ostracized from Athens and died in exile.

## Dreams and Decisions

Von Franz notes that antiquity left us a tradition that Themistocles' military decisions were often guided by dreams. Unlike Socrates (who dreamed but did not understand) or Descartes (who dreamed but rejected the message), Themistocles seems to have **honored his dreams and acted on them**.

This aligns with the ancient view that dreams, especially before major decisions, were messages from the gods or the collective psyche and should guide action.

## Jungian Significance

In von Franz's analysis, Themistocles represents the **warrior archetype** in its positive form: a man of action who:
- Listened to intuition (dream guidance)
- Made decisions that shaped history
- Acted decisively despite opposition
- Did not overthink or theorize, but responded to the psyche's guidance
