# Joseph L. Henderson

American Jungian analyst (1903–1982). One of Jung's closest American students; had a long personal analysis with Jung in Zürich. Founded the C.G. Jung Institute of San Francisco and played a central role in establishing analytical psychology in the United States.

> [!contradiction] Death year vs. cited 1984 publication

## Key Contributions

### Ancient Myths and Modern Man

Henderson wrote Part 2 of *Man and His Symbols* (1964), one of the most accessible and widely-read introductions to the archetypal interpretation of mythology. His central argument: mythological motifs that appear across cultures are not borrowings but spontaneous expressions of the collective unconscious. They track the same archetypal dynamics — above all the hero cycle and the process of psychic maturation.

**Hero cycle**: separation from the collective → trials and monster-confrontation → prize (consciousness, culture) → return or sacrifice. The hero is the ego's archetypal drive to differentiate itself from the unconscious matrix.

**Trickster**: Henderson drew heavily on Paul Radin's work on the Winnebago Trickster figure and interpreted the Trickster as the most archaic layer of the hero spectrum. Pre-moral, shape-shifting, taboo-violating — the shadow of the tribe. As the Trickster evolves through the four Winnebago cycles (Trickster, Hare, Red Horn, Twin), he progressively humanizes, tracking the development of consciousness from raw instinct toward personhood.

**Initiation vs. Hero achievement**: a critical distinction Henderson developed. Most individuation work is initiation — passive endurance of ordeal, surrender of individual will, submission to a process larger than oneself — not heroic triumph over external obstacles. The initiate reconciles opposing forces; the hero defeats them. Conflating the two leads to a psychology of ego-triumph that cannot handle the second half of life.

**Sacred marriage (Logos × Eros)**: the mythological expression of the conjunction of opposites that follows initiation. In the *Beauty and the Beast* pattern, Henderson traces the female initiation arc.

**Transcendence symbols**: snake and bird as dual symbols of chthonic and aerial forces (below and above). Their conjunction (caduceus, Aesculapius's rod) = the mediation of opposites. Modern rockets as contemporary transcendence symbols — the same upward aspiration in technological form.

## Relationship to Jung

Henderson underwent a long personal analysis with Jung and was one of the few American analysts Jung trained directly. He was also one of the contributors Jung trusted to write for *Man and His Symbols*, which Jung conceived as his definitive general-audience statement. Their correspondence and relationship spanned decades.
