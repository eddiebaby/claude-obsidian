# C.G. Jung

Carl Gustav Jung (1875-1961). Swiss psychiatrist and psychologist; founder of analytical psychology. One of the most influential figures in the history of psychology and in the broader cultural study of myth, symbol, and religion.

Navigation: [[depth-psychology]] | [[jung-cw9i-archetypes-collective-unconscious]]

---

## Biography (key facts)

- Born 1875, Kesswil, Switzerland; died 1961, Küsnacht
- Medical degree from University of Basel; psychiatric training at Burgholzli Clinic, Zurich, under Eugen Bleuler
- Early collaborator with Sigmund Freud (1907-1913); broke with Freud over the nature of libido and the role of sexuality
- Founded analytical psychology as a distinct school
- Professor at ETH Zurich and University of Basel
- Traveled widely: North Africa, New Mexico (Pueblo peoples), East Africa (Elgonyi tribe), India
- Autobiography: "Memories, Dreams, Reflections" (1962, posthumous)
- Painted the frontispiece mandala ("Mandala of a Modern Man") in 1916

## Key Conceptual Contributions

**Collective Unconscious**: Jung's most distinctive theoretical move: the personal unconscious (Freudian) rests on a deeper, inborn layer shared universally by all humans. See [[Collective Unconscious]].

**Archetypes**: Structural predispositions in the collective unconscious that organize experience into recurring patterns - the Mother, the Child, the Shadow, the Anima/Animus, the Self. See [[Archetype]].

**Individuation**: The lifelong developmental process by which a person becomes a unique, integrated whole - neither inflation into a collective role nor repression of inner contents. See [[Individuation]].

**Psychological types**: Introversion/extraversion (concepts Jung originated) plus four functions: thinking, feeling, sensation, intuition. (Developed in "Psychological Types," CW Vol. 6 - not yet in this vault.)

**Synchronicity**: Meaningful coincidence: acausal connection between psychic and physical events. (CW Vol. 8 - not yet in this vault.)

## Intellectual Debts

**William James**: Jung acknowledged James directly. James's "subliminal self" became the Jungian unconscious; James's "More" (the wider reality contacted in conversion and mysticism) became the collective unconscious and the Self archetype. See [[William James]] and [[james-varieties-religious-experience]].

## Style and Method

Jung's method is comparative and hermeneutic: he interprets psychological material (dreams, symptoms, active imagination) by amplification - placing the image alongside parallel images from mythology, alchemy, religion, and world literature to illuminate its meaning. This method makes his writing simultaneously clinical, historical, and philosophical.

His prose is dense with classical and cross-cultural allusion. He writes as a clinician reporting from experience, not primarily as a systematist: theories emerge from case material. This creates a characteristic texture: clinical vignette, then sweeping cultural-historical comparison.

## On *Aion* (His Own Words)

Jung kept writing *Aion* despite severe criticism: he felt compelled "to complete his escrow account with history." In a 1947 letter to Victor White:

> "Not very long after I wrote to you, I simply had to write a new essay I did not know about what. It occurred to me I should discuss some of the finer points about anima, animus, shadow, and last but not least the self. I was against it... Only after I had written about 25 pages in folio, it began to dawn on me that Christ — not the man but the divine being — was my secret goal."

To Margaret Ostrowski-Sachs:

> "Before my illness [in 1944] I had often asked myself if I were permitted to publish or even speak of my secret knowledge. I later set it all down in *Aion*. I realized it was my duty to communicate these thoughts, yet I doubted whether I was allowed to give expression to them. During my illness I received confirmation and I now knew that everything had meaning and that everything was perfect."

*Aion* is the book Jung felt he had to write — not wanted to, but compelled to, as an obligation to history.
