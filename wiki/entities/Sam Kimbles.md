# Sam Kimbles

American Jungian analyst; developed the theory of **cultural complexes** and **cultural phantoms** — collective/group-level analogues of Jung's personal complex theory. Author of *Phantom Narratives: The Unseen Contributions of Culture to Psyche* (New York: Rowman & Littlefield, 2014).

Cited in: [[JOAP-68-1-Charura-Bushell-Duoethnographic-Colonialism]] (*JAP* 68/1, 2023).

---

## Core Contribution

Kimbles argues that dynamics of belonging, othering, shadow, and identity politics "enter the superego of our collective consciousness long before one is conscious of how they inform development of our attitudes toward self and others." A **cultural complex** is the group-scale, transgenerational counterpart to a personal complex: autonomous, affect-laden, and capable of shaping perception and behaviour beneath conscious awareness. A **cultural phantom** is the unseen historical residue a culture carries and transmits. See [[Cultural Complex]] for the full concept treatment.

---

## Role in the Source Paper

[[Divine Charura]] and [[Stephen Bushell]] draw on Kimbles to make a specifically explanatory (not exculpatory) claim about [[C.G. Jung]]: "we take the view that Jung would not have escaped these dynamics, given his context and the evidence we note in his theories, approach and beliefs towards different others." Kimbles' framework is the mechanism the authors use to connect the [[Colonizer Archetype]] (structural pattern) to the [[Ethnic Shadow]] (specific transmitted content) at the collective level.
