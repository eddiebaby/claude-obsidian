# Hu Shih

## Overview

Leading modernizing Chinese philosopher (1891–1962), a student of John Dewey and a central figure of the New Culture Movement. In Jung's telling he is the emblem of Chinese intellectual modernity **discarding its own traditional wisdom** — the mirror-image of Jung, the European who insisted on preserving it.

## The Anecdote Jung Kept

In the mid-1930s (likely at Harvard's 300th-anniversary celebration, 1936) Jung asked Hu Shih his opinion of the **[[I Ching Divination|I Ching]]**. Hu called it "nothing but an old collection of magic spells, without significance" — then admitted, when pressed, that he had in fact once consulted the oracle and received a sensible, helpful answer. Jung recorded his disappointment decades later: *"In China… a philosopher like Hu Shih is ashamed to know anything of the I Ching… instead people worship locomotives and aeroplanes"* (Jung, 1976/1990, p. 322).

The irony [[zeng2023-i-ching-jungian-application|Zeng]] draws out: the Chinese scholar embraced a Western-style rationalization of Chinese thought (I Ching as mere logic text, its divinatory dimension discredited), while the European psychologist fought to keep the traditional, practical, divinatory reading alive.

## Connections

- A single-episode figure, but a load-bearing one for [[zeng2023-i-ching-jungian-application|Zeng's]] cross-cultural argument: the "misunderstanding" runs in both directions, and the Western embrace of the I Ching is itself entangled with the Eastern rejection of it.
