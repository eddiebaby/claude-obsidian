# Mikhail Bakhtin

## Overview

Russian literary theorist and philosopher of language (1895-1975), best known for dialogism, heteroglossia, and the concept of the "utterance" as the basic unit of speech communication. Entered the vault via [[Raya Jones|Jones's]] 2023 paper, which frames the entire century-long history of Jung's archetype theory as a single, ongoing Bakhtinian utterance.

## Key Facts

- Key texts cited: *The Dialogic Imagination* (1981) and *Speech Genres and Other Late Essays* (1986).
- Defined "utterance" as an analytic unit of communication ranging from "a short (single word) rejoinder in everyday dialogue to the large novel or scientific treatise," with "an absolute beginning and an absolute end: its beginning is preceded by the utterances of others, and its end is followed by the responsive utterances of others."
- Distinguished two simultaneous forces acting on any utterance: **centrifugal** forces (social and historical *heteroglossia* — the stratifying, diversifying pull of contested meanings and contexts) and **centripetal** forces (the pull toward a single "unitary language" or fixed semantic meaning).
- See [[Utterance (Bakhtin)]] for the full framework and how Jones applies it to archetype theory.

## Connections

- Framework used by [[Raya Jones]] to characterize [[Archetype|archetype theory]] as an evolving, contested discursive object rather than a settled scientific fact.
- Compare/contrast with [[Charles Sanders Peirce|Peirce's]] semiotic categories, also introduced via the same source but addressing a different question (representation vs. communicative history).
