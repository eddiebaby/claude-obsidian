# Sigmund Freud

Sigmund Freud (1856-1939) was an Austrian neurologist and the founder of psychoanalysis. His work fundamentally shaped modern psychology, medicine, and culture. C.G. Jung was Freud's collaborator and intellectual heir from approximately 1906 until their decisive break around 1912-1913. Jung's entire later work, including *Symbols of Transformation* (CW 5), is partly defined by his revision of Freudian theory.

---

## Life Overview

**Born**: May 6, 1856, Freiberg, Moravia (then Austria-Hungary)

**Died**: September 23, 1939, London, England (emigrated 1938)

**Education**: Medical degree from University of Vienna (1881). Studied neurology in Paris under Jean-Martin Charcot (1885-1886).

**Key Works**:
- *The Interpretation of Dreams* (1900) - foundational
- *The Psychopathology of Everyday Life* (1901)
- *Three Essays on the Theory of Sexuality* (1905)
- *Civilization and Its Discontents* (1930)

**Major Contributions**:
- The unconscious as the fundamental stratum of mental life
- The concept of repression
- Dream interpretation as access to unconscious wishes
- Psychosexual developmental stages
- Talk therapy (psychoanalysis) as a clinical method

---

## Freud and Jung: 1906-1913

Jung (then 31) and Freud (then 50) met and immediately recognized a kinship. Jung had been experimenting with word association as a window into the unconscious. Freud saw in Jung both an intellectual peer and a potential successor to lead the psychoanalytic movement.

The collaboration was intense and productive:
- Jung traveled to Clark University with Freud (1909)
- Jung published works expanding Freudian theory
- Jung became president of the International Psychoanalytic Association
- The two corresponded extensively (the Freud-Jung letters are a major historical document)

**The Break**: By 1912-1913, tensions erupted. The central point of divergence: Jung's view of libido and its transformations.

---

## The Core Disagreement (From Jung's Perspective)

In *Symbols of Transformation* (1912), Jung argued:

1. **Libido is not reducible to sexuality**: Freud saw all psychic energy as fundamentally sexual. Sublimation was a defensive redirection of sexual drives. Jung argued libido is undifferentiated psychic energy that genuinely transforms through symbolic forms.

2. **Myth is not a disguise for sexual complexes**: Freud reduced myth to personal/infantile psychology - the Oedipal complex writ large. Jung argued myth is the direct expression of collective archetypal patterns.

3. **The psyche is teleological, not merely causal**: Freud's reductive causalism - tracing all neurosis to early trauma and repressed drives - missed the forward-directedness of the psyche. Symptoms are not just the return of the repressed but meaningful communications attempting growth and transformation.

4. **There is a collective unconscious beyond personal repression**: While Freud focused on the personal unconscious (forgotten and repressed material from individual life), Jung argued for a deeper layer shared by all humanity, containing the archetypes.

5. **The second half of life has its own psychology**: Freud's model was largely derived from treating neurotics (typically younger people). Jung emphasized that the psychology of aging, individuation, and spiritual concerns requires a different framework than infantile sexuality.

---

## Freud's Response

Freud felt betrayed and criticized Jung's theoretical expansions as abandoning psychoanalysis. In Freud's view, Jung was mystifying what should remain scientific and material. The emphasis on myth, symbol, and the transpersonal seemed to Freud a regression toward the mysticism Freud had fought against.

The personal relationship deteriorated. Freud began to see Jung's theories as heretical.

---

## Historical Significance

The Freud-Jung split created two distinct schools:

- **Freudian Psychoanalysis**: Emphasis on sexuality, repression, personal unconscious, talk therapy
- **Jungian Analytical Psychology**: Emphasis on archetypes, collective unconscious, transformation, symbolism, meaning

Both remain influential, but they diverged significantly. Most clinical psychology draws more from Freud; depth psychology and spiritual psychology often draw more from Jung.

---

## Jung's Estimation

Jung, even after the break, acknowledged Freud's genius and contribution. He critiqued specific theories but not the overall project. In fact, Jung saw himself as extending and correcting Freud's vision, not rejecting it.

Key quote (Jung, 1924, Foreword to CW 5): "I have no wish to denigrate Freud, or to detract from the extraordinary merits of his investigation of the individual psyche. But the conceptual framework into which he fitted the psychic phenomenon seemed to me unendurably narrow."

Jung maintained that Freud was correct about the reality and power of the unconscious and correct that this unconscious operates through symbols. Where they differed was in the nature, scope, and meaning of those unconscious contents.
