# Stephen LaBerge

## Summary

American psychophysiologist (Stanford) whose *Exploring the World of Lucid Dreaming* (LaBerge & Rheingold, 1991) is the seminal, field-founding text of modern lucid dreaming (LD) research — cited in [[turchin-active-imagination-lucid-dreaming]] as the origin point of LD as "an industry with large number of participants, evolving techniques, and LD-inducing supplements." Also cited (LaBerge et al., 2018) for later work on acetylcholinesterase-inhibitor supplementation (galantamine) as a lucidity-induction aid.

## Why It Matters

LaBerge is to lucid-dreaming research roughly what Jung is to active imagination in this vault: the founding figure whose empirical/psychophysiological tradition [[Alexey Turchin|Turchin]] explicitly proposes replacing (for people who don't respond well to LD) with Jung's older, less pharmacologically-dependent technique. See [[Lucid Dreaming]] for the four complications his tradition documents (attaining lucidity, retaining it, concentrating on task, remembering result) that motivate the substitution hypothesis.

## Connections

- Founding figure for [[Lucid Dreaming]] as documented in [[turchin-active-imagination-lucid-dreaming]].
- His supplement-based induction research (galantamine, huperzine) is the pharmacological wing of LD induction, contrasted with Turchin's supplement-free AIM protocol.
