# Divine Charura

Black British psychologist/psychotherapist of Zimbabwean heritage (family homeland was colonial "Rhodesia," named after Cecil Rhodes). Based in York, England at time of writing (2023). Co-author with [[Stephen Bushell]] of "A duoethnographic exploration of colonialism in the cultural layer of the objective psyche" ([[JOAP-68-1-Charura-Bushell-Duoethnographic-Colonialism]], *Journal of Analytical Psychology* 68/1, 2023) — introducing the [[Colonizer Archetype]] and [[Ethnic Shadow]] concepts.

---

## Background (from the source paper)

- Introduces African [[Ubuntu]] philosophy into the paper's method, complementing the Jungian layered-psyche/archetype frame Bushell brought.
- Draws on family oral history: his great-grandmother "Nana T," who as a child in Zimbabwe/Rhodesia witnessed the arrival of white colonizers and asked "Why?" and "what will it take to end it?" — questions Charura reports still asking himself in 2021.
- Reports repeatedly encountering resistance, denial, and "you've misunderstood Jung" dismissals when raising Jung's writing on Africans and the African diaspora in professional dialogue with colleagues.
- Co-editor (with Colin Lago) of *Black Identities + White Therapies: Race, Respect + Diversity* (Monmouth: PCCS Books, 2021), cited in the source paper as part of the case for decolonizing the psychotherapy curriculum.
- Author (2018) of "Child development and attachment," in M. Robson & S. Pattison, eds., *The Handbook of Counselling Children and Young People* (2nd ed., Sage).

---

## Role in the Source Paper

Charura facilitated a diversity training session for a London-based Jungian organization at Bushell's invitation — the encounter that catalyzed their two-year duoethnographic dialogue (see [[Duoethnography]]). His account of visiting the International Slavery Museum in Liverpool, and his grandmother's account of first encountering white colonizers ("they had no knees, and they had no ears!"), anchor the paper's central passages on belonging and the [[Ethnic Shadow]].
