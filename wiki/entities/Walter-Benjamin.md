# Walter Benjamin

German-Jewish philosopher and critic (1892–1940), Frankfurt School-adjacent, known for his critique of modernity and instrumental reason. In the fifth thesis of "On the Concept of History," names historical time as **non-continuous, interrupted — a time full of shards**: "the past can be seized only as an image which flashes up at the instant it can be recognized and is never seen again." Cited in [[yago2026-hour-that-never-comes-time-that-remains]] as a founding voice for the essay's whole approach to psychic temporality, and his **Angelus Novus** (the Angel of History, facing the past as a single catastrophe while blown backward into the future) is named as close kin to the essay's imaginal figure for [[The Time That Remains]]: "the wanderer among ruins."
