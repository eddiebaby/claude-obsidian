# Namfundo Lily-Rose Mlisa

South African traditional African health practitioner and Western-trained clinical psychologist. Addressed the 21st Congress of the International Association for Analytical Psychology (IAAP), Vienna, 2019, in dialogue with Jungian analysts facilitated by Peter Ammann — published as "Encountering the other: Jungian analysts and traditional healers in South Africa Part III: the traditional health practitioner's stance and the world view," *Journal of Analytical Psychology*, 65(1), 2020, pp. 212-15.

Cited in: [[JOAP-68-1-Charura-Bushell-Duoethnographic-Colonialism]] (*JAP* 68/1, 2023).

---

## Key Quote

Speaking as a traditional health practitioner engaging Jungian theory, Mlisa named Jung as an ancestor: "without hesitation I thank our ancestor, Carl Jung, for providing traditional health practitioners with a western relevant theory that explains our deep hurt during our spiritual journey."

---

## Role in the Source Paper

[[Divine Charura]] and [[Stephen Bushell]] cite Mlisa's address — alongside Peter Ammann's characterization of Jung on Mount Elgon as a "participant observer" who "allowed himself to be deeply affected and changed by the African experience" — as counterweight evidence within their broader, non-defensive treatment of Jung's legacy on race. The authors do not use Mlisa's testimony to excuse Jung's documented failures on race (see [[C.G. Jung]] and Andrew Samuels' critique), but to support their claim that some of Jung's own cross-cultural duoethnographic encounters were genuinely transformative and can still ground contemporary decolonial-Jungian dialogue, even as his theorizing about non-European peoples is rightly criticized.
