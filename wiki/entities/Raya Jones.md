# Raya Jones

## Overview

Academic psychologist at the School of Social Sciences, Cardiff University (UK), working at the intersection of analytical psychology, semiotics, and discourse/narrative theory. Author of "Dialectics of Sign and Symbol and the Utterance of Archetype Theory" (*Journal of Analytical Psychology*, 2023), a sustained epistemological critique of Jung's archetype concept that treats the theory itself as a discursive object — an "utterance" in Bakhtin's sense — rather than taking its scientific claims at face value.

Not a clinician; her project is metatheoretical: examining what kind of thing "archetype theory" is (sign? symbol? category? biological hypothesis? discursive construct?) rather than applying it clinically.

## Key Facts

- Earlier work: "Mixed metaphors and narrative shifts: Archetypes" (*Theory & Psychology*, 2003) — cited in the 2023 paper as her own prior analysis of Jung's history-of-philosophy sketch as itself an archetypal narrative ("less a *history* of an idea than … the *story* of an archetype, in his sense of the term").
- "Archaic man meets a marvellous automaton: posthumanism, social robots, archetypes" (*Journal of Analytical Psychology*, 2017) — cited for the Khidr/transhumanist-immortality comparison used to illustrate the categorization problem.
- Central move in the 2023 paper: characterizes archetype theory as a Bakhtinian **[[Utterance (Bakhtin)|utterance]]** — a historically situated communicative act pulled between centripetal (fixed, unitary meaning) and centrifugal (contested, evolving) forces — rather than either a settled fact or a dead metaphor.
- Draws the dual-function reading directly from Jung: "An expression that stands for a known thing remains a mere sign and is never a symbol" (Jung, 1921, para. 817) — so the word "archetype" itself functions simultaneously as a **sign** (a technical term known to trained psychologists) and a **symbol** (the best available expression for something "fundamentally unknowable"). See [[Sign and Symbol]].
- Introduces [[Charles Sanders Peirce|Peirce's]] icon/index/symbol trichotomy as a comparison case and shows it does **not** map cleanly onto Jung's sign/symbol dichotomy — the two thinkers are answering different questions (Peirce: how do representations convey information; Jung: why do certain representations carry disproportionate psychological weight). See [[Peircean Semiotics]].
- Surveys the "categorization problem": the same motif (e.g., rebirth) gets assigned to different archetypes in Jung's own corpus (Mother in 1912/1952; Individuation in a 1939 Khidr lecture) with no principled criterion for the assignment — a direct consequence of Jung's own claim that "clear-cut distinctions and strict formulations are quite impossible in this field" (Jung, 1949, para. 301).
- Surveys the scientific-credibility literature (Stevens, Maloney, Goodwyn, Hogenson, Merchant, Knox, McDowell) and sides with **[[Christian-Roesler|Roesler's]]** verdict that Jungians should stop claiming genetic transmission of archetypal content — quoting Roesler (2012): "we Jungians cannot go on basing our theory of archetypes on scientific assumptions which have been falsified.… It is important that we stop arguing that archetypes are transmitted genetically if we want to be taken seriously." This is the same critique elaborated at book length in [[roesler2025-process-of-transformation|Roesler's later work]], cited here from an earlier (2012) paper — independent corroboration, not the same citation.
- Positive proposal: relocate the archetypal not in a switched-on biological structure but in the **probabilistic patterning of the experiential realm** — using Gibson's ecological theory of *affordances* (the ground affords walking, water does not) as the conceptual bridge between physical constraint and symbolic expression (e.g., Jesus walking on water as the symbolic register of an "afforded" impossibility).
- Concluding stance: archetype theory's credibility problem is structural, not fixable by better neuroscience — Jung wanted the theory to be a scientific hypothesis (falsifiable, empirical) but built it as, and defended it with, the interpretive fluidity of a hermeneutic method. "The discourse of archetype is thus characterized by tensions between intellectualization and insight, sign and symbol, the named and the nameless."
