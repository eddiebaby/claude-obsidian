# Alexey Turchin

## Summary

Russian researcher affiliated with the **Science for Life Extension Foundation** and **Digital Immortality Now** — a transhumanist/life-extension research context, not a clinical or Jungian-institute background. Author of *"Active Imagination as an Alternative to Lucid Dreaming: Theory and Experimental Results"* (see [[turchin-active-imagination-lucid-dreaming]]), based on an open-label pilot he ran in 2004–2005 in Moscow with ~100 participants.

## Approach

Turchin treats Jung's [[Active Imagination]] (AIM) as a **portable technique for self-exploration**, detached from its clinical-individuation origins: a reliable substitute for lucid dreaming, a tool for entertainment, parapsychological experiments (remote viewing, past-life exploration), and — his own distinctive extension — a data-collection instrument for **digital immortality** (recording detailed séances as material a future AI could use to reconstruct/resurrect the person; see Turchin, 2018, cited in the source). This is a substantially more pragmatic, non-clinical, and outcome-agnostic use of AIM than any other source in this vault (contrast [[Barbara-Hannah|Hannah]], [[Marie-Louise von Franz|von Franz]], or [[C.G. Jung|Jung]] himself).

## Why It Matters

His mental-screen/visual-screen typology (see [[Mental Screen]]) is a genuinely useful, transferable design insight — independent of whether one accepts his broader consumerist reframing of AIM. It gives concrete design guidance for any app-based active-imagination feature: users must be screened for imaginal type before being promised "vivid visualization," or they will be disappointed regardless of technique quality.

## Connections

- His protocol is compared and contrasted with classical Jungian AIM on [[Active Imagination]] (see the contradiction callout there).
- Cites [[Stephen LaBerge]]'s lucid-dreaming research as the field AIM is proposed to substitute for.
