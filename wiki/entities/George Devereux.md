# George Devereux

Ethnopsychoanalyst (1908-1985) who founded the field of ethnopsychiatry, applying psychoanalytic method cross-culturally. In the vault, known through his 1953 paper "Why Oedipus Killed Laius — A Note on the Complementary Oedipus Complex in Greek Drama" (*International Journal of Psycho-Analysis*, 34, 132-141), the single most-cited source in [[ajuonuma2023-alchemical-oedipus]].

## Key Contribution: The Complementary Oedipus Complex

Devereux coined the **complementary Oedipus complex** — the parent's own filicidal fantasies and hostile behaviours toward the child, which *constellate* the child's own (Freudian) Oedipus complex from the other direction rather than merely reacting to it. See [[Complementary Oedipus Complex]].

Applied to the Oedipus myth: Devereux argues it is not Laius's attempted murder of the infant Oedipus that damns him in the ancient sources, but his prior "hybris" — abducting and raping Chrysippus, son of King Pelops — that he "appears to have retained throughout life a propensity for unconsidered violence" (1953, p.134), and is "a violator of good manners" (p.135) rather than simply a would-be filicide acting on oracular fear.

Devereux also supplies the symbolic-castration reading Ajuonuma leans on: in the *Oedipodeia*, Oedipus removes Laius's sword and belt after killing him — in ancient Greece "the undoing of a woman's belt is a preliminary to intercourse" (1953, p.134) — a symbolic feminization of the father that lets Jocasta's marriage to Oedipus be read as "not only cohabitation with the mother as a woman, but also with the mother as the representative of the now feminized homosexual paternal ogre."
