# Wilfred Bion

British psychoanalyst (1897–1979), one of the most influential post-Kleinian theorists. Known for his theory of thinking (alpha/beta elements, container/contained) and for the concepts [[Faith in O|**O and K**]] developed in *Transformations* (1965) and *Attention and Interpretation* (1970): **O** is ultimate, unknowable reality (the Kantian thing-in-itself, "the formless infinite"); **K** is knowledge *about* something; transformation runs K → O. Bion urged the analyst to work "without memory or desire," in "faith" that O has issued in objects one can become aware of. [[Eckhard Frick|Frick]] ([[frick2025-spiritual-path-transformation|2025]]) uses Bion's O as the bridge between analytic and spiritual language, mapping it onto the Jungian Self and reading Jung's "we are created, not creators" ([[Createdness]]) as "Jung's way of expressing Bion's faith in O." Though not a Jungian, Bion converges with Jung on the primacy of an unknowable reality the ego must receive rather than master.
