# Charles Sanders Peirce

## Overview

American philosopher, logician, and scientist (1839-1914), regarded as the founder of pragmatism and of modern semiotics (the systematic study of signs). Entered the vault via [[Raya Jones|Jones's]] 2023 critique of Jung's archetype theory, where his 1903 lecture on the categories of representation is used as a comparison framework for Jung's sign/symbol dichotomy.

Commonly read as a Kantian philosopher, though the extent of his departure from Kant's theory of categories is debated among scholars (Gartenberg, 2012).

## Key Facts

- In a 1903 lecture, "The Categories Defended" (posthumously collected in *The Essential Peirce*, Vol. 2, 1998), Peirce outlined a **trichotomy of icon, index, and symbol** — see [[Peircean Semiotics]] for the full framework.
- Whereas Jung's focus is on *how or why* certain symbolic representations arise psychologically, Peirce's focus is on *how existing things* (words, pictures, objects) convey information *through their formal properties* — a semiotic (representational) question, not a psychological (motivational) one.
- His trichotomy does not map cleanly onto Jung's sign/symbol dichotomy: a picture of a seven-headed dragon is a Peircean **icon** (it represents by resemblance, regardless of whether dragons exist) but a Jungian **symbol** (it stands for something psychologically unknown). The two systems are answering different questions and are not substitutable for each other.
- Example objects used to illustrate his categories: a statue of a centaur (icon), an old-fashioned hygrometer (index — its physical reaction to moisture persists whether or not anyone interprets it), and any general word, sentence, or book (symbol — representing "solely and simply because it will be interpreted to be a representamen").
