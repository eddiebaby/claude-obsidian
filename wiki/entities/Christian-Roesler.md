# Christian Roesler

## Overview

Contemporary Jungian analyst and clinical psychology researcher who developed **[[Structural Dream Analysis]]** (SDA), a narrative research method that puts Jungian dream theory on an empirical footing by analyzing the dream-ego's agency and its relationships to other dream figures.

## Key Facts

- Key papers: "Structural Dream Analysis: a narrative research method…," *International Journal of Dream Research* (2018), 11(1):21–29; "Jungian theory of dreaming and contemporary dream research…," *Journal of Analytical Psychology* (2020), 65(1):44–62; "[[roesler2026-jung-dream-theory-empirical-research|Jung's Theory of Dreaming and the Findings of Empirical and Clinical Dream Research]]," *Journal of Analytical Psychology* (2026) — the fullest published statement, with the complete 6-pattern typology and formal hypothesis testing.
- Identified **six dream-ego agency patterns** (No dream ego, Threatened, Performance requirement, Mobility, Social interaction, Autonomy — the sixth added in the 2026 paper), with 20 numbered sub-patterns covering over 90% of clinical dreams. A dreamer's series is dominated by one or two patterns tied to their psychological problems; patterns shift from passive → active over *successful* therapy, nonlinearly (a mid-therapy regression to Pattern 2 typically marks direct confrontation with the central complex).
- 2026 paper: three hypotheses (upward pattern movement in successful therapy; rising dream-ego agency; initial psychopathology predicts starting pattern) **empirically confirmed** across 150 C.G. Jung-Institut Stuttgart case reports plus named single cases (Amalia X, C.L., a short-term-therapy case), with a negative case (failed therapy) independently locating the exact session of relational collapse via a downward pattern shift.
- Tested the method on the Freudian specimen case **[[Amalia-X|Amalia X]]** (95 dreams), "empirically confirming" the passive→active movement and finding data that "refute the hypothesis of wish fulfilment" with "no evidence of censorship."
- Argues contemporary evidence supports Jung's **continuity** view (per Domhoff 2017) and his subjective-level method, but **qualifies strict compensation** — the 2026 paper suggests the dream's function is "not so much compensating... but more confronting consciousness with the actual reality of the psychological situation," refining the earlier "completion" (holistic-picture) reading. Also found **no evidence for fixed universal symbol meanings** — the same image (a snake) functioned oppositely for two different dreamers depending on their complex structure.
