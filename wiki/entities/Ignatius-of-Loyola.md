# Ignatius of Loyola

Spanish soldier-turned-mystic (1491–1556), founder of the Society of Jesus (Jesuits) and author of the **Spiritual Exercises** (*Exercitia spiritualia*), a structured programme of meditation and imaginative prayer. Central to Jungian interest (via [[Eckhard Frick|Frick]], [[frick2025-spiritual-path-transformation|2025]]) for two contributions:

- **[[Discernment of Spirits]]** (*discretio spirituum*) — distinguishing movements of consolation (from the Holy Spirit) from desolation (from the "enemy"), developed from his convalescence at Manresa and his serpent-and-cross visions.
- The **"Principle and Foundation"** (§23): *El hombre es criado* / *Creatus est homo* — "the human person is created" — which Jung read psychologically as [[Createdness]], being anticipated by the unconscious.

Jung devoted a 1939–40 ETH seminar to the *Exercitia spiritualia*, comparing Ignatius's visions to his own confrontation-with-the-unconscious material, and was especially intrigued by the difference between Ignatian guided *imaginación* and Jungian [[Active Imagination|active imagination]] — whether imagination opens or closes the door to the uncontrollable ([[Faith in O]]).
