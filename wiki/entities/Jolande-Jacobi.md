# Jolande Jacobi

Swiss-Hungarian Jungian analyst and writer (1890–1973). Born in Budapest, trained in Vienna, became one of the leading systematizers and teachers of Jungian psychology. Known for her ability to present Jung's complex ideas in clear, pedagogically organized form.

---

## Key Contributions

### Symbols in an Individual Analysis: The Henry Case

Jacobi wrote Part 5 of *Man and His Symbols* (1964) — a complete case history of a nine-month analysis. "Henry" (pseudonym) is a 25-year-old Swiss engineer: introverted, rational, mother-bound, on the threshold of manhood. 35 sessions, 50 dreams. The most detailed published example of Jungian analysis in action for a young person undergoing the first half of individuation.

The case demonstrates:
- How dream images evolve across an analysis
- How the shadow, anima, and Self emerge sequentially
- How synchronistic events (I Ching, scarab beetles) function as confirmation
- How the analyst reads amplifications without over-directing

**Key dream sequence**:
1. *Initial dream* (Zinalrothorn): wise old woman as guide; dead men in blue suits = sterile intellect; cloudburst = sacred marriage; voice saying "Be fruitful!"
2. *Fourth dream*: hybrid animals (half pig, half dog) = undifferentiated instinctual *prima materia*; doe = shy, innocent anima
3. *Saint and Prostitute dream*: enantiodromia (prostitute → saint); two caves = instinct and spirit; crimson coat as persona blending two feminine figures
4. *Oracle dream*: South America journey; Chinese gatekeepers; trapper shadow; I Ching consultation → hexagram MENG (Youthful Folly) as synchronistic confirmation
5. *Scarab dream*: black beetles from drawing table; fire destroys breeding ground; scarab = Egyptian solar transformation symbol
6. *"Unlived life" dream*: old man (ruling principle) dying; fortune-telling cards → nine of spades (moral chains). **The dictum that emerged**: "The unlived life is an illness of which one can die."
7. *Final dream (50th)*: four-part mandala (Evening/Night/Noon/Morning); six figures representing different aspects of the masculine psyche; Henry gives the Negro (chthonic shadow) a box of matches = offering warmth and fire; communal feast = masculine totality integrated

Henry married nine months after the analysis began. Jacobi interprets the completed analysis as "initiation into the reality of outer life... the completion of the first half of the individuation process." The second half — right relationship between ego and Self — remains ahead.

### Systematizing Jung

Jacobi was the most systematic of Jung's immediate circle — the one who organized his ideas into clear conceptual structures:
- *The Psychology of C.G. Jung* (1942, 7th ed. 1973): the standard introductory overview, with Jung's own endorsement
- *The Way of Individuation* (1965): thorough treatment of the individuation process
- *Complex/Archetype/Symbol* (1959): philosophical treatment of the key theoretical structures

She was less interested in the cutting edge of Jungian theory than in transmitting what Jung had established.

---

## Relationship to Jung

Jacobi studied under Jung in Zürich, underwent personal analysis with him, and remained a close colleague. She was a founding member of the C.G. Jung Institute in Zürich (1948) and taught there for decades. Her books carry Jung's prefaces and endorsements — he recognized her pedagogical gifts even when he occasionally differed from her systematizing tendencies.
