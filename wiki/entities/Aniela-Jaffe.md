# Aniela Jaffé

Swiss Jungian analyst and writer (1903–1991). Jung's personal secretary during his final years; editor of his autobiography *Memories, Dreams, Reflections* (MDR). One of the most important interpreters of Jung's work for a general audience.

---

## Key Contributions

### Editor of Memories, Dreams, Reflections

Jaffé conducted the interviews with Jung and edited the material that became *Memories, Dreams, Reflections* (1962, published posthumously). This is Jung's only explicitly autobiographical work — Jaffé shaped its form, extracted the key passages, and saw it through to publication after Jung's death in 1961. Without her, this central document of analytical psychology might not exist in its current form.

The degree to which MDR reflects Jung's own words versus Jaffé's editorial framing has been debated by scholars, but the book remains the indispensable entry point into Jung's interior life.

### Editor of Man and His Symbols

Jaffé also edited *Man and His Symbols* (1964), Jung's final project. She coordinated the five contributors (Jung, Henderson, von Franz, Jacobi, and herself), oversaw the integration of the visual imagery with the text, and managed the project after Jung's death.

### Symbolism in the Visual Arts

Jaffé wrote Part 4 of *Man and His Symbols*, an extended survey of how archetypal symbols — stone, animal, circle, mandala — manifest in prehistoric art, world religious traditions, and modern painting. Her central argument: modern art is not merely aesthetic experiment but symptom and expression of the collective psyche's condition. The artist is "the instrument and spokesman of the spirit of his age."

Key themes:
- **Stone symbolism**: unhewn stones as the oldest form of deity-dwelling; modern sculptors (Aeschbacher, Rosati, Max Ernst) letting stone speak without imposing form
- **Animal symbolism in prehistoric art**: Lascaux, Trois Frères; the shaman-figure of the *Lord of the Animals* as the archetype of the human-animal boundary
- **Mandala in architecture**: cities as projected mandalas (Rome as urbs quadrata; Romulus's founding plow-circle; Washington D.C.; Angkor Wat)
- **Circle in modern painting**: Delaunay, Matisse, Kandinsky, Klee — the circle separated from the square = the dissociation of the modern psyche
- **UFOs as projections**: the "flying saucer" phenomenon (mass appearance post-WWII) as visionary rumor — the collective unconscious projecting wholeness-symbols onto the sky
- **Modern art arc**: Chirico (dread), Chagall (warmth), Duchamp (found objects), Schwitters (rubbish as cathedral), Max Ernst (frottages with circles), Marino Marini (horse and dying rider as collapsing hero myth) — traced as a psychic history of the 20th century

---

## Relationship to Jung

Jaffé was Jung's closest collaborator in his final years, holding an unusual position as both personal secretary and intellectual colleague. She was present for many of the conversations that shaped MDR. After Jung's death she continued to write, edit the Collected Works correspondence, and defend the integrity of his work.
